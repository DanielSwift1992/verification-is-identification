## Detail: §2a. From the whole to a forced answer

Under The Cut's relational account, physical names realized relations and their actual constraints on interactions within the whole. It does not introduce another substance outside those relations. An inscription, its interpretation and the interaction it describes can all exist inside the same whole without being the same event. The existence of an inscription does not establish that its stated relation is realized. Identifying an actual recording operation with a structural relation is therefore an internal question about source, interaction and record, not a crossing between two worlds. A realized renaming also changes something within the whole: the inscription, a memory or an interaction. Calling it a change of description says which answers about the referent remain invariant under that change. It does not place the naming act outside physical reality. Conversely, a changed inscription need not change the referent in the way the inscription asserts. The relevant proof binds the actual change to the particular preserved or altered question.

Different questions require different relations. The following routes have separate premises. Certifying one fixed answer need not pass through every row.

| Relation established | Consequence | Existing argument |
|---|---|---|
| Every relevant carrier belongs to Ω | Any source, apparatus, residual and reader is internal | The Cut Step 5 |
| A stated question family registers a difference | Its answers separate the admitted alternatives | Step 3's jurisdiction, Verdict VI2 |
| The interface preserves specified relations under interpretation | Those relations constrain its admissible laws | M65: uniform transport gives selections, equality access admits more |
| The generating relations and permitted ways of composing them are fixed | A preserved operation can exclude a target from the entire expression grammar | VC23a: majority excludes parity from binary Boolean constraints |
| Q = D ∘ E ∘ U for the actual update and later reading | The earlier Q-answer is recoverable through that later reading | M50–M54, `ReadingFactors` |
| The sufficient reading is closed under every admitted continuation | The same record supports the entire future question family | VC15/VC38, IiS19 |

Historical retention enters through The Cut's record commitment. M50–M54 establish the separate relation to later access. In the quantum specialization, the complex state space, tensor composition and admissible channels supply further constraints: IiS18–IiS19 show that physical encodings serving the same questions need not admit every identification allowed by a description. The constructive target is to establish which internal relations the interface must preserve and can read. Global scope alone does not specify that interface, while the interface can force an answer without fixing every other feature of the whole.

An expression medium has a corresponding boundary. VC23a rules out an entire class of binary Boolean descriptions of a joint relation, including arbitrary finite hidden-bit extensions. It also constructs an adequate representation after a specified change of carrier or composition. This provides a stronger diagnosis than another failed candidate: the missing relation cannot emerge from further saturation within that grammar. M65 concerns transport across content interpretations, whereas VC23a concerns operations on several permitted assignments. Both locate the restriction in an actual preservation requirement. Neither treats an unrestricted name for the desired property as its derivation.

The Cut's Step 3 ranges over every possible internal registration. Closure under a declared set of operations covers only the questions generated by those operations. To promote equality of an available image E to identity under the book's rule, establish that every registration Q in that universal scope has a decoder D_Q through E. Then E(x)=E(y) gives Q(x)=Q(y) for every such Q, and the adopted jurisdiction identifies x and y. A generating family can supply this route when both its completeness for that scope and closure of its decoders are established. This is the separation and factorization argument already used in M54 and VC15, with its quantifier made explicit. M65's internal reserve provides a concrete boundary: an interface closed under its earlier preparations can still omit a registration available through another resident preparation. The containing whole has not acquired an exterior. The earlier interface did not cover that registration.

Nor does a possible separator for every pair supply one guaranteed acquisition procedure. VC26 already gives a finite model in which every pair can be distinguished but every first intervention destroys an unresolved distinction for some other pair. The book's registration rule concerns whether a difference belongs to reality. Acquiring a sufficient record from an unknown source additionally needs compatible access through an actual course of interactions. VC30–VC31 supply the existing question-preserving policy criterion, tracking required earlier answers through each intervention. M65's resident examples retain their source during acquisition and therefore meet that preservation condition. This use of a retained source is part of their construction, not a consequence of pairwise registrability alone.

The relevant necessity concerns a question. Let A specify the relations being preserved, and let C(A) be a nonempty family of admissible constructions with a common interpretation of question q and its answers. A forces answer a when q(c)=a for every c in C(A). The constructions can differ. Their raw encodings, internal resources and other answers need not coincide. A concrete admissible construction prevents an empty family from passing as a unique answer. The reasoning quantifies over descriptions and realizations compatible with the stated relations, not over additional actual worlds outside Ω.

This is the question-invariance criterion of Verdict VI2. It does not require identifying the entire source. An explicit finite certificate covers its declared candidates. A generic constructor can derive a relation for every admissible input in its family. In either case, assuming the desired answer again as an input condition would not derive it. Nor would choosing one successful construction establish agreement of all others.

**What supplies the first obstruction?** The Cut now separates bare happening at Step 1 from the encountered contrast given at Step 2. Merely asserting that relations occur cannot replace that contrast. Consider a description containing only affirmative relation instances and equalities, with no other laws, refusals or actual nonidentities. Interpret every name as one point and every relation as containing every tuple of that point. All its statements remain satisfied, however many names and contacts were written. Additional requested identifications remain satisfied too. This construction tests what that description says, without proposing a physically possible one-point reality.

The exhibited contrast defeats this collapse precisely because it includes nonidentity. Its being a positive datum does not make it a contentless predicate that may also relate a point to itself. The requirement is to retain what actually differs. Two names do not supply it: Swift's `Pair` permits equal fields, and `Null` is one of its refinements. A graph can carry the relevant exclusion through an irreflexive contact, an addressed refusal, or a concrete constructor distinction. The meaning of that relation must be established. Otherwise its name adds no resistance.

**A short name for the whole is not a point identification.** Let one known structured source g contain the retained contrast a≠b. On the singleton source family {g}, the code E(g)=* and decoder d(*)=g recover every specified question about g, including that contrast. The decoder retains the structure omitted from the isolated code. This does not identify a and b: coding the whole g is a different map from sending each participant to one interpreted point. A singleton code alphabet therefore need not describe a singleton structure. In bare Swift, one alias for Paired<A,B> still exposes the two declared roles through its definition. The spelling need not repeat that definition at every use.

The fixed decoding relation is essential. On a source family K, one constant code and one fixed decoder recover q exactly if and only if q is constant on K. Necessity is q(x)=d(*)=q(y). If q has one common value, the constant decoder returning it suffices as a mathematical reading. In particular, exact identity recovery with one constant code requires K to contain at most one source. Giving each x a different decoder d_x(*)=x changes the complete input. If c identifies the supplied decoder configuration and D(c,m) evaluates that configuration on code m, the actual requirement is q(x)=D(c(x),E(x)). Different required answers force a difference in that joint input, even when all codes are equal. This is VC9's factorization criterion applied to the code together with its interpretation.

The existence of one actual whole does not supply the missing decoder. Writing d(*)=g specifies a reading using g. It does not discover which detailed description is correct about that whole. Testing several descriptions compatible with established facts does not posit several actual worlds. It tests whether those facts already determine the requested answer. Compression can reuse a retained interpretation and its source correspondence. The prohibition concerns erasing the required distinction from that complete relation, not the number of names used to write it.

### M71

**M71 (a witnessed identity obstruction survives every completion preserving its premises).** Start with established relations at specified addresses. Include a positively registered distinction between two of their contents a and b. Suppose a proposed additional contact, together with those relations, gives a finite derivation of a=b by equality substitution at the same addresses. No enlargement of the description can realize that contact while preserving those relations and that distinction. The enlargement may contain arbitrarily many other forms, interactions, records and internal readers. It need not belong to an enumerated candidate family.

**Proof.** Carry the addressed premises into the proposed enlarged realization. Every equality used in the derivation still relates the same interpreted endpoints. Reflexivity, reversal, transitivity and substitution into a specified reading preserve equality at each step. Induction over the finite derivation therefore gives equality of the two interpreted contents. Their retained positive distinction refuses that equality. Additional objects cannot remove this finite obstruction without changing at least one relation used by it. The finiteness belongs to the argument, not to reality or to the family of its possible descriptions. QED

The proof uses identity of addressed content and its positive separation. It does not assign a Boolean answer to every question, infer disagreement from a missing conformance, or apply a rule that arbitrary conclusions follow from coexisting conflicting reports. Two reports can occupy different addresses. The proposed contact fails when it requires their different answers to be one settled answer at one address.

LQ13 supplies a complete conditional instance for local square laws. Their joint extension is possible exactly when every zero-sum list of addressed arguments also cancels its local answers and cross-pairing terms. A failure has a witness of at most r+1 arguments, where r is the dimension of their represented span. That finite contradiction persists in larger ambient descriptions preserving the listed relations. Agreement on every pairwise overlap is sufficient for all local families exactly when those overlap identifications generate every dependency in the sum map. The obstruction concerns the attempted joint account, without making reality finite or treating context-dependent reports as one context-free answer.

**The obstruction can precede a single-valued reading.** Let a partial description carry established admissions P_R and established refusals N_R for each specified finite-arity contact R, together with actual nonidentities D. Include the source and context among the addressed roles whenever they affect the contact. Let ≈ be the equivalence generated by the established equalities and proposed identifications. For this description alone, a representation realizing those identifications and retaining every listed fact exists exactly when neither of the following occurs:

- A pair in D has ≈-equal endpoints.
- An admitted tuple in P_R and a refused tuple in N_R have ≈-equal entries at every corresponding place of the same R.

**Proof.** In the first case the representation identifies contents it must distinguish. In the second it makes one and the same contact both admitted and refused in the same sense. Conversely, represent each role by its ≈-class and carry the images of P_R and N_R as separate, disjoint sets of established statuses. The two conditions make this well-defined and retain every listed fact. Unlisted contacts remain unresolved. This constructs a compatible partial description, not the physical availability of a proposed contact. Additional laws, if supplied, impose their own obligations. QED

Every failed condition has finite support: a finite-arity tuple comparison or one nonidentity, plus a finite equality chain for each compared place. Thus its refusal survives every enlargement preserving that support, without requiring a finite whole or a complete contact catalogue. No deterministic source-to-answer function was used. A contact relation may admit both (x,a) and (x,b) with a and b distinct. Identifying those two complete contacts, or requiring their answers to equal one answer, adds the demand that creates the obstruction. Two different outcomes alone do not prove that their source projections differ.

### M71a

**M71a (complete identity questions for a finite functional contact diagram).** Let V be a finite nonempty set of addressed roles, without presuming that different role names denote different contents. Supplied facts consist of identity equations E, registered contrasts D, and finitely many rows

    k(a₁,...,aᵣ)=b.

Each k denotes one common, possibly partial function of its stated finite arity on an arbitrary nonempty carrier Y, defined at the supplied rows. Its actual identity across rows must be established; a common label alone is insufficient. No totality, injectivity, inverse, algebraic identity, finite capacity or exhaustion of Y is required. No additional domain refusals are specified in this fragment; an absent row is not a refusal.

Form the least equivalence ≈ on V containing E and closed under the supplied rows: whenever two rows use the same k and corresponding arguments are ≈-equal, join their outputs. Each strict join reduces the finite number of classes, so this closure terminates. It operates on the supplied diagram, without enumerating or bounding the unknown medium.

The diagram is realizable iff no contrast in D has ≈-equal endpoints. Necessity follows by substitution through a common function. For sufficiency use the carrier V/≈. Each row defines k at the tuple of its argument classes; closure makes this single-valued. Leave other tuples undefined, or assign any one class to all of them if a total completion is wanted. The resulting functions and role map a↦[a] retain every supplied equation, row and contrast. This constructs a compatible interpretation, not its physical realization or an identification of the actual Y with that finite carrier. Arbitrarily many further states can be adjoined while retaining every old tuple's value and assigning the new tuples freely.

For a consistent diagram, the identity question for a,b is therefore exact:

- Equality is forced iff a≈b. Propagation proves the forward implication; if the classes differ, the constructed interpretation separates them.
- Distinction is forced iff adding a=b to E and closing again collapses some registered contrast. Any identifying interpretation would violate that contrast. Conversely, a consistent new closure supplies an actual quotient interpretation with a=b.
- Otherwise the original quotient separates a,b and the new quotient identifies them, while both retain all supplied facts. The question is unresolved by this diagram, with two constructive witnesses of that underdetermination. An unsuccessful proof search is not the witness.

The consistency qualification prevents vacuous identification from impossible premises. The two interpretations are extensions of an incomplete representation of one given source, not two actual realities. This is completeness for a specified question and contact fragment, not a Boolean answer to every question about the whole.

Contrasts cannot force additional equalities in this unrestricted functional fragment: its original quotient keeps every unforced pair separate. M64k's inequality graph can force an equality because it adds a bound on the number of available values. Without that capacity relation its all-distinct interpretation would remain possible. The different conclusions identify exactly where the bound contributes information.

A finite further contact expression with its required contacts admitted can be included by adding its role and defining row. This does not create admission at an untested input. A registered negative comparison k(a)≠b can be represented by its actual reply role c with k(a)=c and c≠b. This adds no guessed correspondence between replies from different contexts. Nor does closure of this finite diagram establish an unrestricted future continuation profile; VC107 and VC108 concern that stronger question. For nonfunctional contacts the output-joining rule is unavailable: the relational result above permits multiple distinct outcomes at one projected input.

This is the established congruence-closure principle expressed as an addressed realization and question criterion. The standard equality and disequality criterion appears in [Barrett and Tinelli, Satisfiability Modulo Theories, §4.1](https://cs.stanford.edu/~barrett/pubs/BT18.pdf). Here it supplies the explicit scope of the current contact constructions, not a claim to a new decision procedure. No runtime solver is introduced.

`ContactDiagram.swift` gives a generic implication and its two completion boundaries. An actual common Left contact f satisfies f²(a)=a. A further return f³(a)=a forces f(a)=a: transporting the first equation by f gives f³(a)=f(a), and the second closes the identity. `ReturnsForceFixedPoint` derives it without a supplied one-step identity or inverse. A fixed node and a two-cycle both realize the two-step diagram alone. Actual iterated projections give the same recorded positive return fact, while complete records retain their different sources. Their one-step identity answers differ. An additional Right reading in the two-cycle gives Never and Succ<Never>; identifying those nodes would collapse this actual contrast.

Equal supplied successors also need not identify their source. `MatchingSuccessorsModel` supplies equal Left and Right successors but an additional internal Answer reading separates its nodes as Never and Succ<Never>. The distinction is witnessed by another possible reading, rather than asserted from different type names alone. Claiming that the earlier successors exhaust all possible readings would add a completeness or joint-recovery premise absent from this diagram. These addressed implications and model boundaries are checked statically; the general quotient-completion theorem is the mathematical proof above.

### M71b

**M71b (a conditional boundary can preserve compatibility of whole diagrams).** Consider two M71a diagrams G,H whose role sets meet only at a finite boundary B. Their contact-symbol sets are disjoint, with no additional laws relating the two sets. This is a condition on the displayed constraints, not statistical independence or a claim that the containing reality has no other relations. Renaming a genuinely shared contact to make it private would change this premise.

Let P_G be the set of partitions of B that some realization of G induces: roles in one block have equal images, and roles in different blocks have different images. Membership of a proposed partition π is an exact M71a question. Add its within-block equalities and between-block contrasts to G and close the diagram. The partition is admissible exactly when no supplied contrast collapses. Thus P_G is a finite relational interface even when the private carrier or the containing medium is not bounded. It need not be stored as a list; a construction answering its compatibility questions can represent it.

The two diagrams have a joint realization iff

    P_G ∩ P_H ≠ ∅.

Necessity restricts any joint realization to its boundary partition. For sufficiency choose a compatible quotient realization of each diagram for one shared π. Take their disjoint union and identify just the corresponding boundary classes. Each local carrier still embeds injectively, so every local contrast survives. A private contact retains its prescribed values on its own carrier's tuples; the other diagram places no requirements on that contact. Leave the remaining tuples undefined or complete them freely. This constructs a joint realization without inspecting any other possible interiors.

This instantiates Seam SG2's boundary-image criterion. It also proves minimality for the stated class of later partners: if two interfaces disagree about one partition π, a partner supplying exactly π as boundary equalities and contrasts distinguishes their compatibility. This uses the declared availability of such finite boundary-identity constraints. A restricted partner grammar can permit a coarser interface. The result preserves existence of a joint realization; it does not by itself select one common action for every unresolved actual source, as M25a distinguishes.

Unconditional answers to individual boundary questions are insufficient. Let B={a,b,c,d} and let G contain f(a)=c and f(b)=d, with f private and no given boundary contrasts. Both an all-equal and an all-distinct interpretation are possible, so every individual pair's identity is unresolved. The empty boundary diagram G₀ has exactly those same unconditional pair verdicts. Yet the partner H imposing a=b and c≠d joins G₀ and cannot join G. The missing relation is conditional: a=b forces c=d through the shared f inside G. A list of current verdicts lost how a new identification changes the allowed answers.

If contacts themselves are shared between G and H, even the whole set of boundary partitions may be insufficient. Take B={a,c,d}, with c≠d in both diagrams. Let G contain f(a)=x, g(x)=c and H contain f(a)=y, g(y)=d, where x,y are private roles but f,g are the same contacts. Each diagram admits all three boundary partitions with c≠d. Their boundary profiles therefore coincide and intersect. Joining the actual contacts nevertheless forces x=y and then c=d. The common path g∘f, which the identity-only interface discarded, supplies the obstruction. Neither agreement of boundary names nor equality of the two profiles establishes that this path is private.

There is a constructive sufficient boundary for this shared-contact case. Suppose two displayed realizations are identified along a common substructure C: each shared contact is defined on C's argument tuples, its outputs remain in C, and the two restrictions agree there. The contacts can remain partial elsewhere. Form the disjoint union of the carriers over C. A tuple lying in both old carriers lies entirely in C; the two prescribed answers are therefore the same element of C. Any other old tuple is prescribed on only one side. Combining these rows and leaving mixed tuples free yields shared partial functions extending both realizations. Private contacts extend as before. No local identity or contrast is changed.

This sufficient construction concerns the specified shared contacts. If a proposed boundary is not closed under them, their output roles may have to become part of the shared interface as well. A hidden output is not made private by omitting its address from publication. The two-step example already shows that obstruction at a finite depth; no general finite bound on all future shared continuations is inferred. For comparison with established mathematical theory combination, see [Barrett and Tinelli, §5](https://cs.stanford.edu/~barrett/pubs/BT18.pdf). The argument here uses its explicitly stated free-contact scope.

`ContactBoundary.swift` preserves the conditional relation constructively. `FunctionalBoundaryReading` reads (a,b,f(a),f(b)) using actual source projections. `AliasActivatesBoundary` takes an identity of a,b and derives the equality of the two resulting fields, without requesting it as a second premise. `UncoupledBoundary` supplies the contrasting case with independent c,d. `SharedEntryForcesHiddenAgreement` derives equality of both the actual private intermediate and the final reading along a shared two-contact path. The separate paths returning Never and Lit1 cannot be joined at that same source while preserving the common path.

`ClosedBoundaryJoin` gives a positive shared-contact witness. Its boundary has nodes P,Q with f exchanging them and g fixing each. A private node on the first side has both contact outputs P; the second has both outputs Q. Both local views are actual projections of one constructed whole and reuse its same boundary. The input conditions contain only the four boundary contact equations; the private rows and preserved local views are constructed. A further internal reading supplies a positive P/Q contrast, and composed readings distinguish the two private nodes. Thus their common boundary permits a joint carrier without making their interiors identical. The static checks cover these constructions and refusals; the general partition-interface theorem is the proof above.

### M71c

**M71c (conditional equality is a complete interface for the free-contact fragment).** The finite interface in M71b need not be a catalogue of partitions. For a boundary equivalence S, add its equations to G and perform M71a's closure. Write C_G(S) for the resulting boundary equivalence. If an original contrast collapses, retain that obstruction instead of declaring an answer about an actual source. For every consistent S, C_G(S) is the least partition extending S that G admits. Its quotient model realizes that exact partition, while every other realization contains its equalities. Thus the interface can return newly forced relations with their derivations, or a witnessed obstruction.

For two diagrams with M71b's disjoint contact symbols, this gives an exact constructive exchange. Start with the discrete boundary partition and repeatedly apply C_G and C_H. Stop with incompatibility if a local contrast collapses. Otherwise every change joins previously separate boundary classes, so there are at most max(0,|B|−1) strict joins before both closures agree. At a common fixed partition S, each local quotient realizes exactly S. M71b joins those quotients and proves compatibility. Conversely every propagated identity is required by a joint realization, so no encountered obstruction can be repaired while retaining its support. This is a complete existence test in the stated fragment, with no guessing of a boundary partition. Only boundary equations need to enter the other diagram; each remains supported by its actual local derivation. The bound counts strict boundary mergers, not local proof cost or communication messages.

A one-time list of unconditional answers still fails in M71b's example. The exchange succeeds because a newly received identity is supplied to the current local construction and can activate its conditional answer. It does not copy a verdict status or equate two conclusions on the basis that both were accepted. If a contact is actually shared, the disjoint-symbol premise fails and M71b's hidden-path obstruction still applies. The standard theory-combination literature calls the relevant absence of irreducible equality alternatives *convexity*; see [Barrett and Tinelli, §5.2](https://cs.stanford.edu/~barrett/pubs/BT18.pdf). This direct proof supplies its particular contact scope, not a new solver.

There is also an exact description of which finite boundary families the language can express. Regard a partition as its set of equal pairs. For a nonempty family P on a finite B, the following are equivalent:

1. P is the boundary profile of a consistent M71a diagram, permitting fresh private contacts of arbitrary finite arity.
2. P is closed under intersections of nonempty subfamilies.
3. P is specified by finitely many conditional rules S⇒(a=b) and forbidden mergers S⇒⊥, where S is a finite conjunction of boundary equalities.

**Proof.** For (1)⇒(2), take the product of any nonempty finite family of realizations of the same diagram. Map each role to the tuple of its images; apply each contact componentwise where every component is defined. Every supplied row remains defined and correct. Every supplied contrast still has different endpoints. Two product images are equal exactly when their images agree in every component, so the boundary partition is the intersection. B has only finitely many partitions, which also covers nonempty intersections indexed with repetitions. No empty-product closure is required: an empty product would identify every role and can destroy a supplied contrast.

For (2)⇒(3), consider each partition π outside P. Let P_π be the members of P containing every equality of π. If P_π is empty, forbid the conjunction of π's equalities. Otherwise their intersection μ belongs to P, contains π, and differs from π. Choose one equality in μ but not in π and make it the conclusion of that conjunction. Every member of P satisfies the rule; π violates it. Finitely many such rules admit exactly P among equivalence relations. This is an existence construction, not a claim that the smallest rule presentation is unique or cheap to obtain.

For (3)⇒(1), realize each rule

    (a₁=b₁ ∧ ... ∧ aᵣ=bᵣ) ⇒ c=d

by a fresh contact k and two rows k(a₁,...,aᵣ)=c and k(b₁,...,bᵣ)=d. Their functionality imposes exactly that conditional identity. For a forbidden merger, use two fresh output roles p,q with p≠q instead of c,d. An empty antecedent becomes a given equality or an immediate inconsistent contrast. For any boundary partition satisfying the rules, equal argument tuples have equal prescribed outputs; other tuples remain independent. Choose the fresh contrast outputs separately and define each private contact at its supplied rows. This extends that partition to a diagram realization. Conversely each realization satisfies all rules. No capacity, injectivity or unlisted contact law is added. The availability of fresh finite-arity contacts is part of this converse; a fixed smaller signature need not express every such profile. QED

This exposes a limit of the fragment. The requirement a=b or a=c, with neither alternative forced, cannot be imposed by these free contact rows and contrasts alone. The patterns {a,b}|{c} and {a,c}|{b} have the all-distinct partition as their intersection, which violates that requirement. Equivalently, M71a's least quotient simultaneously separates every pair whose equality is unforced. A consistent diagram therefore forces a finite disjunction of equalities only if it already forces at least one member.

This does not prohibit alternatives in reality or in VI as a whole. For example, established b≠c and a carrier capacity of at most two values force a=b or a=c without selecting either. The two permitted patterns above have a three-valued product, which the capacity relation excludes. The extra law changes the expressive environment; silently assuming a binary carrier would have supplied exactly the missing content. M64k uses that kind of capacity explicitly. A range restriction, an admitted alternative relation or another acquired law must likewise be presented when it does this work.

`AlternatingBoundaryClosure` exhibits three actual exchanges: an input identity passes through G's Left contact, H's Right contact and G's Left contact again. Each constructor consumes the preceding derived identity. The final answer is bound to `AlternatingBoundaryPath`'s actual composed reading. Only the entry identity is supplied; neither the intermediate nor terminal equalities are requested from the caller. Two unmerged entries with terminal answers Never and Lit1 show the obstruction that a later entry merger activates.

`ProductBoundary` constructs joint role images and recovers both original boundary triples by their component projections. `AlternativeBoundaryProduct` uses (0,0,1) and (0,1,0); the product roles (0,0),(0,1),(1,0) have neither of the original alternative identities. Actual component readings provide both positive contrasts. These static witnesses check the displayed constructions and failed mergers. The general product and interface representation claims have the mathematical proof above.

### M71d

**M71d (eliminating private support can require a wider boundary condition).** Fix m≥2 and boundary roles a₁,b₁,...,aₘ,bₘ,c,d. The target interface admits exactly the partitions satisfying

    (a₁=b₁ ∧ ... ∧ aₘ=bₘ) ⇒ c=d.

Role names do not assert distinctness. This entire family is realizable using only private unary contacts and m−1 private intermediate roles. Put h₀=c, hₘ=d, introduce h₁,...,hₘ₋₁ and a fresh unary fᵢ for each link, and supply the two rows

    fᵢ(aᵢ)=hᵢ₋₁,       fᵢ(bᵢ)=hᵢ.

If all input identities hold, functionality joins each consecutive pair of h roles, forcing c=d. Conversely fix any boundary partition obeying the target implication. Join neighboring h roles exactly where aᵢ=bᵢ holds. Identify the endpoint classes as required by the given c,d; choose fresh values for any remaining private classes. When c≠d, at least one missing input identity separates the endpoint segments. When c=d, joining the two endpoint classes is permitted. In either case every equal-input pair of rows has equal outputs. Each fᵢ has only its two prescribed rows, so the functions are well-defined and the boundary partition is unchanged. Leave other inputs free.

This proves exactness of the eliminated boundary relation, not only its forward implication. Unexpected identities between different boundary roles cause no extra row comparisons across different fᵢ: their contact symbols are private and distinct. Reusing one actual contact for all links would change this premise and can add further constraints. No injectivity or inverse is assumed in constructing the chains.

The construction also strengthens M71c: fresh unary contacts already suffice for every finite profile characterized there. Replace each nonempty Horn antecedent by such a chain. For a forbidden merger, its two endpoint roles are fresh and carry a contrast. Empty antecedents are supplied equalities or immediate obstructions. Different clauses use separate private roles and contact symbols. Their local constructions therefore join on every boundary partition satisfying all the clauses. Higher contact arity is unnecessary for this expressive result.

Now require an interface containing only the original boundary roles, with rules whose antecedents are conjunctions of boundary-pair equalities and whose conclusions are one boundary equality or ⊥. No auxiliary roles, aggregate terms or additional predicates are admitted. The smallest possible maximum antecedent width for the displayed target is exactly m, even if arbitrarily many rules are allowed.

**Proof of the lower bound.** Let π identify just aᵢ with bᵢ for every i, keeping all these m blocks, c and d separate. It violates the target. Any rule system defining exactly the target must contain a rule violated by π. Its antecedent is true in π, so each nonreflexive atom lies within one of the m input-pair blocks. If its width were less than m, at least one block would not be used. Let π′ identify only the input pairs used by that antecedent, leaving every other role separate. The antecedent is still true. The conclusion is still false: an equality absent from π is also absent from its subrelation π′, and ⊥ remains false. Yet π′ is admitted by the target because at least one input identity is missing. Thus this rule would exclude a required boundary possibility. No such system of smaller-width rules can be exact. The displayed m-antecedent rule attains the bound. QED

This is a bound within the specified interface grammar. A single equality between two constructed tuples can carry all m input identities. That expression retains a joint object and its projections; it is not one of the original boundary-pair atoms. Likewise, retaining intermediate roles can replace a wide condition by short local implications. The result does not assign an absolute number of logical atoms to the dependency. It identifies what cannot be discarded while shortening this particular interface: the joint support must remain in a condition, an intermediate structure or an equivalent acquired relation.

`JoinedConditionalBoundary<A,B>` presents the two-link case. A supplies a=b, B supplies u=v, and the actual first-contact output at b is bound to the second-contact output at u. The constructor derives the equality of the outer readings using the two previously established projection constructors. It does not request that final equality. Removing either input identity separately admits an `OpenConditionalChain` whose outer readings are Never and Lit1 while the other identity and intermediate seam survive. Both omissions therefore matter.

`PackedConditionalBoundary` takes one equality of the paired input records. Actual projections recover A and B and feed the same two-link constructor. This demonstrates how a shorter static expression keeps the needed joint structure. `ClosedConditionalChain` supplies a nonzero joint realization and binds the constructed equality to both actual outer readings. The packed input does not obtain the two premises from less information. The general width theorem concerns all boundary-only rule systems; the Swift witnesses establish these addressed chains, their packed use and both missing-premise obstructions.

### M71e

**M71e (a finite diagram can force future agreement without determining future answers).** Consider the following two-sorted fragment of M71a. A finite diagram names source roles, answer roles, equalities and contrasts within each sort. It supplies rows o(x)=y for finitely many common unary partial contacts o, explicit refusals o(x) undefined, and rows E(x)=a for one common total reading. Realizations may use arbitrary nonempty source and answer carriers. There is no capacity bound, injectivity requirement, inverse law or other unlisted condition. Answers are not fed back as contact inputs in this fragment. A missing row is unknown, not a refusal.

Close the supplied equalities under functionality: equal inputs of the same contact join their supplied outputs; equal inputs of E join their supplied answers. Assume consistency: no supplied contrast collapses, and no source class both admits and refuses the same contact. Let C be the n resulting source classes. Different classes need not be distinct in every realization, but the least quotient keeps them distinct and realizes all supplied rows and refusals. Missing contact rows can remain undefined, and missing readings can be filled freely.

Fix roots x,y. Ask whether **every realization** of this diagram makes them equivalent for every finite word in the declared contacts: the word is executable at both or at neither, and its defined E answers agree. This is VC47's behavioral question, now asked across completions of an incomplete diagram rather than over one fully specified transition graph.

The following finite pair test is exact. Start at the unordered pair ([x],[y]) and inspect each reached pair at most once.

* At a diagonal pair (c,c), close the branch. No outgoing row or E answer is needed there: it is the same actual input of every common contact.
* At a distinct pair (c,d), both E rows must be supplied and their answer classes must coincide. For each contact, either both inputs have an explicit refusal, or both have a supplied successful row. In the latter case inspect the successor pair. Any other case fails the test.

**Sufficiency.** Relate the images of every inspected pair in an arbitrary realization and include all diagonal pairs of its carrier. At each off-diagonal listed pair the rows establish equal current answers and matching availability. Successful successors are again related. At a diagonal pair these facts follow from one common partial function and one common reading at one actual input, irrespective of the unknown future. Induction on word length therefore preserves availability and every defined answer. The induction ranges over words, not over a supposedly finite whole. Repeated pairs close a finite certificate; no prediction of the number of future source states is required.

**Necessity.** If the test fails, reach a first failing distinct pair by a prefix of supplied successful rows. Use the least source quotient, keeping all its classes distinct. If two supplied E answers have different classes, keep them distinct too. If an E row is missing at either input, fill the missing readings to make the answers different, using a fresh answer if necessary. This respects all the diagram's other facts. The prefix itself now distinguishes the roots. Otherwise failure concerns a contact: a supplied success opposed to a supplied refusal already separates availability. If one or both rows are missing, complete them so that the contact is defined at exactly one of these distinct inputs, respecting any supplied row. A fresh output source may be used. Leave other missing contact rows undefined and fill other readings arbitrarily. The prefix followed by this contact distinguishes availability. These choices build a finite compatible realization, rather than a gap in a proposed proof.

There are at most n(n−1)/2 distinct unordered pairs to inspect. A shortest route to a failing pair has no repetition and cannot pass through a diagonal. Thus a counter-word of length at most n(n−1)/2 suffices whenever the test fails: a reading failure uses that route, and an availability failure adds one contact. This is a sufficient bound, with no claim of optimality. It does not bound the cost of acquiring the supplied diagram.

The criterion answers whether future agreement is *forced*. A countermodel refutes that proposed implication; it need not prove that the actual roots will differ, nor that agreement remains possible. A supplied answer contrast can already force disagreement in every realization. Likewise, the necessity proof depends on the declared freedom to complete missing rows. Additional reversibility, totality, capacity or coupling laws can forbid its countermodels and require a different criterion.

The diagonal case removes a real completeness obligation. Suppose the roots have equal current E answers and every declared first contact sends them to one common source for that contact. Their entire future behaviors then agree, although even the next E answer can remain unknown. With one contact o, the rows E(x)=E(y)=a and o(x)=o(y)=z suffice. No row at z is required. Compatible completions can give z different readings or arbitrarily different future structures, while preserving the comparison of x with y. This does not contradict VC107: the identity of the actual reached input is supplied structural evidence, stronger than matching finitely many readouts. VC108 supplies a different route through a justified bound on future profiles. Neither route establishes the other's premise for free.

`JoinedContinuation<A,B,R>` gives the static common-input construction. Actual first projections from A and B feed the same supplied suffix R; their input identity derives the suffix-answer identity without requesting it separately. `RecordReading` retains both original roots beside their facts. `JoinedRoots` supplies equal current answers and an additional private contrast, which survives those records. `UnjoinedVisibleRoots` shows equal current answers with different answers after the same two projections when the joining relation is absent. These constructions check the joined-input case for an arbitrary admitted suffix, not the full pair test or arbitrary partial-function semantics; those have the proof above.

**A new partner changes the continuation question.** M71e ranges over a fixed list of common unary contacts. Consider instead a finite consistent ground diagram with one common partial binary contact B and one total reading E. The source and answer carriers can be enlarged, missing rows are free, and there are no other capacity, inverse or universal coupling laws. Equalities are closed under functionality as above. For two named roots x,y, the following property is forced in every realization exactly when their source classes have been joined:

    for every internal h, B(x,h) and B(y,h) have matching availability
    and, when admitted, E(B(x,h))=E(B(y,h)).

If the classes are joined, both applications have the same actual ordered input. Otherwise retain the least quotient and adjoin one fresh internal form h. Admit B(x,h)=x and refuse B(y,h). These input tuples occur in no old ground row, so the extension preserves every old equation, contrast and refusal. Availability now differs, even if E has only one possible reply. This finite realization refutes the proposed universal agreement. The proof is an application of M71a's free completion, not a new general decision procedure. A closed partner inventory or universal laws on B would change its premises.

The boundary is not restricted to a failed application. If E has at least two available replies, instead adjoin fresh u,v, admit both B(x,h)=u and B(y,h)=v, and give u,v distinct E replies. When the old diagram is consistent with total B, fill its other missing rows arbitrarily to obtain a total counter-realization. Thus mandatory availability of contact still does not establish agreement of its replies. A globally constant E would remove this latter distinction; it would not remove the availability test for a partial B.

The binary arity is not by itself the obstruction. For a fixed named partner h, the derived contact B(−,h) is unary and can be included in M71e's vocabulary. The stronger claim quantifies over partners not represented in that finite evidence. Enlarging the family of possible internal contacts is different from repeating any number of the old contacts. All fresh forms in the counter-realization are inside its one carrier; an external disposal place or observer is not being supplied. These are alternative completions of the given description, not a claim that a new participant can physically be created or that the actual sources must eventually be separated.

**Reversibility does not remove this extension freedom.** Let C be a finite carrier containing distinct x,y, and let the established whole-contact rows form a partial bijection on C×C. Extend it to a permutation of C×C: each finite directed path can be closed into a cycle, and already closed cycles remain unchanged. Adjoin three fresh forms h,u,v to C. Keep the old permutation, exchange (x,h) with (u,h), exchange (y,h) with (v,h), and fix every remaining new pair. The four selected pairs are distinct and disjoint from C×C. This defines one permutation of the complete enlarged pair carrier. Reading its first output through E, with E(u)≠E(v), separates x and y at the same new partner h. Every old row survives. The whole contact is reversible and the distinguishing participant remains internal. The construction requires room for these forms and the stated freedom on unaddressed pairs; it cannot override an independently fixed complete carrier or an additional contact law.

**A constructive boundary can nevertheless admit arbitrary partners.** Give each participant a visible field and a private field. Consider the actual operation

    U((a,p),(b,r)) = ((b, χ₁(a,p,b,r)), (a, χ₂(a,p,b,r))).

The two private outputs may depend on all four inputs. The visible output is exactly (b,a), independent of that private update. Consequently two complete inputs with the same visible pair give the same visible reply, even when both private inputs and both private-update laws differ. This is obtained from the displayed construction, not by assuming that the visible replies agree. Neither private role has to be autonomous.

On fixed full carriers V×P×V×R, this whole U is bijective exactly when every private map χ_(a,b):P×R→P×R is bijective. The visible output recovers (a,b) by exchange, after which inversion is precisely the corresponding private-map problem. Thus source-dependent reversible private dynamics are allowed. Taking χ₁=p and χ₂=r supplies a complete involution. No reversibility is claimed for an arbitrary supplied private update.

At every permitted contact the same visible exchange law applies. Induction therefore determines the visible evolution for any finite common schedule, including newly introduced participants with corresponding visible inputs. A schedule chosen from the common visible history has the same property. If a further selection mechanism reads the hidden fields and chooses different partners, that is an additional contact to be justified, not a consequence of this induction. The construction protects a question through its interface without asserting completeness of the containing whole.

`VisiblePartnerExchange<S,R>` constructs the outputs from S's actual visible fields and the actual answer fields of a private reading R bound to S. `VisiblePartnerAgreement` derives the shared visible answer for two such sources and updates. `VisiblePartnerReturn` constructs the private-identity involution through two source-bound applications. The retained private fields provide a positive contrast that prevents promoting visible equality to whole-source identity. A separate refusal rejects substituting a private update whose source has equal visible fields without whole-source identity.

**One fixed contact can expose the omitted field through a new participant.** On source pairs (a,p) and a control partner c∈{0,1}, define U₀((a,p),0)=((a,p),0) and U₁((a,p),1)=((p,a),1). These are the two disjoint clauses of one controlled exchange; both retain c and return after two applications. With only partner 0, sources (a,0) and (a,1) have identical visible replies for every repetition. Partner 1 gives different visible replies in one contact. `IdlePartner`, `ExposingPartner` and `FreshPartnerContrast` build these paths using identity, field exchange and a retained partner. The operation names do not prove the clauses; their actual fields and compositions do. This finite example is not the general extension proof, but exhibits its continuation boundary without irreversible erasure.

This sharpens the scope rather than invalidating earlier sufficient evidence. For the declared unary continuations, M71e's joined future can remain exact. For all new partners, finite free ground evidence forces the stronger agreement only when it already forces identity of the roots. A constructed membrane such as the visible exchange instead admits a large partner family while preserving a smaller question by its uniform law. Establishing that law for an unknown physical contact remains a distinct acquisition or structural obligation.

This is not an erasure law for the containing whole. Agreement concerns the selected working input, reading and contact family. Retained histories can remain distinguishable. If a later contact consults such history, its full input is no longer just the common working source used in this proof. An identical label, a shared instrument name or an equal partial reading does not establish that common-input premise. The contribution here is the exact finite-diagram criterion and its countercompletion, combining M71a's free extension with VC47's continuation argument; it is not a claim to a new general theory of behavioral equivalence.

**Passing the present diagram has a different extension rule.** Return to the relational partial description under “The obstruction can precede a single-valued reading”, before M71a, without functionality or other extra laws, and let E be the equivalence generated by its established equalities alone. A proposed set of additional identity equations remains compatible with every consistent enrichment of that description exactly when all those equations already belong to E. Here an enrichment may add a registered nonidentity while retaining every old fact, and compatibility asks whether the enriched facts and the proposed equations have a common realization.

For sufficiency, every enrichment already includes E, so those equations add no requirement. For necessity, choose a proposed u=v with u and v in different E-classes. The canonical partial realization on the E-classes retains the original description and u≠v. Adding that registered distinction is therefore a consistent enrichment, but it excludes the proposed identity. A single newly retained contrast suffices. With additional laws, their forced identities must be included in the analysis: the bare equality closure alone would no longer be complete. This result concerns what the partial description permits, rather than asserting that every compatible enrichment occurs physically.

The asymmetry matters for saturation. A finite refusal cannot be repaired while its premises are retained. A merge that passes an incomplete diagram may need to be withdrawn when another actual relation is established. Calling the current diagram complete would add precisely the obligation missing from that permission. Keeping two roles unresolved also differs from asserting them different. Neither equal spelling nor different addresses alone settles their actual identity.

**Equal answers can remain equal when a later question separates their sources.** Suppose E(x) already suffices for a fixed question q. Adding another question q′ does not falsify that old factorization. It can make E insufficient for the enlarged task. Among descriptions that retain E and answer q′, the joint reading R(x)=(E(x),q′(x)) is least informative in the recovery order: any description from which both fields are recoverable also recovers their pair. Two sources have the same R exactly when they agree in both fields. This construction specifies the required refinement, but does not supply q′ from E when the old image omitted it.

Nor does storing a settled answer beside its evidence automatically retain the source. `FirstAnswerAndEvidenceOmitSecond` in `Checks/ClosedInformation.swift` uses the existing `AnswerWithItsEvidence` constructor. For arbitrary A,B,C, the sources (A,B) and (A,C) give exactly the same paired answer and evidence (A,A). The common payload still cannot answer the second-coordinate question. `FirstAnswerRecordRetainsSecond` instead composes two projections from the actual `RecordReading` record and recovers B for arbitrary A,B. Its decoder receives the record, without a separately supplied earlier source. The two records with B=0 and B=1 supply a positive difference, and `WHOLE_ANSWER_EVIDENCE_ANSWERS_NEW_QUESTION` refuses a new-question factorization through the old payload. These constructed records illustrate the distinction between retaining a question's evidence and retaining its source. They do not assert universal physical access to earlier sources.

The statuses here concern actual admission and refusal of the specified contact. Two records asserting opposite statuses can coexist as records. A failed attempt, a missing record or an unavailable instrument does not by itself establish the universal refusal used in the proof. An encountered nonidentity already supplies a narrower exact exclusion: identification of those contents while retaining their contrast. That is where the foundation begins.

LQ13's Malcev example supplies a finite test for proposed reversibility. Its three established product equalities coexist with cancellation and a positive distinction between two other products. Giving one specified element a right inverse while preserving cancellation on the new products forces those two products equal. The example has faithful injective actions and inverses on their respective images, so that partial recovery is insufficient to justify a total inverse. LQ13 also constructs a one-sided recovery extension retaining every original product and distinction. Cancellation fails there on the new factors. This establishes why a law over the expanded domain is an additional premise, even when its restriction to the old source remains correct. M70's reversible preparation construction makes no claim to preserve every such law as a total action on its enlarged carrier.

The addresses need no absolute names. A bijective renaming of all roles carries the facts, proposed identifications and every supporting equality chain together, preserving both obstruction conditions. What matters is which places refer to the same content across those relations. Renaming all occurrences of a role differs from identifying two roles or moving just one endpoint. Verdict §2's joint symmetry example exhibits this distinction: two roles can be exchanged without erasing the contrast between a repeated role and two different roles. The restriction can therefore be relational even when neither role has an intrinsic label.

The smallest application is VC60's common reading. Let the actual source readings have answers a and b. A proposed single decoded answer d is required to answer both faithfully:

    a = d,
    b = d.

These requirements force a=b. If the source readings positively distinguish a and b, that exact common-answer contact is impossible. Its impossibility is derived from the source distinction and the requested contact. No geometry, probability distribution, storage bound, global reversibility or temporal ordering has been supplied. The result does not prohibit keeping both answers in two fields, or carrying their contrast as a third answer to another question.

This application uses more given content than Step 1 alone: the two readings, their source relations and their registered contrast. Their actuality must be established. The bare fact that something is happening does not supply that apparatus or identify those sources.

There are precise ways to change the attempted contact. A source-dependent hidden record can make the full decoder inputs different. Two decoder constructions can return different answers from the same coarse image. A changed question can ignore the source distinction. Each can define a valid different interface. None realizes the original requirement that the same decoded answer answer both original readings. An internal remainder may help a local reset preserve information, as M70 constructs, because that contract permits different complete outputs. It cannot make unequal source answers into one faithful common answer. Unbounded capacity and preservation of the requested relation are separate issues.

This changes the completeness obligation for a concrete prohibition. The argument does not need to prove that every interaction in reality has been listed. It needs to establish the particular relations it uses, their shared referents, and the derivation of the obstruction. A newly discovered interaction may expose a false premise or a mistaken identification of addresses. If it preserves those premises, it cannot reverse the conclusion. Failure to find a witness in a finite catalogue is different: a larger catalogue may supply one. M71 requires the positive distinction and an actual implication to its collapse. An absent example supplies neither.

**A short proof and a stable prohibition answer different questions.** Stability in M71 depends on preservation of the actual supporting relations. The number of written proof steps depends on which derived rules have been given names. Replacing a long equality chain with one reference to its composition theorem shortens the expression while retaining every contact on which that instance depends. VC23 exhibits arbitrarily large minimally inconsistent chains with only binary values and unary or binary requirements. Their generic proof is short, but deleting any one contact removes that particular obstruction. A useful small certificate minimizes the necessary support within a specified premise vocabulary. It need not have a unique minimal support, and its source relations still need to be established.

VC23 also gives an explicit scope in which every incompatible family must contain a finite incompatible subfamily, even with infinitely many addresses: finite local carriers and admission fully specified by finite-address requirements. Its counterexamples show why a finite description of each requirement alone is insufficient. This separates stability of a found obstruction, existence of a finite ground witness and availability of a finite generic proof. The research priority is to expose the relations whose preservation makes the prohibition unavoidable. A short declaration of impossibility cannot replace them.

There is a corresponding prohibition on the witness medium. VC23 excludes exact certification of an unbounded existence question by any countable network of finite-valued local contacts with complete-assignment admission. A natural-valued witness address supplies an exact alternative representation. The distinction concerns what the contacts can express, including their hidden support, and does not require a metric. It also exposes a misleading form of closure: assertions can support one another at every local step while none reaches the source occurrence they assert. Such a network is an actual form in the model. Its existence does not certify its claimed referent. Requiring a finite generated witness reaching that referent supplies a stronger relation than recursive agreement.

The positive version has the same scope. If an addressed derivation forces a specified question's answer to equal an established answer, every realization preserving its premises gives that answer. Other questions and carriers can remain undetermined. Actual jointly established premises supply the connection to the given reality. A constructed type supplies a realization of the formal constraints, but its existence alone does not establish that those constraints describe the intended physical event. Conversely, a model with no realization cannot identify an actual answer by vacuity. Givenness supplies a source to preserve, not a rule for declaring an inscription true.

The static relations already contain this argument. `AnswersThroughSharedDecoder` derives the necessary equality from the actual common decoder. `CommonAnswerAcrossRealizations` derives agreement across two constructions that share one source reading, although their other memories and decoders can differ. `ZeroSuccessorBoundary` binds a positive constructor distinction to the two actual answer fields. `Checks/CommonBoundary.swift` checks these generic implications, permits separately stored unequal answers and refuses a common decoder for them. No protocol naming unrestricted completeness is needed. Those type proofs establish their addressed implications. Applying them to a physical contact still requires the source relation just stated.

M71 isolates a necessary restriction on a realized distinction: a joining cannot preserve its witnessing relations while identifying what those relations distinguish. This is a concrete structural resistance. It does not determine the entire contact relation of reality. Nor does existence of the distinction establish that a specified later cut must recover it. M50 states that additional temporal relation. The conclusion closes one route from an actual distinction to a prohibition without first selecting a metric or claiming an exhaustive model of the whole.

There is a corresponding test for an unresolved question. If the established relations concern a retained source H but put no condition on the next local answer, the two constructions `RetainedWhole<H, Never>` and `RetainedWhole<H, Lit1>` retain exactly H and have positively different current answers. They preserve every reading of H. This is not a choice between two actual worlds: it separates two extensions of an incomplete description of one given source. It does not establish that both extensions can occur physically. It establishes that equations about H alone cannot select their added field in this grammar. M70 gives the stronger separation for whole families of proposed local laws. Requiring the new field to answer an earlier question adds a relation, and that relation can turn the open choice into M71's forced answer or obstruction. An actual change may alter current readings while retaining the source event in the history. Calling its altered output the same earlier reading would be the mistake.

A retained distinction supplies a direct example independent of the chosen storage layout. Suppose one recovery decoder r satisfies r(G(x))=x for the complete output of each admitted input. For distinct inputs x and y, a local reset can make their workspace outputs equal, but the complete outputs must still differ: otherwise applying r would identify x and y. If the complete output is represented by workspace together with the rest of the whole, their remaining coordinates must distinguish the pair. M31 shows that this difference can require joint access to several coordinates. No particular register, material or probability distribution enters this argument. Every implementation preserving the stated inputs has this constraint. M35 adds the finite residual-capacity bound.

This is resistance in the structural sense relevant here: a proposed continuation cannot both erase every retained distinction and satisfy recovery. The obstruction is the incompatible relation, regardless of who proposes the continuation. It does not depend on a conscious form failing to understand the record. Quantitative force, duration or thermal cost becomes a further question about specified interactions and readings, to be constrained by the same method.

Existence Is Finite supplies a stronger uniqueness result at a different level. Finite generation, recoverable ordered pairing and a floor distinct from every pair determine one free constructor medium up to the unique constructor-preserving isomorphism. Thus descriptions of that medium do not introduce alternative structural-equality answers. Recovery proves injectivity of pairing by applying the two projections to an alleged output equality. A retained distinction between a leaf and a construction makes their images disjoint. These are explicit relations from which the representation result follows.

Whole-history retention must be applied at its actual scope. It need not make a local compressed result recover its two inputs on its own. Their distinction may remain in other parts or joint relations. Nor does retention require every formal ordered pair to be an admissible interaction. The free-medium theorem additionally uses total pairing and finite generation. The exact next foundational questions are therefore whether the intended complete construction record supplies operand recovery and a distinct terminal, and which constructor applications it admits. Those questions replace an unspecified demand to become physical.

One whole does not require one answer to every partially specified question. It requires that claims about shared actual relations remain compatible. The productive target is the least set of those relations that forces the answer being asked. A unique answer can be established before a unique construction, encoding or complete dynamics is known. `ReadingFactors` and `SharedDecoder` already express a fragment of this route: supplied source relations lead through one actual decoder application, forcing their answers to agree. No new protocol is needed to rename that implication.

Existence Is Finite EF8 now makes the construction requirement weaker and more precise. Operand recovery, a distinct origin and finite generation give unique decomposition under partial construction. Fixing which trees are admitted then fixes the partial medium. Even before that language is fixed, structural equality of two shared admitted descriptions is the same in every such medium. Total pairing is needed for the unrestricted medium, not for this answer. EF9 supplies the complementary internal example: different full records can give the same local result while preserving their different operands. `SameBoundaryForcesOperands` derives an equal recovered answer across different histories and local outcomes. These results locate both invariance and remaining freedom in addressed relations of the construction.

VC61–VC65 supply the corresponding internal acquisition: distinguish a positive obstruction from an unresolved query, acquire evidence of compatibility from resident readings, and retain the actual archive address through instrument reset. Compatibility alone does not supply evidence of it, and adding the same blank memory does not separate merged inputs. VC66–VC67 then give a concrete temporal boundary. An exchange with a resident zero makes a previously omitted contrast relevant to the next question. The displaced value remains inside the whole and affects the following exchange. The earlier certificate keeps its earlier question, while the changed question requires that additional distinction.

## Detail: Questions that force an answer

The comparison with classical accounts must name the competing contract. Classical mathematics can express every construction below. VI's proposed demand is that a usable answer retain the relations that justify it, including the source of a reference or preparation. A simpler description can still be correct for its own question.

| Exact question | Forced answer under the stated relations | Description that fails this contract |
|---|---|---|
| Can a deterministic reading choose one of two exchanged candidates while preserving every symmetry of its full available input? | No. VI1a forces an invariant answer, whereas either selected position changes under exchange. | Choosing the first written label and treating that convention as recovered identity. |
| Can the same symmetric input determine a probability law on exactly two exchanged outcomes? | Yes. If the kernel is equivariant and normalized, each probability is 1/2. This fixes the law, not the individual occurrence. | Inferring a unique event from a unique distribution, or assuming deterministic selection was established. |

These exclusions concern explicit alternative representations and inferences, not an opposing universal classical theory. VI1a gives an exact local criterion for symmetry: the answers fixed by the actual input's stabilizer. The unknown internal object may be the relation needed to reduce that stabilizer. Finding such a requirement does not identify its material carrier or establish access to it.

## Detail: Where further precision changes the conclusion

Precision is relative to a stated obligation. Fix a nonempty family D of admitted contact descriptions, a determinate target q on D, and retained evidence E. At an acquired image e, write Dₑ={x∈D:E(x)=e}. The evidence determines q exactly when q is constant on this nonempty family. An actual decoder or derivation must supply that answer. One can finish this question while many source descriptions remain. A positive pair x,y∈Dₑ with q(x)≠q(y) instead certifies that this evidence lacks a required distinction. A certificate about one actual source is narrower than a uniform certificate on D. These are the existing obligations of Verdict §6c and VC84, applied to research precision.

This also locates the value of further questions. For a fixed finite family of additional readings, their joint evidence is sufficient exactly when they separate every pair with different q-answers still merged by E. VC84 proves this criterion and permits several different minimal sufficient families. An individual question need not narrow the set of target answers immediately. On the four binary pairs with target a XOR b, reading either coordinate alone leaves both target answers possible after either result. Reading both determines the answer. Rejecting the first reading because it did not yet settle q would reject every successful route through this interface. By contrast, reading an unrelated coordinate can halve a larger candidate set while leaving both values of an unread target coordinate possible. Candidate counts alone do not rank questions by their relevance.

This sufficiency test presumes that the readings can be joined on the stated source. For interventions, VC26 shows why separate availability does not guarantee a successful sequence: an early contact can destroy a distinction needed later. A useful question therefore needs a justified route to its answer and a stated effect on subsequent contacts. The criterion supplies neither those routes nor a unique cheapest policy without their costs and domains.

Four places require different precision. The source binding must identify which contact supports the claim. The target interpretation must specify what counts as the same answer. The continuation domain must include the changes across which reuse is claimed. Numerical bounds are needed when a quantitative separation decides the question. Uncertainty about a detail outside these dependencies can remain. A claim of physical impossibility needs its realized premises preserved by every extension it excludes, as M71 proves. A proposed interpretation can remain open without being counted as such a prohibition.

For the additive comparisons of S25, suppose each addressed offset obeys a justified bound |δᵢ−δ̂ᵢ|≤εᵢ in the same rate unit. For the loop sums L=Σδᵢ and L̂=Σδ̂ᵢ, the triangle inequality gives |L−L̂|≤Σ εᵢ. Hence |L̂|>Σ εᵢ certifies L≠0. No statistical independence is assumed. If the resulting interval contains both zero and nonzero values, that bound alone decides neither exact equality nor its failure. The singleton interval {0} would certify equality. Sharper joint bounds or a structural identity can settle a question that the separate bounds leave open. Changing local clock origins cancels in the loop sum, so absolute origins need not be acquired for this question. This numerical argument remains conditional on the actual additive comparison and calibration relations. A more precise number cannot repair a wrong source binding or comparison model.

For the foundational chain, a useful question exposes a premise that several conclusions use differently. LQ16's self-contact, M55's proper record extension and M72's recovery boundary are such points. Whether a repeated local form carries a new occurrence changes the time claim. Whether an earlier distinction factors through a later complete cut changes the retention claim. Whether a proposed replacement preserves the actual contact changes the identification claim. Each can be tested by an addressed equality or recovery equation, with a positive difference supplying an obstruction. Resolving every microscopic field, fixing an absolute origin or selecting one canonical name is unnecessary when the requested conclusion is already the same throughout those remaining choices.

## Detail: §2b. A Past Distinction Crosses Every Cut

This section is an entry. It gathers laws of this paper, of Verdict and of Distance that decide when a past distinction remains answerable, and it adds no theorem of its own. Labels marked history refer to accepted research notes listed in §7.

**Question.** What must a whole retain so that a distinction made earlier can be answered later? Which records of the past can keep growing?

**Result.**
1. A later reading answers a question about an earlier occurrence exactly when the question factors through that reading on the declared compatible histories. Established recovery then requires a witness of the distinction at every complete cut in between.
2. The witness can be joint. A local reading can coincide on two histories while their joint relation still separates them.
3. Growth has conditions. A whole that returns completely carries no count of its returns. Under an invariant probability a reading that never decreases along the step is almost surely unchanged by it.

**Recovery is factorization.** Fix a family H of compatible complete histories, a question Q about an earlier occurrence and a reading E of a later cut. A decoder D recovers Q exactly when Q=D∘E on H (M50). Two histories with one E-image and different Q-answers leave no single-valued decoder. Let J join admitted prefixes p and continuations f inside one whole, and let a reading r of f recover q(p) on every joined pair. Two prefixes that share a continuation then have the same earlier answer, and different answers need disjoint continuation families (M72). Here every prefix has a nonempty continuation family N(p), and the cut is complete for J: (p,f)∈J exactly when (c(p),f)∈S for some relation S. Every complete cut between the occurrence and the reading then carries the distinction. Without nonempty families a constant cut of an empty relation could merge different answers.

**A joint witness.** The carrier of that recovery need not be one register. In a reversible exchange a local reading can miss a distinction that the joint state keeps. Under VC26a's conditions, a comparison has reciprocal differential dependence. Let the complete whole be a finite-dimensional nondegenerate symplectic product over the cut, with every controller, clock and memory on its assigned side. Let Φ(m,c)=(F(m,c),ψ(m,c)) be a symplectic map on an open product. Then D_cF=0 if and only if D_mψ=0 at each point. On connected open factors this gives equivalence of global independence. Dependence in one direction only across the full cut requires a failed premise, for example a nonproduct form or a prepared closed layer (G14d). Individual directed edges between smaller subsystems can be one-way. For a finite whole with a fixed residual alphabet, exact recovery of the whole from a compressed image needs at least as many residual values as the largest fiber of the image (M35).

**When a record can grow.** A complete recurrence carries no distinguishable lap count. A counter that separates the returns is a coordinate in which the whole has not returned (M51). Let U preserve a probability measure on the entire whole. A measurable reading V with V(Ux)≥V(x) almost surely then satisfies V(Ux)=V(x) almost surely (M36). The step leaves it unchanged outside a null set, while V itself can vary across the whole. Let U be a bimeasurable bijection preserving μ, and let Γ be measurable and forward invariant, with μ(Γ)<∞. Its entry cut B=Γ∖UΓ is null, because μ(UΓ)=μ(Γ) and UΓ⊆Γ (history SA49.4). The measure of the entire carrier need not be finite. Recoverability alone defines no strict temporal order. That order needs a proper extension of the record (M55).

**A residue is not a counter by name.** In the field model of the research history the full Source phase is an actual residue (history AD97b). Its formula can depend on the pass number n (history HPU77), and a nonzero resonance can return the same Source at the ends of different passes (history HOR45). That dependence alone does not make the phase an injective record of n, because the angle is cyclic and the contact times depend on the contents. An injective record must be exhibited with its carrier, access and update law.

**Scope of §2b.** These are conditions on what a record can certify. They do not supply a physical carrier, a reader or the energy of an update. A measure premise is a premise. A Hamiltonian field gives no finite invariant measure by its form alone.
