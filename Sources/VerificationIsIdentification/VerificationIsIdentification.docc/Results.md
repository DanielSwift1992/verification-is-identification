# How a Result Is Recorded

A result record connects a question and its grounds to what has been established. It lists what a reader needs to check the answer, how far it reaches, and what it leaves for the next step.

## Overview

A result here relates a question about the actual whole to a derived claim or construction and its permitted continuation. A reader checks it in one order. First what was asked, then what was given, then how the answer was obtained, how far it reaches, and what remains. Each step is one field of the record, and each field guards one known misreading.

| Field | What it holds | The misreading it prevents | Grounds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Question | the question `q`. For a neighboring question `q′`, its link to the original requirement and who or what checks that link | a convenient neighboring answer counted as the original one | U2c, §1b of <doc:verification_is_identification> |
| Grounds | the actual source, its representation, the conditions, and the compatible set `S` | the lone member of a model taken for the thing itself while the target lies outside `S` | §0 "On encoding", §1a |
| Equality | what "the same answer" means, and which operations are available | equal readings taken for one source, or for an executor | §0 step 4; ``Law`` §0′ |
| Derivation | a witness, a construction, or an exact conditional derivation | a named conclusion standing in for its derivation | U2c, §1a; ``Law`` §T for a typed translation |
| Reach | scope, quantifiers, the checked limit, and the address of a refusal | a few examples, or one failed attempt, deciding a whole class | U2c, §1; ``Law`` §0′ and §T |
| Status | the property established and how far its check went | a missing derivation read as a negation | U1; ``Law`` §0′ |
| Continuation | the actual result, its residue, who can access it, and the grounds of the next use | a possible residue entering a composition in place of the produced one | §1a, §1b |
| Relation to the task | what was provided rather than acquired, and its price | a conditional scheme or an approximation counted as a performed action | §1b |

A small example fills only the fields in play. It states its scope, and when it claims no execution, its Continuation field says so.

The Question field records how the answer remains tied to the original requirement. A correct derivation checked only for a substituted question does not by itself establish that link. A participant or an instrument can check it against the retained requirement, including through a record that encodes the requirement and supplies the corresponding comparison. Such a check catches part of these errors. Adequacy remains a premise of the internal check unless established from further grounds. §1b U4 of <doc:verification_is_identification> states a sufficient rule for transporting a proved claim back to its original question.

## Outcomes and states

For a fixed question and compatible set, three cases are distinguished. No candidate fits: `S` is empty. One answer: `S` is nonempty and `q(S) = {a}`. Several answers: `q` still varies on `S`. "Not yet established" is a state of the check and stays separate from the three outcomes.

For an action on a declared family of starts, the outcome is whether one admissible program meets the requirement at every admitted start: the intersection of the admissible sets over the family is empty or nonempty. A nonempty intersection is existence as a set. The supplied choice and its realization are recorded under Continuation (<doc:acquisition_keeps_its_question>, Adequacy).

A record also names its relation to the original requirement: identification of `q`, identification of a neighboring `q′`, a proved limit, an approximation, or a construction with provided parts. One move can carry two of these. Proof status and execution status are recorded separately.

Record each sufficient derivation and the premises it uses. When a premise loses support, review the applications that depend on it. The claim remains established at its stated reach if the remaining valid grounds suffice for that claim. Otherwise record the claim as not yet established, unless separate grounds establish its negation. A correctly recorded measurement remains a fact about its original setup. Its use on a changed setup requires an established relation to the new conditions. Selective review is complete only when the recorded dependencies cover the actual grounds of the affected applications.

> Important: The record presents a result, and its Derivation field carries the proof. A filled form adds no grounds of its own. Free-form prose can contain an argument. State which premises and inferences were checked and which were supplied. Merely storing an explanation supplies no check of that explanation. A sentence used as a premise in a later step needs grounds of its own or an explicit status as a supplied assumption.
