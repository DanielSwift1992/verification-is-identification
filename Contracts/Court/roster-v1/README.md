# Roster question and replay · v1

`Tools judge roster <sources.swift...> --protocol P` asks for all nominal conformers of the declared protocol P in the complete presented flat world. The deterministic JSON response is `vi.court.roster/1`; the roster is ordered by original path, byte offset and nominal name. Its `inputs` bind every original UTF-8 source by SHA-256. Each row has its original byte/line address, a protocol-admission certificate, every associated-type axis of P and its ancestors, the axis's normal form and its DefinitionReduction derivation. A normal nominal may separately carry literal bytes. Repeated paths, unknown sources, unavailable protocol definitions and unsupported source regions do not yield an empty success.

The question is a static relation. Let D be the finite domain of presented nominal declarations, P the requested declared protocol and A(P) its declared associated axes. The roster is exactly `{d ∈ D | the presented conformance closure of d names P}`. Each row additionally requires admission `d : P` under the existing ApplicationCourt calculus. For every a ∈ A(P), its field expression `d.a` reduces under the source equations to a closed, irreducible nominal n. The answer consists of these declarations and equalities; it supplies no program, transition table or new equations to run. An unadmitted candidate is not silently removed from the domain.

The application certificate is the existing finite graph of root/form/member/contract relations, dependencies, strict prerequisites and source-defined reductions. ApplicationCourt verifies the complete dependency boundary and excludes circular strict admission. The projection rules are precisely DefinitionReduction's name, arguments, field, unfold and join rules. Their local equations come from the addressed source; no proposed right-hand side is trusted. Induction on the derivation tree gives its endpoints; join requires identical shared endpoints. The replay then checks the original input `d.a`, the returned endpoint and irreducibility of every returned subterm under the same definitions. It rejects an unresolved axis, unknown nominal or still-reducing alias. This admits the declared relation conditionally on these existing calculi and the read source; the fixture observations below are not its admission.

`Tools judge roster-replay <response.json> <sources.swift...> --protocol P` only replays the supplied proof graphs and reduction trees; it does not search for replacement proofs. It also checks the exact candidate/axis coverage, order, source hashes, addresses and carried bytes against the original source. Input labels, row names and address paths compare by UTF-8 bytes, so a canonically equivalent Unicode spelling is not a replacement for the requested label. Dropping a candidate, replacing a normal form or changing a source does not leave a valid certificate. Completeness is relative to the bounded flat source reader, not all Swift. The source reader itself has no grammar-derivation certificate under P4 yet: debt T0084. Replay does not attest provenance of externally supplied bytes or the implementation of the replayer.

A direct `static var typeName: String { <literal> }` (or single static-let literal) is a byte carrier. Its response object has `judged: false`, base64 `bytes`, and the original content's start/end byte offsets and line. Those bytes exclude literal delimiters and retain escapes, Unicode and CRLF exactly as written; they are not Swift's evaluated String, do not dedent multiline strings and do not evaluate interpolation. A raw `#"…"#` literal conveniently carries shell quotes without escaping. A literal property adds no judgment. Interpolated literals, computed properties, concatenations and methods are outside this reader's carrier fragment. The consumer must distinguish an absent carrier from a proved relation; commands are executed by the separate physical host.

`holds` requires a nonempty complete admitted roster; `empty` is a zero-row result, never holds. Unsupported syntax, unavailable definitions and resource boundaries yield `unsupported` with an address. An unestablished candidate's membership/axes yield `not-established` with its original address; other admitted rows may be disclosed, but the complete answer is not holds. Exit codes are respectively 0, 2, 2 and 1. Legacy where/count questions and their frozen contracts retain their meanings. Open generic nominals, conditional declarations, nested declarations, actor/class declarations, unknown top-level syntax, functions/local types, computed carriers, conflicting axes and imports beyond a direct module name are disclosed as unsupported rather than omitted. The reader does not implement module resolution or a full Swift compiler.

`contract.json` and the SHA-bound Swift fixtures declare finite input/outcome observations. `Checks/court-contract.py --roster --tools /path/to/Tools` executes them and replays the successful/empty certificates. They do not establish general equivalence or judge admission. `RosterTests` additionally mutate proof coverage, admissions and endpoints and observe original CRLF/Unicode carriage. The actual law-record adversaries are declared by transformations in `Contracts/Checks/law-record-controls.json`, without copying the owner roster. Both observation drivers are debt T0084; test selection is not proof. Rust/wasm support for this new question is debt T0084 and is not claimed here.
