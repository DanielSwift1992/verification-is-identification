## Detail: §4h. Recovering dependence from admissible records

### M66

**M66 (when records determine a fixed prerequisite order).** Fix a finite set E of addressed occurrences inside the whole and a family C of admitted partial records, each represented by its subset of E. Require ∅ and E to belong to C. Membership means that an occurrence is included in that record, not that every internal reader can recover it. The family specifies possible records for this inquiry. It is not a population of realities outside Ω, and E need not exhaust the whole.

Define a relation without a prior clock:

    e ≼ f  iff  every H in C containing f also contains e.

This relation is reflexive and transitive. Every admitted record is downward closed for it: recording f requires recording each e≼f. The converse is exact under the following condition:

    C is all downward-closed subsets for ≼
        iff C is closed under unions and intersections.

*Proof.* Downward-closed subsets are closed under both operations. Conversely, suppose C has those closures. For f∈E, let P_f be the intersection of all admitted records containing f. There is at least one such record, E. The intersection is admitted and contains exactly the e with e≼f. Every downward-closed D is the union of P_f over f∈D. Each P_f is contained in D, and each f belongs to its P_f, proving equality. Union closure and ∅∈C now give D∈C. Finiteness makes the displayed unions and intersections finite. QED

The relation is antisymmetric exactly when C separates each pair of distinct occurrences: some admitted record contains one without the other. If two addresses always occur together, their mutual prerequisite relation is a preorder equivalence. Quotienting by that equivalence gives a partial order on the distinguishable blocks. It does not prove that the original occurrences were identical. Their separation may require a richer record interface.

These are constraints on record inclusion. Interpreting them as causal requirements of a process additionally needs the process's actual admissible transitions. A selected sample, a reporting convention or a restricted preparation can correlate entries without making one event produce another. Internal closure by itself establishes none of those transition bindings.

Under both closures and pair separation, this is the unique partial order whose downward-closed records are C. Indeed, every partial order can be recovered from its downward-closed sets by the displayed test: if e is not below f, the principal past P_f contains f and excludes e. This proves uniqueness from the admitted family, rather than choosing an order and calling it necessary. A sampled subfamily can introduce false prerequisites by missing its distinguishing records. Coverage of C remains an obligation.

Union and intersection obey the distributive set laws here, but complementation need not give an admitted record. For a strict prerequisite a≺b, the complement of P_a contains b and omits a, so it is not downward closed. Under the closure and separation premises, every complement is admitted exactly when the order has no strict prerequisites. In that case every subset is admitted. Thus this algebra of histories is generally not Boolean. This does not reject classical reasoning about sets. It prevents interpreting independent Boolean choices as the admissible event combinations when actual prerequisites exclude some of them.

The closure conditions have content. Take E={a,b,c} and admit every subset except {c}. Here c requires at least one of a or b. Both {a,c} and {b,c} are admitted, but their intersection {c} is not. No distinct individual occurrence is required by c in every admitted record. The recovered preorder is therefore equality, whose downward-closed family would incorrectly admit {c}. A single fixed prerequisite order loses the alternative support. A second obstruction is incompatibility: for E={a,b,c}, the family ∅,{a},{b},E has both endpoints but excludes {a,b}. Its fixed prerequisite relation also admits {a,b}. The failure of union closure carries information that pairwise precedence alone misses.

The first family has a static realization. Interpret Never and Lit1 as the absence and presence of each of two addressed prerequisite records. `WitnessedOccurrence<R>` appends a successful actual reading, retaining its input and the operation that supplied its Lit1 answer. Admit the two templates FirstOccurrence<B> and SecondOccurrence<A> in Checks/ClosedInformation.swift, with A and B restricted to Never or Lit1. These fix both the actual projection and its recorded operation name. A first projection accepts exactly the inputs with a present. A second projection accepts exactly those with b present. Neither accepts (Never,Never). Together with the four inputs before appending c, their projected occurrence sets are precisely the seven members of C. This exhaustiveness refers to that finite projection grammar, not to arbitrary conformances of PredicateReading.

`OccurrenceImage<R>` reads the prior pair and the appended answer from the actual record, omitting the operation field. On the input (Lit1,Lit1), the two successful projections give the same occurrence image while the complete records retain different operations. Generic checks derive both construction families, source recovery and operation retention. Negative checks reject appending with either absent witness, identifying the complete records from their common image, and reading a different construction's source. The disjunction has not lost its realized support from the whole. This particular interface has omitted which support supplied it.

Thus absence of an outside does not force every internal description to have a fixed prerequisite order. A description can merge different internal supports. Recovering a richer relation requires retaining or reading those supports. M66 gives the precise closure criterion for when the simpler description suffices. The proof about arbitrary finite families is the set argument above. The Swift construction realizes its alternative-support obstruction and retained resolution, without claiming to encode every finite order.

The transfer question already has a kernel in VC60–VC62. A common decoded answer does not require common histories, and an established agreement on the actual resident source can reuse an observation through AnswerThroughAgreement. VC83 handles a changed mechanism for a specified question. VC84 separates the minimum answer content from the addressed inputs that can provide it: several incomparable input subsets may each suffice. Their intersection need not answer the question. Alternative realized support therefore needs neither a new equality principle nor a unique chosen carrier.

### M67

**M67 (a fixed order determines its permitted serializations, not a universal clock).** Apply M66 when its closure and separation premises are satisfied. A maximal chain of admitted records from ∅ to E adds exactly one occurrence at each step, and its sequence respects ≼. Conversely, any ordering of E respecting ≼ has admitted prefixes and gives such a chain. For the first direction, if a chain step added more than one occurrence, choose a minimal new occurrence. Adding it alone to the earlier record gives an intermediate downward-closed record, contradicting maximality. The converse follows because each prefix contains every prerequisite of its members.

For distinct e,f, e≼f exactly when every such serialization puts e before f. One direction follows from the prefix requirement. If e is not below f, add f before e to the finite order. This creates no cycle, since a cycle would require an existing path from e to f. Repeatedly selecting a minimal remaining element gives a serialization of the augmented order, and hence one of the original order with f before e. QED

There is exactly one serialization iff the order is total. An incomparable pair permits both relative orders by the same argument. Thus a chain recording a before b does not establish that their common prerequisites force that order. A particular internal recorder can add and retain that distinction. Its chosen chain then belongs to the richer record being described.

Any two serializations can be connected by exchanges of adjacent incomparable occurrences. To prove this, take the first occurrence of the target serialization and move it leftward in the other. Each crossed occurrence is incomparable with it: a required predecessor would also have preceded it in the target, and a required successor could not precede it in the current valid sequence. Fix that first position and repeat on the remaining finite sequence. Consequently, a question about serializations factors through the underlying partial order exactly when its answer is unchanged by every such exchange. This is the existing question-factorization criterion applied to the connected family of serializations. The question may still distinguish other partial orders.

G14b constructs the local answer agreement for actual disjoint pair applications. Extending that agreement through a larger execution additionally requires valid intermediate applications and preservation by their surrounding operations. M67 does not infer commutation of arbitrary effects from a diagram. It also does not erase a recorder's selected order from the full retained history.

Neither the number of occurrences nor the number of serializations supplies physical duration or an entropy law. An entropy over serializations first requires that those alternatives be distinguished by the declared reading and assigned probabilities. An append-only record can have a strict prefix order without increasing uncertainty, as M48 and M63 already distinguish. The fundamental gain is a reconstruction question with an exact answer: which prerequisite relations does this admitted record family force, and what information does a simpler order omit?

Space S27 now identifies one missing relation precisely. Even a linearly ordered real carrier with a retained unit interval admits different positive additive durations. A supplied hierarchy of equal halves, in contrast, forces the normalized interval answer to agree in all increasing real readings. Such a reading exists exactly when its grid separates every distinct ordered pair of occurrences. The proof does not require the occurrence carrier to fill a continuum. Finite depth gives a bound unless an exact constructed endpoint is identified. The value carrier matters: lexicographically ordered pairs retain all midpoint relations while permitting a change below every real subdivision of one reference unit. Thus an internal clock reconstruction needs justified interval comparisons and a value contract, beyond the prerequisite order reconstructed here.

**Two-sided extension gives an order of regions without choosing an event orientation.** Fix an internal occurrence g and two addressed arms of occurrences extending from it. Let I[m,n] retain g, the first m occurrences of the first arm and the first n of the second, with the same occurrence correspondences and all specified relations on those retained occurrences. Assume all the considered pairs of prefixes are jointly admitted. Then actual inclusion gives

    I[m,n] ⊆ I[p,q]  iff  m≤p and n≤q.

Either arm can extend while the other is retained. Adding one occurrence at each arm gives the same region in either order. The two intermediate regions differ. If the arms have interchangeable content and contact laws, their exchange preserves this inclusion relation and fixes the central region. It does not select a first arm. A chain of construction steps records an additional choice of order, as M67 already proves. The arms and their joint availability are premises, rather than consequences of a centre having two incidences.

This permits a precise meaning of construction in both directions: the retained region can extend at either end. It does not yet make one end past and the other future. A record-dependency law in which each new occurrence retains its inward predecessor points outward on both arms. A line orientation through g points inward on one arm and outward on the other. Reading the common incidence pattern does not equate those laws. Nor does revealing an earlier part of a description show that the earlier event was produced by that revelation.

The static example now includes identical local rules at different places. `RecordedIndependentOrders<F,G>` lifts each rule to the full paired source, composes the two valid orders and retains their actual lifted applications. Its generic equality recovers the same initial source and final answer. `SameRuleAtTwoSides` uses one root-growth rule on both fields. The final regions coincide, but the first recorded applications have answers `(node,empty)` and `(empty,node)`. `DISTANCE_SAME_LOCAL_RULE_ERASES_ORDER` rejects identity of those complete records. Keeping only the local rule name would record the same rule twice in either order and lose which place changed first. These are represented constructions, not assertions that every formal serialization is a physically admitted history.

**A scalar clock can increase without representing the complete order.** On the prefix regions, τ(m,n)=m+n strictly increases on every proper inclusion. Yet I[1,0] and I[0,1] are distinct and receive the same value. This equality alone is no contradiction: distinct incomparable occurrences may share a date. Indeed the four-region square with m,n∈{0,1} admits a scalar representation of its strict order by that rank, with the two middle regions tied. It fails to identify the regions, but it preserves that strict-order question.

The next finite pattern excludes even this weaker scalar representation. Set A=I[1,0], B=I[0,1] and C=I[2,0]. Then A is a proper part of C, while B is incomparable with each. If a reading into one linear order satisfied

    H is a proper part of K  iff  τ(H)<τ(K),

incomparability would force τ(A)=τ(B)=τ(C), contradicting τ(A)<τ(C). Thus no scalar reading can preserve and reflect all those precedence answers, even with tied dates. The witness has only three regions and remains an obstruction in any extension preserving those relations. Choosing a scalar required only to increase on the admitted inclusions is still possible. Its other numerical comparisons cannot all be read back as prerequisites. The three records already have finite tree presentations in G1's actual address grammar: a common root with only its left child occupied, only its right child occupied, or its left child and that child's children occupied.

**Independent records force a lower bound on exact ordered coordinates.** Let n≥1 addressed occurrences be independently retainable over one common record: every subset gives an admitted record, with its original occurrence identities. Suppose a k-coordinate reading T into a product of linear orders is required to preserve and reflect inclusion by componentwise comparison:

    H⊆K  iff  T_j(H)≤T_j(K) for every j=1,…,k.

Then k≥n, and n presence coordinates attain the bound. To prove the lower bound, omit the common record from notation and put A_i={i}, B_i={1,…,n}∖{i}. Since A_i is not contained in B_i, some coordinate must have T_j(A_i)>T_j(B_i). A single coordinate cannot serve two different i and l: the crossed inclusions A_i⊆B_l and A_l⊆B_i would give

    T_j(A_i) ≤ T_j(B_l) < T_j(A_l) ≤ T_j(B_i) < T_j(A_i).

Hence the n refusals need n different coordinates. The upper bound reads whether each actual occurrence is present. QED

This is the standard order-dimension obstruction, whose singleton/complement example is recalled by [Kim et al., §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.08641). Its use here is a finite refusal of an underspecified clock representation. The lower bound needs only those witnessed records, their crossed inclusions and their omitted occurrences, not a catalogue of the entire whole. It constrains an exact product-order reading. A single real number can encode a finite record with an arbitrary decoder, but its ordinary numerical order then does not supply this inclusion relation. Other relations may accompany a scalar physical clock and carry what its date omits. No claim of n spatial dimensions or n fundamental physical times follows from the bound.

**Past, future and traversal have different addresses.** For a specified strict event order ≺ and an occurrence e, define P(e)={x:x≺e} and F(e)={x:e≺x}. One relation x≺e gives x its predecessor role relative to e and e its successor role relative to x. The two roles are determined together. In an admitted record extension adding y and the relation x≺y, the retained occurrence x also acquires its past role relative to y. The extension adds a relation involving x without replacing its earlier recorded content. A reading of that old content need not describe x's enlarged relational context. An occurrence incomparable with e belongs to neither P(e) nor F(e). Thus the complement of a chosen past need not be its future. These definitions use the declared order. They cannot orient a general contact or turn mutual support into temporal precedence.

For a≺c and b≺c with a and b incomparable, both a,b,c and b,a,c serialize the same prerequisite structure. Their first item differs, so firstness in that list is insufficient to identify an intrinsic first event. If an internal recorder distinguishes which it acquired first, its acquisition adds an ordered relation to the full account. Forgetting that recorder can recover the common prerequisite image. Keeping it can distinguish the two acquisitions. The reader and its traversal remain inside reality in both descriptions. A change of enumeration is harmless to a question exactly under M67's exchange criterion, rather than because all traversals are physically interchangeable.

M72 expresses the same distinction at a contact boundary. A reading on either side can restrict which descriptions of the other side fit the same actual joining. Learning a later record can therefore determine an earlier answer that was previously open in the account. This statement does not supply an operation rewriting the occurrence. Likewise, listing every member of a finite event family is different from possessing a readable representation of all reality. A relational description can specify a question while leaving most of the whole undescribed.

Neither a finished graph viewed from outside nor a graph growing against an external clock is required by these arguments. In the record model, proper extension is an internal relation between records. In an account of actual becoming, what extends, which occurrence establishes that extension and how it can be registered must belong to the account. Calling the whole static or growing without a distinguishing internal relation does not settle that question. A global numerical time coordinate is a further presentation to justify. Even when an order admits such a coordinate, its numerical origin, scale and a unique traversal need not be determined by that order.

**A reversible capacity constraint can escape every shorter compatibility check.** M67's record-family premises cannot be recovered from pairwise compatibility alone. For any m≥3, take the forms K={0,1}ᵐ with the all-one form omitted. A coordinate toggle is admitted exactly when its result also belongs to K. Every admitted toggle has the same toggle as its inverse. From 0ᵐ, any sequence filling at most m−1 distinct coordinates is admitted in every order, and the result depends only on which coordinates were filled. Filling all m is forbidden in every order. Thus checks of every shorter distinct-filling sequence miss the joint obstruction. No fixed test depth covers all such media as m grows.

This is also a limit on interpreting resistance as information loss. Each admitted transition is one-to-one and reversible, yet the medium excludes a continuation. The unrestricted bit-flip formulas commute, but they forget the missing form. The partial laws agree on final values wherever both orders are admitted. Their domains can nevertheless differ: start with only j vacant, clear an occupied i, then fill j. Toggling j first would leave no vacancy and is forbidden. The toggle values alone therefore omit part of the interaction law. VC46–VC47 already specify the missing domain relation.

For m=3, the admitted forms after filling each pair retain a directly checked vacancy. All three possible vacancy clauses reject 111 in `Checks/Interactions.swift`. These are static compatibility witnesses, not a physical filling process or a universal transition implementation. The model's capacity law is stipulated. It is a counterconstruction to the proposed inference from reversible local behavior to unrestricted joint availability.

VC23b then asks what internal form can witness that capacity condition. For three positions, three central states express its existence through binary contacts. A deterministic witness acquisition respecting all position permutations needs seven states. A cyclic orientation reduces that conditional bound to four, and a total order permits three. The change concerns actual relations of the chosen medium. No observer is added as a primitive to select a vacant position. The bound identifies what such a selection would require. A physical interpretation would still have to establish the realized contact relations, available acquisitions and resource measure.

Liquid LQ12–LQ14 lift this question to the state of the medium itself. LQ13 derives which replacements preserve an internal contact: a retained partner permits a uniform local replacement exactly when its compatibility set is included in the replacement's set. Equality gives the reversible case. The least complete boundary is determined by these sets, and need not be one identical value on both sides. LQ14 derives a necessary coordination scale by considering all admitted paths, including internal context changes. Vacancy, parity and repetition constraints require widths one, two and n in their complete compatibility envelopes. A temporary internal release of an equality contact can reduce a width-two replacement to width-one steps before restoring the contact. This gives a precise resistance question before assigning energy, mass or duration: which bounded changes cannot fit the actual relations, even through their permitted detours?

LQ15–LQ16 then ask which decomposition those questions may use. Independent blocks are recovered from free splices under the stated finite or finite-character conditions. A partial contact can instead determine two coupled roles through exact self-contact and contextual substitution. Retaining both complementary recombinations gives an involution wherever both are admitted. In the vacancy relation, one result can fit while its complement cannot, so two individually admitted sources do not guarantee this closed exchange. A proposed physical account of resistance must establish the full internal completion, including its admission, rather than assume that a legal local result has somewhere compatible to place every omitted distinction.

## Detail: §4i. What an unrestricted internal remainder permits

The gap after M29 is larger than one closed reset counterexample. Suppose the stronger recovery requirement is granted. Does that now select which local changes the whole can realize? M35 bounds a remainder once its carrier is fixed. The following construction tests what remains of that restriction when the carrier may still be supplied freely. All preparation, records and control positions below belong to the same declared whole.

W below describes complete configurations of the declared model, including every resident degree of freedom used by U. Finiteness of W is a premise for the recurrence and capacity results. It is not derived from finiteness of individual descriptions, and it does not identify a current configuration with Ω's entire history. Requiring all occurrence distinctions to remain in a present recoverable record is a stronger condition, addressed separately. No past occurrence is made external to reality by distinguishing its historical relation from a current record of it.

### M70

**M70 (unrestricted internal completion does not select a local deterministic law).** Let S be a nonempty finite local carrier and f:S→S any function. There is a finite complete carrier W, a permutation U of W and an internal prepared record b such that the local output of U(s,b) is f(s) for every s. The construction can respect every symmetry of S that respects f. More strongly, for any finite horizon N there is such a whole whose local outputs are fᵗ(s) for every 0≤t≤N. Thus closure, complete recovery and these symmetries do not by themselves rule out any finite local function over a prepared finite horizon. They constrain its completion and reuse when the actual internal resources are specified.

The differences counted in S are represented source distinctions. Unregistered names do not supply additional physical states. Any fixed internal reference or structure grounding those distinctions belongs to the complete model too. The theorem does not derive S, its interpretation or a supply of physical record forms from nonemptiness.

**One use with a complete inverse.** Let E be the disjoint union of one ready form b and one record [s] for each s∈S. Put W=S×E. For each source s, exchange the two whole forms

    p_s = (s,b),             q_s = (f(s),[s]).

Every p_s is different from every q_r because ready and recorded forms are disjoint. Distinct q_s retain distinct s even when their local outputs agree. These exchanges are therefore disjoint pairs. Define U by those exchanges and fix every other whole form. Then U² is the identity on all W, and the prepared use has exactly the requested local output. This is a permutation of a fixed carrier; the record was available before use. Applying U again restores both the source and readiness. It also undoes the local result, so this inverse is not a fresh use of f on that result.

If a permutation g of S satisfies gf=fg, let it fix b and carry [s] to [g(s)]. It sends p_s to p_g(s) and q_s to q_g(s), so it commutes with U. The record inherits the source's relational transformation; no choice of unrelated labels or preferred source is needed. This does not contradict LQ16's symmetry obstruction for a prescribed residual carrier. It supplies a different carrier whose source records have the required symmetry.

The local law can also occupy an internal address. For any finite family of functions, take the disjoint sectors (f,s,e), leave the f address unchanged and use the corresponding exchange in each sector. One complete permutation covers all the sectors. In particular, F=S^S is finite, so one fixed whole W=F×S×E admits every local table as a prepared internal mode. This strengthens the quantifiers: a separate complete dynamical rule need not be chosen for each local f. Different mode values are differences within the complete input, not competing results of an identical input. A written label alone would not establish the evaluation relation; the construction uses each actual function table. It does not establish that a particular physical medium contains those tables or those record forms.

For F=S^S, any relabeling g of S acts on the entire preparation by (f,s,e)↦(gfg⁻¹,g(s),g_E(e)). The complete U respects this action too: the relabeled table applied to g(s) gives g(f(s)), and the record becomes [g(s)]. Thus allowing the law form itself to transform does not break the construction. Holding one f fixed recovers the earlier symmetry statement. This checks the local rule and its record together, rather than placing a fixed rule outside the whole and renaming only its input.

**Any finite number of successive uses.** For N≥1, choose E={b} ⊔ (S×{1,…,N}). For each initial s, make one cycle

    (s,b) → (f(s),(s,1)) → … → (fᴺ(s),(s,N)) → (s,b).

All cycles are disjoint: the recorded source and internal position distinguish their members. Fix every unused member of S×E. This again gives a permutation of a finite fixed whole and commutes with every symmetry of f. The position belongs to E; counting applications does not supply a controller outside the whole. The first N outputs realize the successive local law. Given the retained source, position and law, one uniform reading also recovers the finite occurrence record (s,f(s),…,fᵗ(s)) at every prepared position t≤N. Those records extend properly even when successive local values agree. The cycle's last return retracts that record, so the construction does not satisfy indefinite strict retention. For N=0, the identity suffices.

**Unlimited autonomous reuse changes the answer.** On a finite whole, a permutation eventually returns each complete prepared source to itself. If one fixed U were to display fᵗ(s) for every t≥0 and every prepared s, some p>0 would therefore satisfy fᵖ(s)=s for each s. Every member of the finite S would be periodic, which forces f to be a bijection. Conversely, a bijective f acts directly as a permutation on S. Hence indefinite exact repetition from every local source in a finite reversible whole is possible exactly for bijective f. With a restricted preparation, the necessary condition concerns its prepared sources: each must lie on a cycle of f. An internal controller, clock or spare record belongs to the finite whole in this argument. Repeated external resets of such a controller would change the stated boundary. M51 supplies the recurrence principle; here it restricts an entire proposed local law. The quantifiers distinguish the results: a suitable whole for each finite horizon does not give one finite whole suitable for every horizon.

**Indefinite proper records still do not select f if their carrier is unrestricted.** Admit the finite histories h_t(s)=(s,f(s),…,fᵗ(s)) for every t≥0. Appending f of the last entry defines one total internal extension on this countably infinite history carrier. Removing the final entry recovers its source on the extension's range. Every record retains the initial source and every earlier position, and each extension is proper, including when f repeats a value. The local reading at the last entry obeys the same arbitrary f. The law can again occupy an unchanged internal address. This construction satisfies faithful retention and room for another finite record; it assumes an unbounded admitted record carrier. It is injective and invertible on its image, not a permutation of a finite whole. It therefore cannot be used to evade the finite-carrier theorem above. The record requirement constrains the complete histories without selecting a particular local f when this further resource remains free.

**An exact boundary without first selecting a memory.** Let L:W→S name the local form, with |S|=n and a distinguished 0∈S. For each s, let p_s be an admitted prepared complete configuration with L(p_s)=s. Suppose one permutation U keeps the local result at 0 for the first N≥1 applications after every p_s. The rest of each preparation need not be identical, and W need not be a product of independently supplied parts. Write W₀={w∈W:L(w)=0}. Then

    |W₀| ≥ 1 + (n−1)N.

To prove this, consider Uᵗ(p_s) for s≠0 and 1≤t≤N. All have local value 0. They are pairwise distinct. An equality at equal t would identify initial sources by invertibility. If t>u, an equality Uᵗ(p_s)=Uᵘ(p_r) would give Uᵗ⁻ᵘ(p_s)=p_r: the left has local value 0 and the right r≠0. Thus these forms use (n−1)N distinct zero-local states. The further state Uᴺ(p_0) is outside this family. Otherwise Uᴺ(p_0)=Uᵘ(p_r) would give Uᴺ⁻ᵘ(p_0)=p_r, again equating a zero local value with r≠0. This yields the additional state and the bound. Only the actual local distinctions, preparations and one-to-one complete transition were used. L is the specified local distinction in this inquiry, not an observer placed outside reality.

If a remainder reading R:W→E additionally gives a faithful complete description (L,R), it must distinguish all members of W₀. Its carrier E therefore needs at least the same number of forms. This derives the residual capacity obligation after establishing the necessary multiplicity of the whole. Changing which fields are called memory, law or control does not remove it. The bound also holds for an injective U that is not onto: cancelling equal finite iterates in the proof uses injectivity alone.

The same cancellation has a consequence for one prepared trajectory, without a family of alternative initial sources. Suppose L(p)≠0 but L(Uᵗ(p))=0 for every t≥1, with U injective. No two Uᵗ(p) can agree: cancelling a repeated position would return p after a positive number of steps, contradicting its different local value. An indefinitely settled local change therefore requires endlessly distinct complete configurations along that continuation. This is the nonrecurrence consequence of M51, not a derivation of a universal clock or of permanent settling from a finite observation. It also does not construct an internal reader of the position. It identifies a way that continued local stability can require changing distinctions beyond that local reading even without postulating an appended readable history.

The bound is attained. Take E={b} ⊔ ((S∖{0})×{1,…,N}). Fix p_0. For each nonzero s, cycle p_s through (0,(s,1)), …, (0,(s,N)) and back to p_s. Fix everything else. This is autonomous, finite and reversible; it realizes the reset for the requested horizon and respects every permutation of the nonzero source forms. The local value returns to s at the next application. The proof is uniform in n and N, not a count inferred from a finite program run.

If a single internal reading must additionally recover the initial source and distinguish every recorded occurrence position 0≤t≤N, the exact bound strengthens to

    |W₀| ≥ 1 + nN,           hence |E| ≥ 1 + nN for a faithful (L,R).

Now all Uᵗ(p_s), with s∈S and 1≤t≤N, must differ, even at unequal positions, because their recoverable records differ. They must also differ from p_0, whose occurrence record has length zero. All these nN+1 forms have local value 0. The finite-horizon construction above attains the bound with E={b} ⊔ (S×{1,…,N}). Thus the extra demand of recording each occurrence has a concrete consequence even when the local source already equals zero. This demand is stronger than reversibility or the continued equality of local values. The counting argument for these faithful records does not itself need invertibility; the record already separates the source-position pairs.

**Entropy does not replace the resource boundary.** Under a uniform preparation of the n initial local values and one ready remainder, the joint Shannon entropy is log₂n throughout either permutation construction. After a reset, the local entropy is zero and the remainder's entropy at each fixed position is log₂n. These values do not grow with N, while the required remainder carrier does. For n=1, a strict record of N occurrences still needs N+1 remainder forms although the ensemble entropy is zero at every position. The extra forms distinguish positions of a possible retained history; they are not extra uncertainty about the initial source. Uncertainty about the position itself would require another specified probability law. Equal entropy is also not stationarity of the prepared distribution, as M36 requires.

`ReadyLawState<S>` and `RecordedLawState<S>` express the two exchanged phases in bare Swift. The local successor is S.Right in the supplied law graph. The recorded phase carries both that actual successor and S; its next phase is the original preparation. Their generic double-step equalities establish the involution on this declared two-phase family. `ReadPreparedLawSource<O>` reconstructs the prepared fields from the output record alone, without an earlier-source parameter. The generic source-separation proof derives equal sources from equal complete output fields. The existing Never/Succ predecessor relation supplies a reset on its zero/one subfamily: local outputs agree, while the records differ and the second complete step restores the original one.
