# Seam Is Game (Cross-Encoding Agreement)

V=I proves a claim within its declared encoding and checking scope. A cross-encoding claim additionally needs relations connecting the descriptions to the question being preserved (V=I §5.4). Those relations can themselves be represented under an explicit contract. Being outside one pipeline's inputs does not put them outside reality.

The question is how to make that contact explicit without confusing compatibility with identity.

A **seam** is an addressed pair of declarations and a specified relation they must satisfy. A finite correspondence grammar with a terminating check gives a V=I game about it. The sub-game licences of V=I §5.13 and §5.15 do not establish those premises by naming a higher level.

Zero, one and several exhaust the cardinalities of a finite survivor set (SG1). An answer independent of unresolved revisions requires constancy over their admitted family (SG2). A declaration certificate applies to behavior through an established relation to the implementation (SG3). These are separate obligations, rather than consequences of one three-state vocabulary.

## §0. Derivation

The construction assumes addressed inputs, a finite correspondence family and a terminating acceptance relation. Counting gives the outcome classification. Question factorization determines which input distinctions are needed. The chosen compatibility relation and its connection to actual behavior require their own grounds.

## §1. The Premise Is a Game

Two statements draw the edge.

**V=I §5.4 (Encoding independence).** "Each encoding defines its own function space F and its own f*. … Cross-encoding agreement (that two encodings of the same task produce the same decoded test output) requires an additional premise: both encodings must be semantics-preserving (they represent the same target function on the same domain). This premise is external to the formal system."

**V=I §5.24 (Encoding-bounded scope).** "Two different encodings of the same task create two separate, non-communicating pipeline executions."

Two licences allow building on it.

**V=I §5.13 (Sub-game spawn).** "At any node in the pipeline, the agent can create a new V=I game to solve a sub-problem … The sub-game is a valid V=I instance."

**V=I §5.15 (Structural recurrence across levels).** "The system at level N and level N+1 share the same triple structure: (finite space, decidable check, unique solution). The spaces, checks, and solutions differ in content, and the structural pattern is identical."

So construct the game. Fix a boundary and the finite set of **addresses** the two sides may speak about. At an address, side A *states* a declaration and side B *claims* one; either may be silent.

- **Space.** F is a declared finite family of correspondences, such as maps between two finite sets of positions. Finite declaration syntax alone does not make a family of arbitrary interpreting programs finite.
- **Check.** A terminating test states whether a correspondence satisfies the declared relation. Pure structural matching supplies one such test. General semantic compatibility needs its own argument.
- **S.** The correspondences that pass.

These premises supply a finite checking game. A comparison of fixed declarations remains true of those declarations. Retaining its record is a separate update property, and reusing it for changed inputs needs a transport relation. V=I GC2 distinguishes these requirements.

## §2. Cardinality and Acquisition

**V=I §5, Corollary (Outcome classification).** "Every V=I game terminates in one of three states: |S| = 1 (identification, Theorem 2), |S| = 0 (no solution under this encoding), or |S| > 1 (encoding insufficient). *Proof.* |S| ∈ ℕ (§0 step 10). {0, 1, >1} partitions ℕ. QED"

Read at an address:

| \|S\| | the seam | what has happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **holds** | Exactly one correspondence passes in the stated family |
| 0 | **parted** | No correspondence passes in that family |
| >1 | **open** | More than one correspondence passes |

**SG1 (Completeness of the finite cardinality classification).** A specified finite survivor set has zero, one or several members. These classes are disjoint and exhaustive. This does not classify every operational state of acquiring inputs or performing the check.

*Proof.* The outcome classification, applied to the game of §1. QED

The cardinality result leaves three further questions.

**(a) Silence does not determine |S|.** An absent declaration can leave no candidate, one or several, depending on the grammar and other constraints. Both declarations can be present while a symmetry leaves several correspondences. Waiting concerns acquisition. If every survivor gives the same answer to the actual question, that question is already settled even when |S|>1. VI1a and VC15 separate selecting a representative from an invariant answer.

**(b) One joint record can retain two distinct sides.** A comparison record can represent the addressed pair and both declarations without duplicating its verdict. Symmetry of comparison does not identify the participants, their histories or which declaration is missing. A general compatibility relation can also be oriented.

**(c) An exception changes a contract or adds a record.** A waiver can record that a known mismatch is temporarily accepted. It does not prove equal semantics. Waiver status can accompany the cardinality class without creating a fourth cardinality. If the waiver changes the acceptance relation, its survivor set belongs to that changed question.

## §3. Revision Scope Is Part of the Question

**SG2 (Revision-independent answers).** Let K be a nonempty family of admitted revision pairs and q its declared seam question. One answer applies independently of the unresolved pair exactly when q is constant on K. Pinning the pair makes K a singleton and is sufficient. It is not necessary when a structural proof establishes the same answer throughout K.

*Proof.* A common answer must equal q at every pair in K, which is precisely constancy. Conversely, a constant q supplies that answer without identifying the pair. This is VC15's factorization criterion applied to revision uncertainty. If q varies, there is no one pair-independent q-answer. The set of possible answers and questions such as whether any or every pair passes remain well-defined. QED

LQ13's `ResidentContactExchange` gives a concrete equality-contact family: the local interior changes while the boundary and actual partner remain fixed. These are different local forms with the same contact answer. A revision range confined to such changes needs no selection of one interior to establish that answer. Other questions, including provenance or reproduction, can still require the exact revision.

A cached result about one pair does not automatically certify a range or another pair. V=I GC2 requires immutable keys or explicit context, and VC35 requires preservation of the certified scope. An implementation can require pinned revisions as its input contract. That policy does not imply that all range questions lack determinate answers.

**Defaults and later contacts.** Let S be a set of admitted forms, β:S→B their retained boundary, and P⊆S the forms kept after a proposed simplification. A later contact may impose any K⊆B. The simplification preserves the set of possible boundary answers under every such contact exactly when

    β(P)=β(S).

Indeed, the original joint boundary is β(S)∩K and the simplified one is β(P)∩K. Equal images make these equal for every K. Conversely, if b∈β(S) is omitted from β(P), the contact K={b} has an original completion and no simplified completion. This proof applies to arbitrary sets. It assumes singleton boundary contacts are among the permitted contexts for necessity. A restricted context grammar can permit a coarser description, as VC15 specifies.

This permits substantial simplification: several interiors with the same boundary need not all be retained as candidate witnesses for boundary-only questions. It forbids discarding a distinct boundary possibility while claiming compatibility with every future boundary contact. If β is the identity, every candidate must remain available for this unrestricted reuse claim. Preserving a relation can instead avoid listing its members at all. Enlarging the contact to read a previously hidden field changes the scope of the simplification.

Matter M71a–d makes this preservation constructive for finite free functional contact diagrams. With disjoint contact symbols, the exact boundary is represented by conditional equality consequences and obstructions. Reapplying these local constructions after new boundary identities gives a complete compatibility test without enumerating partitions. If a contact itself is shared, its hidden path can carry an obstruction that an identity-only boundary misses. Eliminating private support can also require a wider joint boundary condition: simple unary contacts can carry a dependency on arbitrarily many identities together. Keeping an intermediate structure or a recoverable tuple permits a shorter expression while retaining that dependency. These are scopes of the preserved relation, not statuses attached to its summaries.

For U2c's two widths, let S={(a,b): a,b≥0, a+b=10}. Choosing the smallest a keeps only (0,10). Joining the later condition a≥4 to that chosen result gives no solution, although joining it to S admits every (a,10−a) with 4≤a≤10. Reapplying the same minimization after the join gives (4,6). Selection before composition and selection after composition thus differ even with a unique optimizer at each feasible stage. The objective is an explicit additional rule. Keeping only its earlier winner is not an exact boundary description.

The existing four-cycle schedule supplies a static instance. Without a pin, the schedules beginning on MachineA and MachineB both satisfy all four conflict edges. A new contact with an independently pinned MachineA neighbor admits the MachineB-first schedule. A default that had kept only the MachineA-first schedule cannot satisfy that same new edge. `LaterContactAdmitsExchangedSchedule` in `Checks/Coverage.swift` checks the successful extension through the actual conflict constructor. In the negative control `RejectedEarlyDefaultContact`, the compiler rejects extension of the prematurely selected MachineA-first schedule. No new scheduling algorithm or nominal claim of global consistency is introduced.

Existence of a completion is also different from a response valid for every possible actual source. If hidden conditions permit {a,b} and {b,c}, their common input can support b. A later contact permitting only {a,c} leaves each condition individually feasible but supplies no common response. Matter M25a proves exactly when all such restrictions avoid this gap: one of the hidden conditions must already have the least allowed-response set. Requiring the same after every source refinement forces the distinct response sets to be well-ordered by inclusion, hence nested for a finite family. The boundary-image criterion above preserves possible completions. It does not establish this different guarantee about uniform action.

SG4's internal-kernel example shows why this distinction matters beyond layout. When all compatible internal solutions give the same retained output, a chosen minimum-norm internal solution can represent that boundary answer. It does not establish that the actual interior has minimum norm. A further probe of the omitted kernel is a new contact for which the previous boundary may be incomplete. The freedom belongs to the description and question being retained, not to an exterior selector of reality.

## §4. What a Seam Certifies

**SG3 (Scope).** A successful seam check certifies the stated relation between the supplied declarations. By itself it does not establish that an actual implementation realizes those declarations.

*Proof.* If the admitted model permits two implementations with the same declarations and different relevant behaviors, a declaration-only check receives identical inputs in those two cases. Its result cannot distinguish them. Excluding that alternative requires a relation binding declaration and implementation. A source-bound construction or a proof under explicit semantics can supply such a relation for its domain. QED

If the contract asks for equal declarations, structural equality is appropriate. Arbitrary compatibility cannot always be reduced to equality of two separate codes. LQ13 proves the exact condition: three admitted corners of a rectangle must force its fourth. The contact {00,01,10} fails that condition. Shared equality would admit the forbidden 11. Its exact interface instead retains a relation between the two boundary spaces. Neither a higher-level game nor a common PASS label supplies that missing structure.

Choosing an input-output direction imposes another condition. A boundary relation is the graph of a map on its admitted inputs exactly when each has one compatible output. LiR12 supplies a model in which a resonant side mode prevents the boundary from being an invertible left-to-right transfer, while its scattering relation remains finite. An interface must preserve the relation before requiring a functional description in a chosen direction. This is a constraint on the representation of the physical contact, not a change in the given system caused by its description.

## §4a. Elimination, Sewing and Internal Response

**SG4 (Exact elimination retains the constraints paired with a singular response).** Let X and Z be finite-dimensional complex inner-product spaces. Boundary coordinates x,y∈X and internal coordinates z∈Z obey

    Az=Bx,       y=B†z+Dx,       A=A†, D=D†.

All coordinates belong to the described joint system. Calling x an input does not make it an independently available preparation or an exterior cause. Define K=ker A, Q=P_K B, E=ker Q, and F=D+B†A⁺B, where A⁺ is zero on K and the inverse of A on K⊥. Eliminating z exactly gives

    R = {(x,Fx+η) : x∈E, η∈E⊥}.

To prove this, Az=Bx is solvable precisely when Bx is orthogonal to K, or Qx=0. Every solution is z=A⁺Bx+k for k∈K. Its possible boundary outputs are Fx+B†K. Since B†K=im Q†=(ker Q)⊥, these are exactly the displayed pairs. No inverse of A on its kernel was used.

The directions orthogonal to the admitted input domain and the undetermined output directions are thus the same complement E⊥. If r=rank Q, the input domain has dimension dim X−r and each output fiber has dimension r. The whole relation has dimension dim X. A hidden internal kernel K∩ker B† contributes neither restriction nor output ambiguity. A boundary function exists on all X exactly when Q=0, even if A itself is singular. SiE12 gives a graph certificate that excludes this hidden kernel without finding the spectrum.

Self-adjointness also makes F self-adjoint. For any two pairs (x,y),(x′,y′)∈R,

    x†y′−y†x′ = 0.

The F terms cancel, while each η is orthogonal to every admitted x. Together with the dimension result, this makes R a maximal isotropic relation for the displayed nondegenerate skew-Hermitian pairing. Over real spaces the corresponding bilinear statement is Lagrangian. [Baez and Fong, *A Compositional Framework for Passive Linear Networks*, §5.3–§5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1504.05625) give an established use of Lagrangian boundary relations for circuits. The finite elimination proof here states its own Hermitian premises. It does not identify every physical boundary with an electrical circuit.

For one boundary coordinate, LiR13 gives the two singular cases explicitly. If the resonant mode couples to the boundary, x is forced to zero and the conjugate feedback y is free for the rest of the system to determine. If it does not couple, x remains admissible and y is uniquely determined despite internal ambiguity. The infinite coefficient in a failed function description is replaced by an exact relation, not by an infinite field.

The adjoint return B† is material to the result. Replacing it by an unrelated C in y=Cz+Dx changes the output ambiguity to C K, which need not equal E⊥. For scalar spaces, A=0,B=1,C=0,D=0 permits only (x,y)=(0,0). Taking A=0,B=0,C=1,D=0 instead permits every pair. Neither has the dimension or maximal-isotropy conclusion above. Thus the pairing is an actual retained law, not a consequence of giving a contact two names. Global closure has not established that law. The concrete open question is which internal physical comparisons establish the adjoint pairing for a proposed application.

**The same relation always has a unitary direction in normalized boundary coordinates.** For SG4's R, define a=(x+iy)/√2 and b=(x−iy)/√2. The pairing identity implies ‖a‖=‖b‖. If a=0, then b=0, hence x=y=0. The projection R→a is injective. Since dim R=dim X, it is also onto, so exactly one b exists for every a. The resulting map b=Sa is linear and preserves the norm. In finite dimension it is unitary. Thus a constraint in the x-to-y direction need not prevent a total, reversible relation between incoming and outgoing coordinates.

More explicitly, let G=P_E F|_E. On E the map is (I−iG)(I+iG)⁻¹, and on E⊥ it is −I. No eigenvalue of the Hermitian G makes I+iG singular. This is the finite Cayley correspondence, with the sign convention fixed by the displayed a,b. The related boundary parametrization in [Facchi, Garnero and Ligabò, Theorem 3 and §6](https://arxiv.org/html/1703.04091) addresses differential operators with additional domain conditions. Here x and y must first have a specified common normalization. Physical wave amplitudes require a contact establishing that normalization and its flux interpretation. Self-adjointness alone is also not a claim of time-reversal symmetry.

**Testing the norm law requires joint preparations.** Within a supplied complex-linear map T between two m-dimensional calibrated spaces, m² exact norm comparisons suffice to establish T†T=I. Use each orthonormal input e_j, and e_j+e_k and e_j+i e_k for every j<k, requiring output squared norms 1, 2 and 2, respectively. If v_j=Te_j, the first readings give ‖v_j‖²=1. The other two give Re(v_j†v_k)=0 and Im(v_j†v_k)=0 by expanding their squared norms. Thus the columns are orthonormal and T is unitary. Testing the separate basis inputs alone misses T=[[1,1],[0,0]], which preserves their individual norms but merges them. These comparisons require the same map, linear carrier and coherent relative preparations. Finite agreement does not by itself establish linearity, absence of unrecorded preparation dependence or exact equalities from measurements with error. This specifies how a proposed contact law can be tested without deriving it from global closure.

**Finite unitary contacts can be sewn even at a singular internal loop.** Partition a finite unitary U into retained ports e and ports i to be sewn, with

    b_e=U_ee a_e+U_ei a_i,       b_i=U_ie a_e+U_ii a_i.

Sew by a_i=b_i. Any fixed unitary matching of the sewn ports can be absorbed into U first. For every a_e, the equation (I−U_ii)a_i=U_ie a_e is solvable, even if I−U_ii is singular. Indeed, if v∈ker(I−U_ii†), unitarity gives ‖U_ie†v‖²+‖U_ii†v‖²=‖v‖², hence U_ie†v=0. The right-hand side is orthogonal to the left kernel, which is exactly the finite-dimensional solvability condition.

If k∈ker(I−U_ii), the other unitary block identity gives ‖U_ei k‖²+‖U_ii k‖²=‖k‖², hence U_ei k=0. Therefore the remaining output b_e is independent of the internal solution. Finally, cancelling the equal internal norms in ‖b_e‖²+‖b_i‖²=‖a_e‖²+‖a_i‖² proves that the resulting map a_e↦b_e is unitary. Internal ambiguity can remain, but it cannot be driven or read through the retained ports in this contract. The whole system has not lost the internal variable when a boundary description omits it.

This supplies a composition proof without assuming an inverse at the seam. It relies on the full pairing, finite dimension and unitary sewing. For contrast, b_e=a_e and b_i=a_e+a_i with the same sewing require a_e=0. A generic linear loop need not accept every retained input.

Finite dimension matters too. On ℓ²(ℤ), the bilateral shift b_n=a_{n−1} is unitary. Retain only port 0 and impose a_n=b_n for n≠0. The positive tail is then a_n=a₀ for every n≥1, so square summability forces a₀=0. The negative tail also vanishes. This infinite sewing has no nonzero admitted retained input in the stated carrier. Norm preservation alone does not extend the finite existence proof to every infinite system or every proposed steady preparation.

**Existence and unitarity do not make sewing continuous.** A two-coordinate example uses only rational relations. For real ε set

    c=(1−ε²)/(1+ε²),       s=2ε/(1+ε²),
    U_ε = [[c,s],[−s,c]].

The identity (1−ε²)²+4ε²=(1+ε²)² proves unitarity. Sew the second coordinate as above. For ε≠0, (1−c)a_i=−s a_e gives a_i=−a_e/ε, and then b_e=c a_e+s a_i=−a_e. At ε=0, U₀=I, the internal field is free and b_e=a_e. Although U_ε→U₀, the sewn maps differ by operator norm 2 for every nonzero ε. The internal amplitude required by the nonzero branch diverges. A finite, well-defined relation at each parameter does not justify interchanging the parameter limit with elimination.

The sign difference needs its reading. The outgoing intensity alone agrees in both cases. Retain a coherent bypass carrying a_e and compare it with the sewn output: the sum is zero for ε≠0 and 2a_e at ε=0. `SewnQuarterTurn` in `Checks/Seeing.swift` checks the ε=1 instance from the existing exchange and sign reversal, including equality of the sewn fields and cancellation against the actual bypass. In the negative control `RejectedSewnLoopIgnoresCoupling`, the compiler rejects that cancellation when the coupling is removed. The real parameter family and its limit are proved here, not by that finite witness.

There is a positive replacement with an explicit internal bound. Compare finite unitaries U,V on the same normalized ports and sewing. Choose linear internal solution maps Z_U,Z_V, so J_U a=(a,Z_U a) and UJ_U a=(S_U a,Z_U a), and likewise for V. Finite solvability supplies such maps, including at singular points by choosing the solution orthogonal to the internal kernel. Cancelling the shared internal cross terms gives

    I−S_U†S_V = J_U†(I−U†V)J_V,
    ‖S_U−S_V‖ ≤ ‖U−V‖ √(1+‖Z_U‖²) √(1+‖Z_V‖²).

Thus a uniform bound on the internal response supplies a uniform continuity bound for sewing. In the rational example, Z₀=0 and Z_ε=−1/ε. Since ‖U_ε−I‖=2|ε|/√(1+ε²), the bound equals 2 and is attained. The missing control is exposed by the retained field, without changing the physical system or postulating lost information. LiR13's weak side contact gives a frequency-dependent instance and distinguishes this stationary limit from a fixed finite packet.

The bound can be read in the other direction. If Z_V=0 for the reference configuration, δ=‖U−V‖>0 and d=‖S_U−S_V‖, then

    ‖Z_U‖ ≥ √max(0,(d/δ)²−1).

Within the stated contract, a large boundary change caused by a small component change requires a large internal response for some incident preparation. The ports, norm and phase references must agree in both comparisons. This establishes a necessary amplitude ratio, not a count of hidden objects or an energy in unspecified units.

**A phase comparison can read that internal response.** Keep a finite unitary contact U fixed and insert the same phase e^{iφ} on its sewn outputs. Thus U(φ)=diag(I_e,e^{iφ}I_i)U, with identity sewing as before. At a parameter where I−e^{iφ}U_ii is invertible, let Z(φ) give the internal solution and S(φ) the retained scattering map. Differentiate the preceding cross-term identity with respect to its second parameter. Since U(φ)J(φ)=(S(φ),Z(φ)), the result is

    −i S(φ)† ∂_φ S(φ) = Z(φ)† Z(φ).

The right side is positive semidefinite. A phase change on the specified internal paths therefore measures the squared internal response through a boundary comparison. No symmetry under transposition or universal clock is used. Different phases on separate sewn coordinates similarly give the corresponding coordinate contributions. The phase controls and coherent reference must belong to the actual comparison. Renaming input or output phases while dropping the reference would change the derivative being measured.

For one retained port S=e^{iχ}, this reads ∂χ/∂φ=‖Z‖². A physical delay interpretation additionally needs a calibrated relation φ(ω) and control of any frequency dependence in the component U. With U fixed, the contribution to ∂χ/∂ω is φ′(ω)‖Z‖². The narrowband connection between scattering phase and group delay is developed in [Patel and Michielssen, §II-A–§II-B](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.06985), using the opposite time-harmonic sign convention. Their broader electromagnetic formulas require field integrals and, in some geometries, correction terms. The finite identity here establishes neither all of those formulas nor the origin of time from one phase coordinate.

This also tests a precise statement in the literature. [Kostrykin and Schrader, arXiv:math-ph/0008022v1, Eq. (3.4) and Theorem 3.9, pp. 8 and 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0008022v1), extend the product to singular pairs and state continuity in each matrix argument. Set n₁=n₂=2, p=1, V=1, keep U⁽¹⁾=I₂ and vary U⁽²⁾=U_ε above. For ε≠0 their formula gives I₂∗U_ε=diag(1,−1), since the second diagonal is c−s²/(1−c)=−1 and the other off-diagonal blocks vanish. At ε=0 their singular extension gives I₂∗I₂=I₂. The output distance is 2 while the varying argument approaches I₂. This contradicts the unrestricted continuity statement in that version. The internal-response bound above supplies a valid conditional replacement. This comparison concerns the cited preprint statement, not an audit of the journal version or of all its graph-scattering results.

**SG5 (A complete relative-phase cycle counts accessible internal directions).** Keep the finite unitary U and its port partition fixed. Apply the same phase z=e^{iφ} to every sewn output, and retain the coherent boundary map S(φ) over a complete cycle. Write D=U_ii and C=U_ie for this paragraph. Let H_dark be the span of D's eigenvectors with eigenvalues of modulus one, and H_vis its orthogonal complement. Then

    (1/2π) ∫₀²π tr(−i S† ∂_φ S) dφ = dim H_vis.

The left side is the winding number of det S with the specified phase orientation and reference. It is an integer fixed by the complete contact cycle, although a boundary reading at one phase cannot determine it. For several retained ports, obtaining S requires the complex responses to a spanning family of incident preparations in the same calibrated bases. One measured intensity curve does not supply that matrix.

*Proof.* D is a contraction. If Dk=λk with |λ|=1, equality of norms forces D†k=conj(λ)k. The unitary block identities then give U_ei k=0 and C†k=0. Thus H_dark reduces D and is neither driven nor read by the retained ports. On H_vis every eigenvalue has modulus less than one. In finite dimension the powers of this restriction decay, including any finite Jordan factors. Choose zero amplitude in the dark part, without claiming that every internal preparation has that amplitude. On the visible part the solution is smooth for every φ and has the uniformly convergent expansion

    Z(φ)=z(I−zD)⁻¹C = Σₙ≥₀ zⁿ⁺¹ DⁿC.

Here the inverse is only on H_vis. Orthogonality of the phase powers and CC†=I−DD† give

    (1/2π) ∫₀²π ZZ† dφ
      = Σₙ≥₀ DⁿCC†(D†)ⁿ
      = limₙ→∞ [I−Dⁿ⁺¹(D†)ⁿ⁺¹]
      = P_vis.

The sum telescopes on the full internal space, vanishing on H_dark. Taking traces and using SG4's phase identity proves the formula. It also proves that the span of the driven directions DⁿC is exactly H_vis. The analogous identity using U_ei†U_ei=I−D†D proves that these are also the directions accessible to a retained reading. QED

This is a contact-specific dimension, not the number of physical nodes or all distinctions in the whole. Adding a decoupled unitary internal sector changes neither S(φ) nor the count. Conversely, any realization of the same full phase-dependent boundary relation in this grammar must have at least this many internal amplitude directions. The related minimal-realization and lossless determinant results are established in [Peeters, Olivi and Hanzon, §2.1 and §3.1](https://www-sop.inria.fr/members/Martine.Olivi/Papiers/Sysid09.pdf). Their transfer variable is reciprocal to the disk variable used here. The proof above obtains the count from the retained phase comparison without importing their discrete-time coordinate as a universal physical clock.

The preserved reference is essential. A one-port identity contact with no sewn coordinates has S=1 and count zero. Replacing only its displayed output coordinate by b′=e^{iφ}b produces the apparent function S′=e^{iφ} and apparent count one. It violates the fixed boundary-reference premise. A faithful coordinate change also carries the reference and comparison relation, which still read the identity contact. Physically inserting that phase on an additional path instead changes the apparatus. Constant unitary changes of boundary bases multiply det S by a constant phase and cannot change its winding.

For SG4's one-loop contact with real |c|<1 and s²=1−c²,

    S(φ)=(c−e^{iφ})/(1−c e^{iφ}),
    ‖Z(φ)‖²=(1−c²)/|1−c e^{iφ}|².

Its phase-cycle count is one for every nonzero coupling. As c→1 the response concentrates near φ=0, while at c=1 the loop is dark and the count is zero. Thus a finite set of samples with nonzero measurement tolerance cannot certify this count without further control of unresolved phase variation. A fixed phase offset can place the increasingly narrow feature between those samples, making their boundary values arbitrarily close to the constant map. The full boundary cycle and a finite record of that cycle are different inputs to VI. A bound on internal response supplies control of the derivative through SG4 and is therefore directly relevant to closing that gap.

**A finite phase record can certify the count with a bound between samples.** Suppose ‖Z(φ)‖≤B throughout the cycle and there are m retained ports. The derivative of an unwrapped argument χ of det S satisfies 0≤χ′=tr Z†Z≤mB². Choose cyclic sample phases with every gap h satisfying mB²h<π. The true phase increment on each gap is then less than π, so it equals the principal argument of the ratio of the two determinant readings. Summing those principal arguments and dividing by 2π gives SG5's exact integer.

The certificate also tolerates phase errors. If each sampled determinant phase has circular error at most η, the same conclusion follows under mB²h+2η<π on every gap, using the same recorded first sample to close the cycle. Choose lifted errors e_j with |e_j|≤η. Each measured principal increment is the true increment plus e_{j+1}−e_j because their sum lies strictly between −π and π. Around the cycle the errors cancel, preserving the integer. The uniform bound B must be established for the whole interval by the apparatus contract, a proof or independent evidence. Estimating it only at those samples would reuse the very completeness assumption being tested. This is a finite certificate once that additional relation is supplied, not a proof that every experimental scan has already supplied it.

A finite model can supply such a bound constructively. If its visible restriction obeys ‖Dᴺ‖≤ρ<1 for some positive integer N, factor the resolvent as (I+zD+⋯+zᴺ⁻¹Dᴺ⁻¹)(I−zᴺDᴺ)⁻¹. Since ‖D‖≤1 and ‖C‖≤1, this gives B=N/(1−ρ) uniformly on the phase circle. The bound can be loose but is sufficient. Existence of some such N follows from finite-dimensional decay on H_vis. Identifying that restriction and establishing the inequality are retained premises of this certificate.

## §4b. Retaining Actual Occurrences

**Actual overlap is stricter than equal content.** Let A and B be two addressed collections of represented occurrences, with their internal distinctions retained. A specified R⊆A×B declares exactly which entries refer to the same occurrence. Ask for a joint carrier C with injective maps i:A→C and j:B→C such that

    i(a)=j(b)  iff  (a,b)∈R.

Such a carrier exists exactly when R is a partial bijection: no a is related to two distinct b, and no b to two distinct a. For necessity, i(a)=j(b)=j(b′) forces b=b′ by injectivity of j, and the other direction is symmetric. For sufficiency, form the disjoint union of the two addressed collections and identify just the pairs in R. Partial bijectivity makes each resulting class either one entry or one entry from each side. The two maps remain injective and their overlap is exactly R. This constructs the least set of represented occurrences carrying those identifications. Any two maps from A and B agreeing on R induce a unique map out of this quotient, by their values on each class.

For finite collections its size is |A|+|B|−|R|. Two singleton descriptions with equal payload can therefore refer to one occurrence when their entries are identified, or to two when the declared overlap is empty. The payload readings coincide in both cases. The number and identity of the represented occurrences do not. These collections describe the referents of the records, not every form in the whole, and the theorem does not establish the actual overlap from the existence of a quotient. Internal payload relations must also agree at identified entries and any further joint constraints must be satisfied.

Injectivity is the stated requirement to preserve the occurrence distinctions within each input. If an input instead lists several descriptions already known to refer to one occurrence, those internal identifications must be included first. Distinct record positions alone do not prove distinct referents. The theorem applies to the distinctions whose faithful preservation is actually claimed.

Changing the addresses by bijections α and β transports R to {(α(a),β(b)):(a,b)∈R} and gives the same construction up to a corresponding bijection of its classes. No absolute names or exterior clock are required. What matters is the relation among the retained addresses. If several overlap matchings remain possible, identifying every possible match at once changes alternatives into simultaneous equalities and can merge distinct entries within one side. SG1 classifies the number of surviving correspondences, while VI1 describes the alignment ambiguity. Neither makes every alternative an actual identification. SharedDecoder's source-recovery consequence supplies the related prohibition on merging inputs whose distinctions a fixed decoder preserves.

**Locally faithful overlaps can still fail jointly.** Suppose a graph of descriptions has total bijections f_ij:A_i→A_j along its edges, with the reverse edges their inverses. Interpret each map as identifying the same occurrences, and require injective maps g_i into one carrier with g_j∘f_ij=g_i. Such a joint carrier exists exactly when every closed-path composite on each A_i is the identity. Necessity follows by composing the equalities around the path: g_i∘H=g_i, so injectivity gives H=id. Conversely, quotient the disjoint union by the edge identifications. If two entries in one A_i become equal, a closed path takes one to the other. The identity condition makes them equal already, so each g_i is injective. The quotient supplies the joint carrier without selecting a preferred description.

Three two-entry descriptions give a finite refusal: use identity matchings on the first two edges and an exchange on the closing edge. Each edge is a bijection. Their joint identifications equate the two distinct entries of the starting description, so no faithful joint carrier exists. Increasing that carrier or adjoining unrelated hidden fields cannot repair the contradiction while preserving injectivity and all three identifications. This is S25's loop criterion applied to the explicit same-occurrence contract. The existing swap-return witnesses derive precisely that collapse. A physical transport may instead change an occurrence or its state, so a nonidentity transport loop is not forbidden by this theorem. The equality contract, rather than the word cycle, determines the conclusion.

For partial descriptions, distinguish a missing match from an asserted nonidentity. Form the equivalence relation generated by the supplied positive matches on the disjoint union of all inputs. A faithful joint carrier respecting both positive matches and specified nonidentities exists exactly when no generated class contains two distinct entries of one input or any pair explicitly required to remain distinct. Necessity follows by propagating equality along a finite chain of matches. For sufficiency, the quotient by those generated classes supplies the carrier and preserves every specified distinction. This is the same gluing construction with its negative constraints retained. Unknown pairs need not be declared either equal or distinct. Identity between different quotient classes remains open unless a supplied distinction forbids it.

Thus the loop test for injectivity alone does not check an exact partial-overlap contract. Take three singleton descriptions a,b,c, assert a=b and b=c, and require a and c to be distinct. Every input remains individually injective in the positive quotient, and each positive closed path returns its only entry to itself. The asserted distinction nevertheless fails because the two matches force a=c. If the a,c match was only unknown, that quotient would instead be admissible. When R is declared to be the entire overlap of two inputs, every pair outside R is such a nonidentity requirement. Any obstruction to this pure identity contract has a finite match chain ending in a required distinction, even if the family of descriptions is infinite. Additional payload or joint-admission relations require their own checks.

LQ15's proposed regions of joint presence therefore require a retained meaning for their shared roles. Repeated content is not enough to establish an actual overlap, and pairwise matches need not supply a joint identification. Conversely, union of facts can deliberately forget duplicate reports when its question concerns only which propositions are established. The content union and a faithful assembly of occurrences preserve different questions. M55's retention relation distinguishes the latter from repetition of the same abstract history pattern.

**Use once is a condition on referents, when the operation requires it.** Let J be addressed entries, e:J→O their established occurrence correspondence, and c:U→J the entry selected at each requested use. Requiring different source occurrences means exactly that e∘c is injective. This is equivalent to injectivity of c together with injectivity of e on c(U). Removing each entry at most once verifies the first condition. Two entries can still point to one occurrence. Two entries with equal contents can instead point to different occurrences. The reading of content does not settle either case.

The pure identity fragment above gives an exact test even when the correspondence is only partially known. Let ≈ be the equivalence generated by established same-occurrence matches, and let D retain established nonidentities between those classes. Assume no D-pair has already collapsed. A completion may further identify classes provided no D-pair is identified. For the requested distinct-source uses U:

- If different uses u,v select ≈-equal entries c(u),c(v), every completion violates the requirement. This includes using the same entry twice.
- If the classes selected by different uses are different and every such pair is separated by D, every completion satisfies it.
- Otherwise, provided no two uses select already equal classes, the data admit both answers. Keeping all ≈-classes separate realizes distinct sources for U. Identifying a selected pair with no D-relation realizes a collision while preserving every supplied match and nonidentity.

For the last construction, only those two classes are merged, so a new violation of D could only be a D-pair between them, which was excluded. The test also applies to infinite entry sets. Additional joint relations impose additional obligations, so the three cases are exact for the stated partial identity contract. None interprets unknown correspondence as a recorded inequality. A single established alias between two requested distinct sources is already a finite obstruction, preserved by enlargement of the record. The universal positive result instead needs the required pairwise separation or a structural argument that derives it.

`CandidateRemoval` supplies another interpretation: a leaf's position in the declared support is the source occurrence being consumed. Actual finite selection constructors retain fork addresses and replace the selected leaf with Never. Sequential consumers take the preceding remainder. Under this positional contract one leaf cannot supply two removals, while two equal-payload leaves remain separately available. `CheckIceVertex` intentionally reuses a shared edge reading at its two ends and removes each vertex's pending obligation. `ConsumeIceEdge` separately removes the edge occurrence and its two allocated port occurrences. Their differing reuse rules belong to their declared questions. A remaining requirement is fidelity of any mapping from these positions to occurrences in another description or in an actual physical source. Open `CandidateRemoval` conformances also do not acquire the finite selection interpretation from their protocol name.

The removal relation also permits a precise reordering. Let L and R be removal witnesses within separate branches. Removing L and then R uses the sources Fork(L.Left,R.Left) and Fork(L.Remainder,R.Left). The reverse order uses Fork(L.Left,R.Left) and Fork(L.Left,R.Remainder). Both finish at Fork(L.Remainder,R.Remainder). `CandidateSiblingRemovalsCommute` derives the common original support, both intermediate source matches and the common final remainder from the actual selection constructors in one generic Null witness. No equality of L's and R's payloads is required. Applying this at the first differing branch and retaining the common outer context gives commutation for distinct leaf addresses in the finite grammar. Reusing the same address lacks that branch separation. These are equal final obligations, not equal histories of performing them or a proof that port inventories accumulated in different orders are identical.

Repeated reference to an established fact adds no second independent source by itself. Creating a further record of the fact is another occurrence whose relation to the original must be retained. Reading one source for several compatible questions does not automatically consume it, and copying its description does not establish that a physical operation can copy every state it describes. The distinction concerns the actual input, output and occurrence correspondence, without imposing one universal rule of duplication on the logical medium.

## §5. Order-Invariance as a Differential Test

V=I §5.25 states: "With GC2 updates, complete traversals produce the same union of facts, by associativity, commutativity and idempotence of union". V=I CS1 now distinguishes raw entries from their rule projection: "Two complete raw libraries can differ in their feature records. Their rule projections both equal C." This comparison requires the same covered classes and immutable context. Byte-identical emitted diagnostics additionally require canonical rendering, which the following differential test checks.

The recorded implementation test used two agents, one domain, identical content and constituent files declared in opposite orders, comparing judgments and emitted bytes. The first run reported two failures:

1. Declarations from one presented encoding were copied into every other encoding, contrary to the separation of pipeline executions in V=I §5.24.
2. The declarations were copied in declaration order. Two agents with identical content therefore received different line numbers for the same refusal. Declaration order thus entered the address, and the address is part of the refusal.

One correction removed both failures. A foreign declaration enters an encoding only at the position of the encoding's own declaration, which it replaces. A name the encoding never declared is not added to it. The re-run is byte-identical across opposite orders while the overrides still take effect.

This differential test exposed two order dependencies. One process can also compare several orders, and a structural proof can quantify over them. `IndependentReadingOrders` derives equality for actual disjoint applications, while M67 gives conditions for transporting adjacent exchanges through a larger serialization. Byte-identical diagnostics additionally need a rendering contract. The number of agents is not a proof premise.

## §6. What Is Claimed

A seam makes a relation and its input scope explicit. A finite correspondence game gives SG1's cardinality classification. Constancy over admitted revision pairs gives SG2's exact revision-independent criterion. A behavior claim additionally needs SG3's binding to the implementation. A unique correspondence is necessary only when the question requires that correspondence, rather than an answer shared by several of them.

SG4 derives a complete finite boundary relation under its Hermitian premises, proves unitary sewing, and identifies the internal response needed to control its sensitivity. SG5 obtains an accessible internal dimension from a complete phase cycle under the finite unitary contract. Neither result identifies every hidden form from one contact.

These conditions permit a smaller contract when fewer distinctions suffice, and reject one that erases a forbidden combination. Structuring the question exposes those obligations. It does not discharge them by changing the level at which they are written.
