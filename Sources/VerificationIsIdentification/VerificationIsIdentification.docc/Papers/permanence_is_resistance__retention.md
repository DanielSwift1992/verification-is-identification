## Detail: §4g. Which future must carry a real distinction?

The Cut's jurisdiction asks whether a distinction can be registered within reality. To ask about its later recovery, first specify the internal relation giving those readings their earlier and later roles. One whole does not by itself supply a universal current configuration, a division into all past and all future, or a clock for moving that division. Registration at an event, retention in the relations among events, recovery through a specified cut, and access through an actual internal instrument are four claims about the same reality. A trace can distinguish occurrence from non-occurrence while failing to distinguish two causes of that occurrence. A preservation claim must name which alternatives its readings continue to separate.

### M50

**M50 (a historical distinction reaches a later reading exactly through a question-preserving factorization).** Fix a declared family H of compatible complete-history representations, a question Q about an earlier occurrence, and a reading E of a specified later cut. A supplied decoder D recovers that question precisely when Q = D ∘ E on H. If two members have the same E-image and positively different Q-answers, no single-valued decoder on that image can recover Q on both. Here H compares descriptions compatible with stated relations. It does not posit several actual realities outside the whole.

The decoder must be one construction on the available image. Selecting a different decoder for each unobserved history can insert the missing answer through that selection. Internal branching based on E is allowed, because its selecting information is available. `SharedDecoder` and `AnswersThroughSharedDecoder` express the two-case consequence: two valid factorizations through the same decoder derive the same earlier answer. The routing audit separately checks that equal decoder inputs do not identify two different supplied decoder constructions.

**Derivation.** The positive certificate is the displayed factorization, with its decoder constructed from the stated premises. On a common E-image, one fixed D has one answer, so it cannot equal two distinguished Q-answers. The argument concerns this question and these readings. It does not assume a global Boolean valuation of every question. Requiring Q to recover the entire earlier representation strengthens this to input preservation. Requiring an internal instrument to acquire E adds the availability conditions of VC40–VC42. Requiring such access after every admitted continuation adds the transport conditions of VC18 and VC38. Each stronger statement has an explicit additional obligation.

The existing `RetainedWhole<X,Y>` gives a generic separation. Its full record is (X,Y). The first projection recovers the earlier X, while the second projection reads the current Y. Holding Y fixed makes the current reading identical for different X, although the full records remain distinguished by their first projection. `Checks/ClosedInformation.swift` checks both generic projections and a positive zero/successor separator. Interpreting (X,Y) as a history does not put X in an external archive: it keeps X in that representation of the whole. It also does not establish that a current internal device can read the historical coordinate. That would require the further relation just identified.

The realized application and its output also have different content. An application relates its actual source to its answer. `RecordReading<R>` retains that source beside the addressed answer fact. Two such application records can therefore remain distinguished when their output facts coincide. If the complete reality includes those actual event relations, an equal later output does not erase their difference from the whole. The additional question is which later internal reading reaches it. Naming an operation without its actual source would not supply this historical distinction.

This resolves the apparent conflict between historical retention and loss of current information. For a separate fair binary preparation of X and fixed Y, H(X)=1 bit and H(X|Y)=1 bit, while H(X|(X,Y))=0. The complete representation retains the distinction. The current cut does not answer it. These conditional entropies quantify a specified reading and preparation. They neither count newly occurring events nor decide whether every physical distinction has a recoverable future witness. If the random object is instead the entire history with one fixed distribution, its entropy has no changing cut parameter. Calling that unchanged quantity conservation does not establish an invariant of the evolving current-state readings. A physical conservation claim must specify which internally related descriptions it compares.

The same distinction changes the question about time. Two occurrences can share a configuration reading while occupying different positions in the whole's event relations. Recurrence of that reading does not identify those occurrences. Conversely, calling them two positions requires an actual distinguishing relation: an external counter cannot silently supply it. The record commitment provides such historical positions in its model. Whether every later physical state must recover their order is another application of M50, not a consequence of naming the positions.

The remaining foundational question concerns the actual access relation: which internal readings must retain a registered distinction, and what makes them later readings of that occurrence? The existing premises establish registration within the whole and separately commit to the full record. A temporal application must establish its cuts and their ordering before asking for recovery at every one of them. Proving such recovery would constrain permissible dynamics, memory and the entropy of those readings. Neither a universal present nor a cut containing the desired answer can be supplied by its name.

### M72

**M72 (established recovery forces a witness at every complete intervening cut).** Fix an actual joining relation J⊆P×F between two addressed families within one whole, independently of the answer being sought. In a temporal application, P describes admitted prefixes and F their possible continuations through the specified interface. These names need not exhaust reality's past and future. Every p in the claimed recovery domain must admit at least one partner f. Let q(p) ask the source question and r(f) read its recovered answer. Suppose recovery has been established on this relation:

    (p,f)∈J  implies  q(p)=r(f).

Then any two prefixes sharing an admitted continuation have the same earlier answer. In particular, positively different earlier answers require disjoint nonempty continuation families N(p)={f:(p,f)∈J}. Neither determinism nor reversibility of the joining relation is assumed.

**Proof.** A shared continuation f gives q(p)=r(f)=q(p′). Thus a positive difference between q(p) and q(p′) excludes every common f. Equality propagates along every finite path in the bipartite joining graph. Consequently all exactly shared answers factor through its connected components, the shared-content relation already derived in LQ13. Conversely, any value on those components supplies agreeing readings on their incident prefixes and continuations. QED

This also characterizes which source answers the joining relation can preserve before choosing r. Such an r exists on the incident continuations exactly when q is constant on the prefix vertices of every contact component. Define r(f) to be that component's q-value. Thus the component label is the greatest exactly shared content, up to a source-bound change of its code. It is derived from the given admission relation, not installed as a new conservation axiom. Different contact profiles need not give different shared labels. For the path p₀ ↔ u ↔ p₁ ↔ v ↔ p₂, all three prefix profiles differ and the endpoint profiles are disjoint, yet any exactly shared q is constant on all three prefixes. Restricting the source domain to the endpoints changes that conclusion. The intermediate admitted source cannot be silently dropped.

**A crossed group contact exposes what common content omits.** Take LQ13's relation d=a⋅b⋅c and its AC|BD cut. Write p=a⋅c on the row side and t=b⁻¹⋅d on the column side. Let K be the subgroup generated by all b⁻¹⋅a⋅b⋅a⁻¹. Conjugating such a generator gives another of the same form, so K is normal. It is the commutator subgroup [G,G]. The contact equation implies

    t = (b⁻¹⋅a⋅b⋅a⁻¹)⋅p,

so p and t have the same image in G/K. This image is common to both sides. It is also the greatest such content.

*Proof of completeness.* Every row (a,c) connects to the representative row (e,p) through the column (e,p). Every column (b,d) connects to the representative row (e,t). For arbitrary a,b,p, the following is an admitted path, alternating rows and columns:

    (e,p) ↔ (e,p) ↔ (a,a⁻¹⋅p) ↔ (b,a⋅b⋅a⁻¹⋅p)
          ↔ (e,b⁻¹⋅a⋅b⋅a⁻¹⋅p).

The first pair here is a row and the second is a column, so their equal values do not identify their roles. This path multiplies the representative label by one commutator. Reverse paths and finite concatenations realize every multiplication by K. Conversely, each contact preserves the coset, as the equation above shows. Thus the connected components are exactly the cosets of K. Every separately common answer factors through that quotient by the earlier propagation proof. QED

No homomorphism requirement on those answers was assumed. The group quotient is derived as the component label of this contact. Under a finite probability distribution positive on every admitted pair, this is also the deterministic common part obtained from the support graph in [Prabhakaran and Prabhakaran, *Assisted Common Information*, Corollary 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.1916). The support argument is established information theory. The present computation of its group components uses no distribution, sampling process or asymptotic rate.

LQ13 shows that equality of shared codes completes every component to a complete bipartite graph. In this example the smallest such enlargement admits precisely the quadruples satisfying [a⋅c]=[b⁻¹⋅d] in G/K. For a finite group of order m with |K|=k, every fixed a,b,c originally has one admitted d. The enlargement has k such d, giving m³k quadruples in place of m³. This loss differs from LQ13's exact side reduction by the centre, which preserves the original admission relation. Both classifications belong to the same R: the group notation computes its profiles and components, already defined without a preferred reference.

For S₃, any central permutation must commute with the exchanges (12) and (23). Commuting with (12) fixes position 3, and commuting with (23) fixes position 1, forcing the identity. Every commutator is even because permutation parity is multiplicative. A commutator of (12) and (23) is a three-cycle, generating all three even permutations. Thus Z(S₃)={e} and K has order three. The two common components retain only permutation parity, while the complete side profiles distinguish all 36 input pairs. The enlargement adds 432 quadruples to the original 216. The fourth-corner refusal computed by `AdjacentSwapConjugation` is among them.

On the incident families, transposing J exchanges the two sides and leaves their exactly shared content unchanged: q(p)=r(f) is the same equality read in either direction. Either reading can restrict the compatible descriptions of the other. This is a symmetry of the constraint, without a premise about the order of discovering it. It does not construct a physical reverse operation or give an internal reader access to both sides. A temporal orientation, a permitted intervention and a traversal used to solve the equations each need their actual relations.

Now propose an intermediate cut c:P→M as a complete boundary for this continuation family. Completeness has an addressed meaning: some relation S⊆M×F must satisfy

    (p,f)∈J  iff  (c(p),f)∈S.

This preserves admission and refusal of the actual continuations. Equal cut images therefore give equal N(p). Since those families are nonempty, the first part forces equal q-answers. Hence q=d∘c for a unique answer function d on the realized image c(P). Its graph is {(c(p),q(p)):p∈P}, which is single-valued by the argument. This is a derived factorization through the middle, rather than a separate retention premise at each middle state. The same argument applies at each supplied intervening cut. Its ordering comes from the given prefix/continuation decomposition, without a clock outside the whole.

The least exact admission boundary is LQ13's class under equality of N(p). M72 shows that this boundary must retain every answer recovered by the specified later readings. Thus a witness need not look like the earlier value at a chosen local address. It may be expressed in which continuations can join the prefix. If a proposed current image merges prefixes that permit different relevant continuations, it omits an actual relation. Adding that relation to the account refines the image of the same whole. Calling the old image complete would instead conceal the missing dependence.

**A finite complete contact forces unique partners under full recovery.** Take J⊆U×V with |U|=|V|=N>0. Require at least one partner for every x∈U and at most one source for every y∈V. There are at least N admitted pairs by the first requirement and at most N by the second. Equality forces exactly one partner at every form on both sides. Therefore J is the graph of a bijection. No temporal order enters this counting proof.

To apply this as a repeated transition, additionally identify U and V with the same complete current-state carrier K and use the same J at each step. It is then a permutation. The claimed completeness includes the instruments, controllers and other relations relevant to these transitions. A finite local alphabet does not establish that scope, and calling reality one whole does not supply such a state decomposition. Within this temporal application, forward determinism and inverse totality follow from full source recovery, continuation and the fixed finite carrier.

Each condition matters. Without a successor for every source, J={(0,0),(0,1)} branches while retaining its source on a two-form carrier. Without source recovery, J={(0,0),(0,1),(1,1)} branches and permits every source to continue. On the unbounded carrier of finite binary words, J={(w,w0),(w,w1)} has two successors per source and exactly one predecessor for every nonempty output word. Deleting its last bit recovers the entire earlier word. M71's `RetainedWhole<H,Event>` gives the same separation in the record grammar: both next event fields preserve H. Thus complete past recovery does not generally choose the next event. It does so in this fixed finite case with a successor at every form. Preserving only a question q rather than the complete prior form is also insufficient for the permutation conclusion.

This sharpens M54's finite-carrier statement by removing its separate forward-determinism premise. If normalized successor probabilities are additionally assigned to such a finite transition relation, each source's only available successor has probability one. M32 then gives invariance of the joint Shannon entropy under transport of an arbitrary initial distribution. Neither those probabilities nor the finite-carrier and recovery premises follow from happening alone. Finiteness of each record does not make a growing record grammar a fixed finite complete carrier.

Every unbounded path made of these transitions is periodic. If a complete return actually occurs, M51 forbids an advancing internal lap count on the completely returned state. An indefinitely growing distinguishable event record would therefore require more than this fixed finite carrier. The conclusion concerns the asserted completeness of the state description, rather than identifying the whole history Ω with one of its current configurations. An available successor is still different from an actually occurring next event, as M61 separates.

**The entropy balance separates retention from new uncertainty.** For a finite joint probability distribution of two specified readings X and Y, expanding H(X,Y) in its two orders gives

    H(Y) − H(X) = H(Y|X) − H(X|Y).

If Y recovers X, the last term is zero. Its entropy can still increase when the next reading has alternatives not determined by the earlier one. For Y=(X,B), projection recovers X and the increase is H(B|X). If B is determined by X, the increase is zero. If B is an independent fair bit, it is one bit. The fixed finite complete-carrier case above excludes this branching and gives zero increase. A probability assignment on an unbounded word grammar or an expanding record need not do so.

Conversely, unchanged entropy alone does not certify retention. Two independent fair binary readings have H(X)=H(Y)=1, while H(X|Y)=H(Y|X)=1. Their equal marginal counts conceal both lost recoverability and an undetermined new answer. The distinction already carried by M72 is source-bound, whereas the entropy balance summarizes a supplied distribution. As M63 establishes, zero conditional entropy gives universal recovery on a finite admitted domain only with positive weight on every admitted case. This identity neither specifies where the new field originated nor identifies a change between two readings with destruction of an event in the whole history.

The physical cut must account for every relation used by the remaining process, including its controller, preparation and any source-related reserve. Closure of reality does not by itself prove the displayed joining equation for an arbitrary current-state description. For instance, if a future program is selected using the earlier answer, that selection is a crossing relation. It cannot be omitted from c and then used to claim that the answer returned from nothing. The continuation f must contain the relevant program or outcome distinctions, rather than name one coarse command whose different realizations are selected from the hidden past.

A chronological slice is not automatically such a complete cut. A relation between an earlier occurrence and a later one may cross the slice without being represented among its listed local state fields. M72 requires the boundary to account for that relation too. It therefore does not infer a local memory cell at every intermediate time, or assume that current local fields determine every future possibility. The derived witness belongs to the complete relational boundary. Finding its physical carrier or establishing a local state representation is another question.

The hypotheses have distinct failure modes:

| Proposed relaxation | Explicit boundary |
|---|---|
| Use the same coarse cut value for every prefix | With J={(p₀,f₀),(p₁,f₁)} and different paired answers, the cut projects both futures but cannot admit exactly the right one for each actual prefix. |
| Treat any different continuation profiles as a fully recoverable bit | N(p₀)={u} and N(p₁)={u,v} differ, yet their common u forces q(p₀)=q(p₁) whenever recovery is exact. Sensitivity of admission is weaker than an exact historical record. |
| Drop the requirement of an admitted continuation | A prefix with N(p)=∅ satisfies the recovery implication vacuously and can carry any earlier answer. No later witness has been supplied for it. |

For a concrete prohibition, a shared admitted continuation and the two recovered source answers already supply the finite obstruction of M71. An exhaustive enumeration of all futures is unnecessary for that witness. Establishing that a proposed cut represents an entire continuation family has the stronger two-way admission obligation above. The relation J must describe the contact under investigation. Defining J afterward by q(p)=r(f) would install the recovery premise instead of discovering it. Likewise, one coincidentally correct observed outcome does not establish the displayed implication over an untested preparation family.

There is a direct functional construction. If an established endpoint factorization is q=D∘B∘A, the decoder on the cut after A is D∘B. It has only that cut as its input, with the same remaining process B. The middle answer need not be supplied by an independently chosen middle decoder. VC37 already exhibits end-to-end correction with a wrong intermediate reading under the old interpretation. M72 requires a sufficient middle relation, not correctness of that old reading. Existence of the abstract d, availability of a finite physical reading procedure and a record already decoded at the middle are separate properties.

`RecoveryBeforeSuffix<A,B,F>` constructs this middle factorization from F's established recovery and its exact observation `SequentialReading<A,B>`. Its body builds the suffix decoder with `DecodeRefinedEvidence`, binding every input through the actual seam. It introduces no retention protocol or new equality of answers. The constructor checks a supplied binary split. The relational argument above covers every complete cut and does not depend on this particular expression grouping.

`Checks/ClosedInformation.swift` applies it to the existing two swaps (x,y)→(y,x)→(x,y). Endpoint recovery of x supplies the decoder at (y,x). A generic type equality identifies that decoder with `ReadAfterResidentSwap<M>`, whose only parameter is the middle M. It receives no earlier source or earlier answer. For y=0, the middle's first field is the same at x=0 and x=1, yet the recovered answers differ. The derived decoder uses the second field. Refusals check substitution of the naive first-field reading, omission of the rest of the cut and substitution of another suffix. These are static composition relations, without a claim that two scheduled swaps implement an autonomous physical clock.

This gives a backward obligation from an established later witness: the specified distinction was not absent from any complete interface through which that recovery factors. It does not supply a later witness for every event. The stronger forward permanence claim remains open. To use M72 in investigating reality, establish a source-bound recovery relation and test which intermediate descriptions can actually support its continuations. No global information-conservation premise is required for that particular exclusion.

### M51

**M51 (complete recurrence cannot carry a distinguishable lap count).** Let Γ be the declared complete current-state carrier, including the controller, instruments and memory, and let U be its fixed deterministic internal transition. Suppose U^p(x)=x for p>0. For every specified reading R of Γ, R(U^p(x))=R(x). More generally U^(n+p)(x)=U^n(x) for every finite n, so the same internal continuation produces the same subsequent readings. A lap counter that distinguishes these returns cannot be a function of this fully recurrent state.

**Derivation.** Substitute the returned source into R. Apply U repeatedly to the source equality to obtain equality after every further finite continuation. An adaptive controller does not escape the result when its state is included in Γ: its choices and memory also return. If a counter advances, a record accumulates or a changed instrument distinguishes the returns, that difference is a coordinate in which the declared whole has not returned. `AnyReadingAfterWholeReturn` checks the first implication generically for every supplied `PredicateReading` after the existing double internal swap, without assuming equality of the reading's answers. The finite-path conclusion follows by induction on the declared deterministic transition.

The conclusion concerns internal distinguishability of completed periods. Different phases within a period may remain distinguishable, and an event graph may distinguish historical positions. If access to those positions distinguishes the returns, that access was omitted from Γ or its permitted readings. An outside observer counting repetitions is unavailable in the closed whole. Thus a claim of both complete recurrence and an advancing internal lap count identifies a concrete inconsistency in the state declaration. It does not establish that time requires an unbounded record, that all dynamics are recurrent, or that the historical positions themselves have been identified.

This does not forbid an unchanged certificate of the return law. A record establishing U^p=id can be present before and after a recurrence and remain correct. It does not distinguish which pass has completed. A temporary signal within a period can likewise disappear on return. The excluded claim is a fresh distinguishing witness at the complete return, or one obtained afterward through the same restored internal continuation. A static proof of a law and acquisition of a new fact about this occurrence are different questions. Nor does the whole of occurrence relations in The Cut automatically equal Γ: a complete current-configuration model needs its own scope. Comparing internal cuts of one reality does not place that reality on an exterior time axis. M55's proper retention concerns those actual occurrence relations, independently of whether a selected configuration pattern repeats.

Together M50 and M51 locate the unresolved physical question: what makes successive occurrences distinguishable within the actual relations of the whole? The record commitment supplies growing historical relations in its model. Current-state injectivity supplies another, narrower preservation property. Neither property should inherit the other's conclusion because both are called no loss.

### M52

**M52 (mutual recovery within each state does not imply recovery across states).** Let a state contain a resident law address and two records of the same value: (law,(x,x)). Both records determine one another. For any specified update f, the next state (law,(f(x),f(x))) has the same mutual-recovery relation and retains the law address. This property places no injectivity requirement on f. In particular, identity preserves x, whereas the constant-zero law merges x=0 and x=1. Both are internal update descriptions satisfying the same endpoint-registration constraints. The argument extends to any specified finite number n of copies: replace (x,x) by the diagonal tuple of n copies. Applying f to their shared value preserves every pairwise agreement, independently of whether f preserves distinctions of x. The two-copy constructor supplies the minimal witness of this boundary.

`MutualRecordUpdate<R>` derives the two equal-record endpoints for arbitrary supplied readings R. Its Pair also represents the two-endpoint history. That complete history retains the input in both examples. `IdentityReading` and `ConstantResetReading` supply the two concrete update laws, whose answers follow from their bodies. The current reset output includes the resident law address and both zero records, with no input-dependent residual hidden in its type. `Checks/ClosedInformation.swift` derives mutual equality at each endpoint, retention of the law address, historical recovery, identity preservation and generic merging of reset outputs. The two reset histories are positively distinguished by their original values. The attempted preservation certificate for the one-input reset is refused.

| Shared requirement | Identity update | Constant-zero update |
|---|---|---|
| Law address retained inside each history | Yes | Yes |
| Each record determines the other at both endpoints | Yes | Yes |
| Original value distinguished in the complete history | Yes | Yes |
| Original value recovered from the later current state | Yes | No |

These are two mathematical possibilities under the listed relations. They establish that mutual recoverability and historical retention do not select future-state preservation. They do not establish physical realizability of a reset whose complete physical output has no residual. If such residual preservation is imposed, the constant current-state output must be extended accordingly. The issue is whether that stronger requirement follows from mutual witnessing. The shared endpoint relations do not supply it.

For a separate uniform binary preparation in each fixed-law example, the complete represented history has one bit of entropy. Identity leaves one bit in the current records and zero conditional uncertainty about x. Reset leaves zero bits in the current records and one bit of conditional uncertainty about x. The two records agree with probability one in both cases. Therefore agreement between records and lower entropy of the records do not by themselves establish retained knowledge of the earlier source.

The missing relation runs across the transition. If Q names the earlier distinction, U the actual update and E the later available reading, preservation requires a constructed D with Q = D ∘ E ∘ U. Equal records at the next endpoint may answer a different question, such as the new value f(x). Requiring them to witness the same earlier Q adds the temporal fidelity just written. Its composition along admitted transitions is the work of `ReadingFactors` and the existing transport constructors. A stronger use of the word registration can include this requirement, but then it must enter as a premise at that use.

This answers the current foundational test. Mutual witnessing understood as the displayed recovery relations within a slice, together with retention of event relations in the whole, admits both later-access outcomes. To force a future witness, the theory needs a relation preserving the particular distinction through the change. Closure, the existence of two records and their mutual recovery cannot replace that relation. M53 tests a stronger response condition, and M54 supplies a sufficient temporal-witness condition.

### M53

**M53 (reciprocal input dependence still permits a joint merger).** On the declared two-bit carrier, compare swap S(a,b)=(b,a) with F(a,b)=(a XOR b,a XOR b). In both laws, varying either input while fixing the other changes the other participant's next value. Thus the specified dependence goes in both directions for every setting of the fixed bit. Nevertheless S is invertible and F merges 00 with 11, and 01 with 10. On the initially mutually recorded states 00 and 11, both laws again produce equal endpoint records. Reciprocal dependence does not select the preserving law.

| Input | Swap S | Duplicate parity F |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | 00 | 00 |
| 01 | 10 | 11 |
| 10 | 01 | 11 |
| 11 | 11 | 00 |

The difference is joint. Changing one input is detectable while the other is fixed, but changing both together can leave F's complete output unchanged. A response to each separate variation is weaker than separating all admitted joint variations. `WitnessTransfer.swift` supplies the four F rows in its declared binary grammar. The checks positively distinguish every peer-response pair, derive the same response property for swap, and derive F's merged outputs with the resident law address retained inside the state. Recording either transition as a complete history retains its original source. A named refusal rejects recovery of 11 from F's 00 image.

This tests an explicit functional model of reciprocal causal sensitivity. A claim about physical interventions must also establish the corresponding internal preparations and response law. As in M52, no residual-free physical erasure is inferred. The mathematical conclusion is that the displayed reciprocal-response condition, even combined with the shared record conditions, does not imply joint preservation. If an additional physical constraint rules out F as a complete-state law, that constraint contributes to the preservation argument.

A stricter causal restriction can change the answer. For a binary law of the pure cross form (u(b),v(a)), with no dependence on the receiving component's own earlier value, reciprocal sensitivity makes u and v nonconstant maps of a two-element set. Each is then a bijection, so the whole map is invertible. F is outside this restricted family because each output depends on both earlier inputs. This finite corollary shows why the complete input dependence matters. For larger value sets, nonconstancy alone would again be insufficient.

### M54

**M54 (a complete set of faithful future witnesses gives whole-representation recovery).** Fix a finite addressed Pair/terminal tree schema for a nonempty family of admitted earlier states, with all distinctions being claimed included at its addresses. Let E be one actual later image of the transition. Suppose each terminal value has an addressed, uniform decoder from E that returns that same earlier value on every admitted source. Joining those local decoders according to the declared tree reconstructs the whole represented state from E. No separate whole-recovery equality is a premise. Conversely, a supplied whole-recovery decoder gives each local decoder by projection.

**Derivation.** At a terminal, use its supplied temporal witness. At a Pair, use the two already constructed child decoders on the same E and pair their answers. The result equals the earlier Pair because both addressed components are recovered. Structural induction reaches the root of the finite grammar. A missing child would leave a source distinction uncovered. If source topology can vary, its shape must also be recovered or fixed by a justified scope restriction. Selecting a reconstruction tree from the unobserved source would violate the same uniformity condition as selecting a decoder from it. For the converse, follow the terminal's fixed address through the recovered whole. Both directions use one image and compatible questions, without choosing decoders from an unobserved source. This reconstructs the declared structural representation. Equality of arbitrary nominal wrappers, or completeness of an encoding for physical reality, requires the corresponding additional representation claim.

The no-merger argument itself does not require a finite whole. More generally, let a declared family of questions separate the earlier distinctions being claimed. Suppose every question in that family has a uniform faithful decoder through the same later image. If two later images coincide, every transported question has the same answer on their earlier sources. This contradicts any positive separator of those sources. Thus all distinctions covered by the family are preserved. The family may be infinite. A finite combined inverse, finite access to all its witnesses and completeness for physical reality are further claims. The finite tree theorem above supplies a constructive combined decoder on its stated scope. `AnswersThroughSharedDecoder` implements the question-by-question equality used in the general argument.

`JointEvidenceFromMemory` already implements the induction join. `EarlierFirstThroughSwap` derives recovery of the earlier first value from the next second field. `EarlierSecondThroughSwap` derives the other temporal witness from that same next image. `EarlierPairThroughSwap` joins them and recovers the earlier coordinate pair for arbitrary structural values. The decoder receives exactly the later image. `EvidenceBesideInput` carries the resident law alongside this recovery. The wrong next address is refused. These constructors derive both local equalities from the actual swap, then derive the whole-coordinate result from the local witnesses. The caller supplies neither a final answer nor a whole inverse.

This specifies a sufficient preservation mechanism in the language of the theory: every earlier distinction has a faithful later witness, those witnesses cover the declared whole, and their interpretations join on the same actual image. One later witness can read several internal components jointly, as in M31, so preservation need not assign each earlier value to an isolated storage cell. Faithful witnessing across time is stronger than mutual agreement or reciprocal influence within a state. The stronger relation concerns which earlier alternatives remain separable under joint change.

For a fixed finite complete carrier with a successor at every form, full source recovery forces permutation dynamics even if the transition was initially specified as a relation rather than a function, by M72's counting argument. M32 then gives invariance of the joint Shannon entropy under transport of the stated probabilities. With an expanding history representation, recovery on the image still follows, while surjectivity onto a fixed carrier does not. A repeated-knowledge claim additionally needs these temporal witnesses to compose through every permitted step and remain accessible to the learner. These are the precise bridges from witnessing to permanence, entropy and reusable knowledge.

### M55

**M55 (addressed retention needs a proper record extension to define strict order).** Recoverability or `Permanent` alone does not define an irreflexive temporal order. A recursive Pair may have itself at its left address. `SelfRetainingRecord` supplies that type and `StationaryRetention` conforms to `Permanent` with identical endpoints. Both conformance and endpoint equality are positive witnesses. This type lies outside the well-founded finite-tree interpretation.

For a declared finite record grammar, proper prefix inclusion does give an internal strict order without first assigning clock values. A proper extension contains additional constructor structure, so it has strictly greater finite size. It cannot be its own proper prefix or occur in a finite cycle of proper extensions. Prefix composition is transitive. Branched records need not be comparable. The size argument proves these properties of the grammar. **A proper extension can preserve the abstract history type.** Let i:A→B be the actual injective retention of the represented occurrences, preserving the specified old relations, and let b∈B be a witnessed occurrence outside i(A). Consider the ordered whole of integer-addressed occurrences and its cuts Hₖ={m∈ℤ:m≤k}. Each Hₖ is a proper subhistory of Hₖ₊₁ under the actual inclusion, with k+1 a new occurrence. The integers here supply a presentation of the example, not an independently existing physical coordinate system or a claim that reality has an infinite past.

**The admitted transition relation bounds every nondecreasing state record.** Let R⊆X×X be the stated directed relation between complete configurations for this question, with all used contexts included. For an admitted edge xRy, some monotone record can distinguish its advance strictly exactly when there is no R-path from y back to x. Necessity follows from the return inequality. Hence H(X|Y)=1, H(X|R)=2, and H(Y|R)=1. Nevertheless H(R|Y)=H(R|R)=0: replacing the image pair by its relative bit loses no answer to this question. Adding deterministic records while retaining X gives H(X,Y,R)=H(X)=3. These use the ordinary discrete entropy definition in [Shannon's original paper](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf). The table and this application are the present construction. The numerical values require the stated probability law. Neither the raw length of a record nor its set of possible spellings fixes that law.

This separates three orders. Retaining more historical structure can extend a prefix without changing its ensemble entropy. Acquiring another accessible distinction can refine the reader's answer classes without eliminating any whole-source case. Finally, shortening a sufficient representation can merge image cases while preserving every requested answer. None of these orders alone specifies a thermodynamic arrow or a duration. In M60's admitted frame family the relative class suffices to select whether the second image needs crossing. Reusing an earlier acquired class on a later channel pair still requires a relation between those two occasions. M62 does not silently supply that temporal premise.

### M63

**M63 (refinement orders possibilities, while entropy averages their weights).** Fix a finite admitted source domain, one question Q and an available reading E. A further retained reading Z gives (E,Z). Every new compatible source cell is a subset of its old E-cell, so its set of possible Q-answers cannot grow. This conclusion uses no probability law. For any fixed probability law, the averaged conditional entropy obeys H(Q|E,Z) ≤ H(Q|E). It does not assert H(Q|E=e,Z=z) ≤ H(Q|E=e) for each realized z. The averaging and concavity properties are standard results of [Shannon's entropy analysis](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf). Their distinction matters to the book's account of learning.

The same eight M62 cases give an exact counterexample to a decrease at every realized reading. Give X=000 and X=111 weight 5 each, and each other case weight 1, then divide by 16. The relative answer R is zero with probability 3/4 and one with probability 1/4. Reading only the first reported coordinate U and obtaining U=1 leaves exactly X=010, 011, 100, 101. They are equiprobable and their R-answers are 1, 0, 0, 1. Thus H(R|U=1)=1 > H(R)=h(1/4), where h(t)=-t log₂t-(1-t)log₂(1-t). Four source cases have been excluded, yet the two remaining answers are more evenly weighted. For U=0, the answer-one probability is 1/6. Averaging gives H(R|U)=(3/4)h(1/6)+(1/4), consistent with the general inequality. This changes the probability law on the existing cases, not their actual reading relations. The arithmetic is stated here and uses no runtime experiment.

There is a useful exact bridge back to VI. If every member of the finite admitted domain has positive probability, H(Q|E)=0 exactly when Q is constant on every E-cell. Each conditional entropy is nonnegative, and its contribution has positive weight. A zero sum therefore requires a single Q-answer in every cell. Conversely, a constant answer gives zero conditional entropy. That is precisely the finite set-level factorization Q=D∘E. The proof establishes the decoder as a mathematical map on the admitted image. Access to an implementation remains a separate relation. Without full support, zero conditional entropy certifies only the supported cases and can conceal a different answer assigned probability zero. VI's universal type claims do not gain permission to ignore an admitted case from a probability assignment.

One actual whole does not require a sampler outside reality to make these distinctions meaningful. The admitted cases can describe alternatives not yet separated by an internal reader's evidence. A probability law weights those descriptions and needs its own justification. It is not forced by the statement that nothing exists outside the whole. The stronger endpoint for a fixed VI question is agreement across all admitted cases still compatible with the reading. Changing their positive weights then cannot change the answer. A rare observation can increase the reader's uncertainty without undoing retained knowledge. Once the question is settled across its whole admitted cell, further refinement cannot unsettle it unless the question, domain or correctness of an earlier reading changes.

### M64

**M64 (an acquired relative fact can survive a change of both connections).** In M60's I/S family let a contrast probe acquire the relative bit r. Later data x,y are read as f′(x),g′(y). The requested output expresses both through the current first connection: f′(x),f′(y). A zero fact selects the unchanged image pair. A one fact selects the same first image and a crossing of the second. If the later connections retain the acquired relative class, these two clauses give the requested output for every addressed x,y. This follows by the four I/S cases and S∘S=I. It requires no identification of their absolute orientations. Preservation of that class is also necessary for this decoder to work uniformly when the later second data pair can be contrasting: a wrong class applies exactly one unwanted crossing to that pair. A restricted diagonal second pair can hide the mismatch, as M60 and VC83 predict.

`MixedFrameReplayAfterExchange<P>` supplies an acquired and source-bound instance. It first acquires the one fact from a common 01 probe through direct/crossed connections. Arbitrary new data P are resident alongside that acquisition. `ExchangeFrameConnections` exchanges the two connections by their actual source projections, yielding crossed/direct. `StepBeside` carries the acquired record, including its old source, through the change. The whole presented to the new reading contains the current data and connections beside that archive. The observation reads the old fact and the new images from those two addresses.

The final decoder `MixedFrameFactDecoder` receives only that fact and those images. The independent question uses `CommonFirstFrameSource` to specify both data pairs under the actual current first connection, then applies `SharedCrossedPairFrame`. The `ReadingFactors` conformance proves their answers equal for arbitrary P. Its caller supplies the data-pair structure, not the answer equality. Generic checks connect the acquisition output to the frame-change input and show that the archive survives. The two decoder clauses are explicit source-free applications on the fact/image input. They do not themselves certify arbitrary claims about unobserved physical channels.

The positive boundary uses the same one fact and the same new images (10,01). In the tracked mixed exchange these arise from data (01,01) through crossed/direct, so the required common-frame output is (10,10). Direct/direct connections on data (10,01) report the identical image pair, but require (10,01). Both configurations can retain the same old archive. Their common decoder input therefore cannot determine both answers. The coordinate contrast and equal available evidence are checked positively, and the attempted untracked replay is refused separately. A retained calibration record certifies its acquisition. Later reuse additionally needs the relation that makes its relative class apply to the new question.

The result connects persistence to scoped change. Knowledge can survive a change in both named mechanisms when the relevant relation survives. Conversely, an intact archive and matching surface images can fail to carry that relation. M64 closes the tracked mixed-exchange bridge left open by M60–M62. It establishes this transition family, not unrestricted persistence of calibration under every future interaction.
