## Detail: §5. Candidate Physical Correspondences

These are questions for an implementation, not completed VI instances. A physical correspondence must declare its state carrier, reading, dynamical operation, record and cost before the relevant conditional theorem applies. Finite dimension does not imply finitely many states. A stable-looking record does not establish indefinite faithful retention. The earlier tables marked several such missing premises as verified; those verdicts are withdrawn.

## Detail: Quantum measurement

**Projective branch calculation.** Fix a nonzero orthogonal projector P on a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space. In the ideal Lüders instrument, an input density operator ρ with p=Tr(Pρ)>0 gives the conditional output L_P(ρ)=PρP/p. The instrument and positive-probability branch are premises. The [measurement formulation](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/learning/en/courses/general-formulation-of-quantum-information/general-measurements/formulations-of-measurements) separates the outcome law from the post-measurement state. [Shor's lecture](https://math.mit.edu/~shor/18.435/oldlectures/lecture6.html) gives the projective update and explains why outcome probabilities alone do not specify a general instrument's residual state.

The map L_P is constant on all such inputs exactly when rank(P)=1. If P=|u⟩⟨u| with u normalized, then PρP=⟨u|ρ|u⟩P=pP, so L_P(ρ)=P. Conversely, if rank(P)>1, choose orthogonal unit vectors u and v in its range. Their density operators each have p=1 and are unchanged by L_P, so the two outputs differ. This proves necessity and sufficiency for the stated instrument and unrestricted input domain. Restricting prior inputs can change that conclusion.

Thus a nondegenerate outcome fixes one post-measurement ray, or its density operator, rather than one vector including an arbitrary global phase. For rank g>1, there are continuously many rays within the eigenspace, not g possible pure states. The integer g counts independent basis directions. Even rank one need not identify the input: whenever the whole Hilbert space has another direction v orthogonal to u, the states u and (u+v)/√2 both permit that outcome and give the same conditional output. A single certain outcome likewise need not identify a state: P=I on a space of dimension greater than one leaves every input unchanged.

Quantum.swift checks a supplied recursive dimension expression against One. HydrogenLevel2 supplies four basis-direction slots, without deriving a Hamiltonian or enumerating all rays in its eigenspace. QuantumMeasurement.swift's chosen SolutionSet=Never supplies no projector, update or detector. These declarations therefore cannot replace the calculation above. An actual VI correspondence must bind its target to the post-measurement ray and its instrument to the stated update. The conditioned subsystem update does not establish global information loss or irreversibility. A detector and retained accessible record require the separate relations of M32 and V=I GC3 within the same whole.

**Response equality and certain discrimination.** A qubit supplies three different identification questions. With Pauli matrices X,Y,Z, write a density operator as ρ=(I+rₓX+rᵧY+r_zZ)/2, where r is real and |r|≤1. A measurement along a unit vector n has effects (I±n·σ)/2 and probabilities (1±n·r)/2, by taking the trace. Exact knowledge of the three response distributions along X,Y,Z determines r and hence ρ. This is injectivity of a map to probability laws. Finite experiments estimate those laws from repeated preparations, rather than returning their exact real parameters from one specimen. [IBM's discrimination and tomography formulation](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/learning/en/courses/general-formulation-of-quantum-information/general-measurements/discrimination-and-tomography) states the different access assumptions.

For example, |+⟩=(|0⟩+|1⟩)/√2 and |−⟩=(|0⟩−|1⟩)/√2 have the same Z outcome law, (1/2,1/2), but opposite certain X outcomes. A different measurement separates this pair. The states |0⟩ and |+⟩ already have different Z laws, (1,0) and (1/2,1/2), but the possible result 0 is shared. Different laws alone do not give a certain answer from one record.

More strongly, two supplied pure states admit a measurement that always distinguishes them from one copy exactly when they are orthogonal. For necessity, group all outcomes decoded as the first state into an effect E, with 0≤E≤I. Certain discrimination requires ⟨ψ|E|ψ⟩=1 and ⟨φ|E|φ⟩=0. Positivity implies (I−E)|ψ⟩=0 and E|φ⟩=0: a positive operator with zero expectation annihilates the vector, by its positive square root. Hence ⟨ψ|φ⟩=⟨ψ|E|φ⟩=0. Conversely, projection onto ψ and its orthogonal complement distinguishes an orthogonal pair. This is the standard [nonorthogonal-state discrimination restriction](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/learning/en/courses/basics-of-quantum-information/quantum-circuits/limitations-on-quantum-information), with its operator proof supplied here. An inconclusive outcome or a nonzero error probability changes the task.

The restriction extends from identifying a state to identifying any exact classical answer q about it. If one fixed measurement and decoder always answer q on a supplied pure-state domain, nonorthogonal states must have equal q-values, by the same grouped-effect argument. Consequently q is constant on every component of that domain's nonorthogonality graph. On the full set of pure qubit states this graph is connected: nonorthogonal pairs are adjacent, while orthogonal ψ and φ both overlap (ψ+φ)/√2. Therefore every such universally certain answer from one unknown pure qubit is constant. Restricting the preparation domain to a known orthogonal pair permits a nonconstant answer. For any fixed finite number k of independently prepared copies of each candidate, a nonzero overlap remains nonzero after taking its kth power, so the full-domain exact discrimination obstruction persists even for collective measurements.

Seeing Is Encoding SiE9 expresses the corresponding fixed finite-outcome condition through overlapping response supports. That condition also applies to classical noisy measurements. The quantum model adds restrictions on which measurements exist, supplied here by positive effects and the Hilbert-space geometry. Neither the overlap condition nor a generic VI candidate set derives that geometry, interference or the Born rule. Classical probability embeds in the diagonal case: diagonal states ρ=diag(pᵢ) and effects E_z=diag(m(z|i)) give Tr(E_zρ)=Σᵢm(z|i)pᵢ. This recovers the classical response law without equating a quantum state with a hidden classical label.

IiS12 makes the scope of a uniform answer concrete. For every fixed reference phase, its two product preparations are orthogonal. Across unknown phases the answer families contain a nonorthogonal pair, excluding one universally certain reader. A positive candidate that passes every promised preparation gives a negative complementary weight on an input available through the same family's filtering. Directly preparing the relative distinction in orthogonal symmetry sectors admits a certain reading on the same carrier. The preparation relation changes which knowledge can be acquired. It does not turn an invariant label on descriptions into an automatically available operation.

The accessible system is part of each statement. If an orthogonal preparation record is retained elsewhere inside the same whole and becomes accessible, reading that record can identify the preparation even when the qubit alone cannot. Adjoining a fixed, uncorrelated apparatus state adds no such label and preserves the initial overlap. Thus the absence of an exterior does not make every internal distinction available to every reader. These are explicit mathematical model results. The current Swift quantum examples do not yet encode the general operator positivity argument or a calibrated quantum instrument. IiS12 now includes the source-bound finite negative-weight counterexample in its type checks.

## Detail: Crystallization

A discretized lattice can supply a finite configuration model if its site values are also finite. One can then ask which configurations a chosen observation distinguishes and whether a specified dynamics reaches a stable answer. An energy weighting alone does not construct an append-only record of eliminated configurations: a positive Boltzmann weight is not exclusion. The earlier ice table did not verify the retention, monotone elimination or unique-configuration premises. This remains a possible finite modeling exercise, not evidence that physical crystallization executes the VI acquisition loop.

**Local ice rule and global compatibility.** In the finite Vacuum/In/Out grammar, induction makes InCount and OutCount the counts of the two direction symbols. IceVertex now requires both counts to equal Two. Previously it required only InCount, admitting two-bond and five-bond expressions as vertices. Both were accepted by the old compiler check and are refused after the correction. Two of each symbol gives length four and exactly six ordered words: choose the two inward positions among four. This is the completeness argument for the six supplied aliases, rather than completeness inferred from a list's length.

For a finite loopless four-regular graph G with N vertices and 2N edges, give each edge one orientation. Its endpoint flags must be complementary: inward at one end is outward at the other. Let Ω(G) contain the orientations with indegree two at every vertex. Restriction maps Ω(G) into the product of the six local pattern sets. Its image is exactly the families agreeing on every shared edge. It is injective because a compatible endpoint flag fixes each edge, and surjective onto that compatible family because these choices reconstruct all edges and preserve every local rule. Thus local rules plus actual shared-edge compatibility characterize the global domain exactly. Local rule satisfaction alone does not supply the compatibility.

These global constraints alone already exclude uniqueness on a nonempty graph, without counting all configurations. Each connected component has an Euler circuit because every degree is even. Orient its edges along that circuit. Every visit pairs an arrival with a departure, so indegree and outdegree are both two at each vertex. This constructs an element of Ω(G). Reversing every edge constructs another element: it exchanges the two equal degrees and changes every oriented edge. Reversal is an involution with no fixed configuration, so |Ω(G)| is positive and even. This concerns configurations on the specified graph, before any quotient by graph symmetries. Further constraints can break the reversal symmetry. The proof supplies existence and nonuniqueness, without claiming an extensive entropy or a physical equilibrium result.

For example, after choosing a reference direction on each edge, write b∈{0,1}^{2N} for its orientation bits. Then the exact finite count is

```
|Ω(G)| = Σ_b Π_v 1[indegree_b(v)=2].
```

Under the uniform law on a nonempty Ω(G), the configuration entropy is k_B ln|Ω(G)|. A nonuniform law requires its weights instead (M32, M63). Replacing the simultaneous vertex conditions by independent probabilities gives 2^(2N)(6/16)^N=(3/2)^N, the Pauling estimate. The independence step is an approximation, as discussed in [Pauling's paper](https://elearning.uniroma1.it/pluginfile.php/656178/mod_resource/content/1/Pauling1935.pdf). [Lieb's exact square-ice result](https://harvest.aps.org/v2/journals/articles/10.1103/PhysRev.162.162/fulltext) has growth factor (4/3)^(3/2), with its two-dimensional lattice and thermodynamic limit. That factor is not an exact enumeration of ordinary hexagonal ice. The type-level local rule proves neither global count nor an equilibrium phase diagram. The retained graph and its edge relations are all internal to the model, without an external reservoir.

### M68

**M68 (which edge readings remove every alternative).** Fix a finite loopless graph G, allowing distinct parallel edges, and a prescribed outdegree α(v) at every vertex. Let Ωα(G) be its admitted orientations. Each edge is one addressed object shared by its endpoints. Reconnecting two edges can preserve all degrees while changing G, so degree-preserving rewiring is not an operation on this fixed domain.

Two members x,y of Ωα(G) differ on an edge set D. Orient D as in x. At any vertex, let a of these edges leave and b enter. Reversing D changes its full outdegree by b−a. Both full outdegrees equal α(v), so a=b. Every nonempty finite balanced directed graph contains a directed cycle: follow outgoing edges until a vertex repeats, using balance to rule out an endpoint with no outgoing edge. Remove the resulting cycle and repeat. This partitions D into edge-disjoint directed cycles. Reversing them one at a time changes x to y, preserving all degrees and the original edges at every step. This is the standard cycle-flip argument for prescribed-degree orientations, stated in [Aichholzer et al., §2](https://arxiv.org/html/1902.06103v1#S2), applied here to the identification question.

Now fix a set F of measured edges and retain their directions from an actual x. Its compatible alternatives are the members of Ωα(G) agreeing with x on F. The same proof connects any two such alternatives using cycles entirely outside F. Consequently,

```
x is the only compatible orientation
    iff the directed graph x restricted to E(G)\F has no directed cycle.
```

If a directed cycle remains, its reversal is a distinct compatible orientation. If another orientation remains, its nonempty difference from x contains such a cycle. Thus an instance certificate must meet every directed cycle of the actual x. For a narrower question q, retained readings determine q exactly when q is unchanged by every admitted directed-cycle reversal in this compatible family. Closure under all these reversals is what makes this local invariance test complete. Restricting the operation to short cycles or adding other constraints requires a new connectivity argument.

For ice, the uniform question has a stronger graph-only answer. More generally, suppose every degree of G is even and α(v)=degree(v)/2. Then

```
reading F identifies every member of Ωα(G)
    iff the undirected graph (V(G), E(G)\F) is a forest.
```

A forest contains no possible directed cycle, which proves sufficiency. Conversely, suppose an unmeasured undirected cycle C remains. A pair of parallel edges may form a cycle of length two. Orient C cyclically. Removing its edges leaves every degree even, so Euler circuits orient each remaining nontrivial component with equal in- and outdegrees. Together these orientations give x∈Ωα(G). Reversing C gives a different y with exactly the same readings on F. This proves necessity without assuming independent vertex patterns. It also shows why the even-degree balanced hypothesis matters: arbitrary prescribed degrees need not permit either cyclic orientation of a given undirected cycle.

The inclusion-minimal uniformly sufficient F are therefore exactly the complements of spanning forests of G. Indeed, deleting any member of F adds that edge to its complementary forest. Minimality says each such addition creates a cycle, which is precisely maximality of the forest inside each connected component. With n vertices, m edges and c connected components, all these minimal reading sets have m−n+c members. In four-regular ice this is n+c. There are generally several such sets, giving a concrete instance of VC84's alternative sufficient supports. The bound concerns fixed selections of individual edge readings, without an adaptive policy or a different measurement interface.

This also gives a constructive decoder on its admitted inputs. Choose the complementary spanning forest. At a leaf, all incident directions except its one remaining forest edge are known. Its prescribed degree forces that direction. Pass that direction to the other endpoint of the same edge, delete the leaf edge and continue. The finite forest guarantees termination. Existence follows here from the premise that the readings came from an admitted orientation. Arbitrary assigned pin bits can instead demand an impossible degree and must be rejected. Actual shared-edge incidence, leaf removal and that admissibility condition are the relations a static implementation must preserve.

For this spanning choice, the final root of each component supplies no independent balance constraint. Sum outdegree minus indegree over that whole component: each retained edge contributes +1 at its tail and −1 at its head, so the sum is zero. If every nonroot vertex is balanced, its root is balanced too. Thus n−c vertex balances force the last c, provided every edge really has its two stated incidences. A duplicated or missing endpoint invalidates the cancellation. If the unmeasured forest instead has several trees within one component, its remaining roots can have compensating nonzero balances and still need checking. This is conservation inside the specified graph, without an exterior to absorb a missing incidence.

Instance and uniform requirements differ. On two vertices joined by four addressed parallel edges, a balanced orientation has two edges in each direction. Measuring the two that point from the first vertex to the second forces the remaining pair. Its residual directed graph is acyclic, although its residual undirected graph has a two-edge cycle. The same two selected addresses fail uniformly: when their measured directions oppose, the remaining pair has two possible orientations. Any three addresses suffice uniformly, in agreement with m−n+c=3. This is a worked boundary of the general proof, not its basis.

The injection into m−n+c recorded bits gives |Ωα(G)|≤2^(m−n+c). For four-regular G and any probability law on its admitted configurations, Shannon configuration entropy obeys H≤log₂|Ω(G)|≤n+c bits. The bit bound is not an exact count: not every pin assignment need extend to an orientation. It supplies neither a probability law nor a thermodynamic identification. For a fixed cycle C, reverse it when it is directed and leave the configuration unchanged otherwise. This update is an involution on Ωα(G), so it preserves the Shannon entropy of a transported distribution on complete configurations. Conditioning on an observed event is a different operation. The local ice constraint alone supplies no preferred direction of time or entropy increase.

`IceIncidence.swift` implements the addressed leaf step. A port is `(vertex, slot)`, and a directed edge is `(tail port, head port)`. `EmptyIcePorts` starts at slot zero. `InAtIcePort` and `OutAtIcePort` require a reading from the same source and context whose actual head or tail matches the next port. Each constructor advances that slot and extends the existing In/Out word. A repeated slot or another vertex cannot substitute for the required incidence. The finite constructor grammar supplies this interpretation. An arbitrary `KnownIcePorts` or `PredicateReading` conformance does not establish it by name.

`IceLeafIn` and `IceLeafOut` read the remaining reference edge from that same source, require its local end at slot three, and derive its directed endpoint pair from the counts of the three known incidences. Completing the word derives a `Survivor`, without a supplied final direction. The inferred reading can enter the opposite constructor at the remote vertex, where its remote slot must also match. The original reference and source remain retained. Eliminating the unknown direction does not require deleting the edge from the whole. The output answers the declared completion question. Relating it to an unmeasured physical direction requires that source to satisfy the declared graph constraints. This layout places a nonroot vertex's parent edge at slot three, so a certificate for another forest must bind its actual port layout.

`Checks/IceIncidence.swift` checks both generic branches, transfer to the actual neighboring port, and two successive inferences inside one larger source using `SequentialReading`. It also closes one complete four-regular component: K5 with four leaves around a center. Six measured leaf-to-leaf edges each appear once in the source and are read at both endpoints. The four remaining star edges are inferred. Their same inferred readings supply the center's four ports and derive its two-in/two-out rule without another measurement. The catalogue below reconstructs the declared source from those same readings, replacing a separate hand-written reconstruction check. All five vertices reach four inspected ports. The incidence assignment therefore uses every one of this graph's twenty ports. This establishes the given completion, not the set of all orientations of K5 or a Swift proof for every graph.

**Static incidence coverage.** `CandidateRemoval` extends the existing SelectCandidateLeaf/Left/Right paths with their remaining support and address. The leaf case returns Never as the remainder. Each branch case preserves the fork and the untouched sibling while using its child's remainder. Induction on the finite path proves that exactly the selected occurrence is removed. The address records left/right choices and ignores payloads, so removing a sibling does not rename the surviving occurrence. `EmptyCandidateSupport` recognizes no remaining leaf in the finite Never/CandidateFork grammar, including padded empty forks. It does not require all such empty expressions to equal Never.

`AllocateIceVertex` uses this removal path to allocate the four different slots of its selected vertex address. Its state retains the original vertex declaration beside the remaining vertices and allocated ports. Start with the whole declaration and an empty port inventory, compose valid steps through `SequentialReading`, and require no vertex to remain. Every original leaf position is then used once: a removed leaf cannot be selected from the subsequent remainder. Different positions have different path addresses even when their payloads are identical. Each contributes four different slot addresses, so the final port inventory has no duplicate port. `AllocatedIcePorts` checks the initial and final boundaries of this construction. IdentityReading supplies the empty sequence when the declaration has no vertices.

These addresses belong to the retained declaration. They are not absolute names for physical objects, and equal path words in unrelated declarations do not establish physical identity. The certificate retains the declaration and binds the allocated ports to the actual incidence source and context being checked.

`ConsumeIceEdge` removes one actual edge application from a declared catalogue, then removes its actual tail and head from the port inventory in that order. The second port selection must start from the first remainder. Its output retains the same reading source and supplies both reduced inventories to the next step. `ExhaustedIceIncidence` binds the initial ports to the completed allocation, matches their contexts, and requires both final inventories to contain no leaf. Within the finite allocation, removal, identity and sequential-composition grammar, this proves that each catalogued edge occurs once and every allocated port occurs at exactly one edge end. No edge end can be spent twice, no vertex can be silently omitted, and equal vertex payloads cannot merge their addresses. With n allocated vertices, the catalogue therefore has 2n edges. This certifies a closed four-regular directed multigraph at its ports. Balance and absence of loops are additional properties of the declared graph.

What is consumed here is a pending incidence obligation. The same retained edge reading may still support both endpoint proofs or another question. Discharging its catalogue occurrence prevents counting one check twice as coverage of two edges. It does not erase information or impose a restriction on ordinary reuse of established knowledge.

`Checks/IceCoverage.swift` applies that same construction to K5. Its five vertex payloads are all Never, while its five selected addresses differ. Those addresses supply the vertex names in the actual pin and reference data. Ten consumption steps exhaust twenty ports and ten applications. This covers the supplied catalogue; identifying it with the original graph requires one further relation.

The same check file gives a positive boundary for that last obligation. One retained source contains the edges p₀→p₁ and p₂→p₃ at four ports of one vertex. `RegroupDistanceLegs` supplies the actual rewiring p₀→p₃ and p₂→p₁. Both edge readings keep that original source, and the rewired catalogue exhausts the same four allocated ports. Yet its endpoint pairs differ from the source pairs. Thus retained-source equality and complete incidence consumption do not establish the original connectivity. This example uses the loops admitted by the general coverage construction. It does not contradict the loopless forest theorem. It identifies the missing relation: each claimed edge must retain its own source reference, not just contribute the right total port inventory.

**Reference binding.** `IceCatalogueEdge` retains both the direction reading R and its original reference reading M. An actual finite selection path must choose from M's endpoint pair or its reverse, and the selected endpoints must equal R's endpoints in order. Both readings use the same source and context. This admits reversing one edge and rejects either attempted orientation of the rewiring above. `IceLeafIn` and `IceLeafOut` derive their own catalogue leaves using the very M from which their directions were inferred. `MeasuredIceEdge` retains the measured reading unchanged.

`JoinIceCatalogue` assembles three parallel trees: original reference fields, resulting direction fields and actual reading occurrences. Its ReferenceEdges projection keeps a support tree of the original endpoint pairs alone. All children share one source and context. Induction on the finite leaf/join grammar gives a position-preserving correspondence between these representations. `SourceIceCatalogue` then requires the source to equal the pair of the reconstructed pin and unmeasured-reference branches. Within the pin branch, its direction tree must also equal its reference tree. Thus every declared field is covered in place and every measured direction is retained. The interpretation of those two branches is part of the declared source encoding; it is not recovered from an unknown untyped object.

`DeclaredIceIncidence` binds the consumed edge inventory to this very catalogue, with the same source and context, while retaining the previous vertex allocation and port-exhaustion requirements. Consequently each original edge occurrence has exactly one catalogue reading, that reading retains its two original ports, and those ports are consumed once. No separate catalogue list or source-reconstruction assertion is needed for K5: both are projections of its constructed catalogue. Binding the original graph and consuming its incidences are distinct obligations, now connected by actual types.

**A residual forest from endpoint removal.** A second finite certificate checks the undirected forest required by M68. `RemoveIceForestEndpoint` removes one original reference-pair occurrence and one of its endpoint vertex positions. Its other endpoint must still be selectable from the vertex remainder. Choosing the endpoint to remove may reverse the pair for this purpose. The certificate never reads the inferred direction or the measured pin values. Every subsequent step receives both remainders. `ResidualIceForest` binds the initial edges to the unmeasured catalogue and the initial vertices to the original vertex declaration, and requires no edge to remain. Vertices left over are allowed.

This dependency restriction matters for the uniform claim. The forest sequence starts from the unmeasured catalogue's ReferenceEdges and the original vertex positions, with a fixed context. Its type contains no pin values or direction readers. If another catalogue has the same reference support and context, the identical sequence remains a ResidualIceForest for it, even if its whole source differs. `IceForestDependsOnlyOnReferenceTopology` checks this generic equality. Geometry therefore supplies a reusable certificate across new measurements; balance of the newly assigned directions still needs its own witness.

Why does completion prove a forest? Number the steps in their supplied order. A removed vertex cannot be selected again because removal preserves surviving addresses and never restores leaves. If another still-pending edge touched that vertex, the step eventually consuming that edge would fail to select one of its endpoints. Therefore, in every completed sequence, the removed vertex had exactly its selected edge among the then-pending edges: it was a leaf. Loops fail immediately because the required surviving endpoint would be the vertex just removed. A cycle, including a pair of parallel edges, cannot be fully consumed: the first removed vertex on that cycle would leave another cycle edge without a selectable endpoint. This proves acyclicity from coverage of the actual edge and vertex positions, without a cycle detector or a supplied acyclicity marker.

Conversely, every finite forest whose endpoints belong to the declared vertex positions has such a sequence. If it has an edge, choose a leaf, remove its incident edge and that leaf, and apply induction to the smaller forest. Both endpoint selections exist at the step. Isolated vertices remain untouched, and the empty case is an identity reading. Thus existence of a complete finite certificate is equivalent to being a forest on the declared positions. With k residual edges it removes k vertices and leaves n−k roots, one per residual tree including isolated vertices. This does not assert one root per component of the original full graph: measured edges can join different residual trees.

A single removal prefix need not have removed a leaf. The checks deliberately remove K5's central root first: that prefix is valid, but its next incident edge cannot be consumed. The property follows from complete discharge of all declared obligations. This is why `RemoveIceForestEndpoint` does not advertise local knowledge of the absent edges. The valid four-step K5 certificate instead removes its four leaves and retains exactly the original center position. The empty graph also checks through identity composition.

**Balance in the same catalogue.** Local balance by itself still leaves a compatibility gap. Reverse all four directed readings used at K5's vertex A. The resulting finite word has two incoming and two outgoing ports and retains the same whole source, but disagrees with the global catalogue. This positive counterconstruction is rejected when its readings are bound to that catalogue.

`CheckIceVertex` takes the actual four-port KnownIcePorts word and four selection paths from the global catalogue. The word's retained readings must be exactly those four selected applications in slot order. Its vertex address must equal the position removed from the original vertex declaration, and its counts must both be Two. Each step removes only that pending vertex obligation. `BalancedIceCatalogue` requires that the sequence starts from all declared vertices and leaves none. Induction through the finite local-port and vertex-removal constructors then proves balance at every vertex using the same directed readings whose incidences were exhausted. The same edge reading remains reusable at its two ends; membership paths here do not consume it a second time.

Together, the three certificates on matching declarations establish a completion and its uniqueness. `DeclaredIceIncidence` supplies exactly the original graph and its pins with complete shared-edge incidence. `BalancedIceCatalogue` makes that same orientation satisfy every local ice rule, establishing existence for those pins. `ResidualIceForest` supplies the undirected forest premise of M68, which excludes another balanced completion on that fixed graph. K5's `K5StaticCompletionCertificate` combines these constructions. Its former manual source-reconstruction and aggregate vertex-count checks are subsumed by the generic catalogue and coverage relations. The empty graph uses identity composition in all three certificates.

This separates two uses of a certificate. The forest property gives a uniform at-most-one result for every pin assignment on this fixed graph, while an actual complete balance certificate establishes that the supplied assignment is admitted. No probability law or search over configurations is needed for either implication. The compiler checks the supplied finite constructions; the induction and M68 explain why those relations prove the claims. Producing such constructions for a new representation and identifying that representation with a physical or learning system remain separate tasks. Arbitrary open conformances to CandidateRemoval, IceCatalogue, KnownIcePorts or PredicateReading do not acquire the finite constructor grammar's meaning.

### M69

**M69 (finite witnesses without assuming a finite whole).** The cycle argument uses the finite support of a difference, not necessarily a finite graph. To separate those conditions, let G now be a finite or countable, locally finite, loopless graph with prescribed finite outdegrees α(v). Fix an admitted orientation x and measured edges F. This is an explicit extension of the mathematical domain: it does not assume that physical reality is an actually infinite graph. A double ray means a simple path extending infinitely in both directions.

If y differs from x on only finitely many edges, its difference D is finite and balanced at every vertex, exactly as in M68. It therefore decomposes into finitely many directed cycles. Conversely, finitely many finite cycle reversals change only finitely many edges. Thus the finite-cycle operations connect exactly the finite-difference classes of admitted orientations. Within x's class, its readings identify x precisely when no unmeasured directed cycle remains. Invariance under those operations likewise identifies a question only within each such class. It does not equate answers in different classes.

For unrestricted differences the exact instance criterion is stronger:

```
x is the only admitted orientation agreeing on F
    iff its unmeasured directed graph contains
        neither a finite directed cycle nor a directed double ray.
```

Reversing either obstruction preserves every prescribed outdegree and every pin. A double ray contributes one entering and one leaving edge at each of its vertices, so its complete reversal is another admitted configuration, whether or not any finite operation produces it. Conversely, a distinct y gives a nonempty balanced directed difference D. Local finiteness makes the indegree and outdegree comparison an equality of finite integers. If D has a finite directed cycle, that is an obstruction. Otherwise, start from one of its edges and follow outgoing edges forward and incoming edges backward. Balance prevents either extension from stopping. A repeated vertex, including a meeting of the two extensions, would form a directed cycle. The extensions therefore give a directed double ray. This proves the criterion without needing an infinite decomposition or a finite procedure connecting x to y.

On the integer line, with edges between n and n+1 and outdegree one at every vertex, orient every edge toward increasing n, or every edge toward decreasing n. These are the only two orientations: local balance equates the directions of consecutive edges. Both satisfy every vertex condition, neither admits a nontrivial finite change, and neither contains a finite cycle. The unmeasured graph with F empty is a forest, yet the complete answer has two alternatives. One measured edge distinguishes these two orientations. This identifies the failed quantifier in a direct infinite extension of M68, without claiming that an infinite rearrangement is a realizable physical operation.

For the uniform balanced question, assume every degree of G is even and α(v)=degree(v)/2. Then

```
F identifies every balanced orientation of G
    iff H = (V(G), E(G)\F) contains neither a cycle nor a double ray.
```

Sufficiency follows from the instance criterion. For necessity, let C be an unmeasured undirected cycle or double ray and orient it consistently. Removing C leaves finite even degree at every vertex. Pair the remaining edge incidences at each vertex. Following these pairings partitions the remaining edges into closed trails or trails infinite in both directions: the auxiliary graph whose vertices are edges and whose links are the paired incidences has degree two. Orient each such trail consistently. Every paired incidence contributes one arrival and one departure, so the resulting orientation is balanced. Together with the orientation of C it gives x. Reversing C gives y with the same pins. Thus both obstructions can occur in the balanced family. Finite cycles and double rays are also the circuits of the algebraic cycle matroid for locally finite graphs, as described by [Bruhn and Diestel, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/1011.4749v3#S3). The directed identification argument here is given explicitly rather than inferred from that name.

For a question about one unmeasured edge e, requiring that whole residual graph to be free of obstructions is unnecessary. The exact uniform criterion is

```
F determines the direction of e in every balanced orientation
    iff e is a bridge of H and at least one of its two sides in H−e is finite.
```

Here each side is the component containing one of e's endpoints. If e is not a bridge, an alternative finite path joins its endpoints, giving a cycle through e. If it is a bridge and both sides are infinite, each locally finite connected side has a ray from its endpoint. One constructs that ray by repeatedly deleting its current vertex and choosing an infinite component accessible through a neighboring vertex, which exists because there are finitely many neighbors. The two rays and e give a double ray through e. In either case, the preceding pairing construction extends a consistent orientation of that obstruction to a balanced orientation of G. Its reversal changes e and preserves F, proving necessity.

For sufficiency, let S be a finite side after deleting the bridge. S may contain unknown cycles. Their orientations can remain unresolved, as can unrelated parts of H. Only the complete crossing incidence of S matters for the requested answer.

Only e crosses from S to its complement in H. All other edges crossing that same cut in the full graph G belong to F. There are finitely many of them because S is finite and G is locally finite. Sum outdegree minus indegree over S. Every internal edge cancels once at each end, leaving only crossing edges. For balanced vertices this gives

```
s(e) = − Σ s(f),  f ∈ δG(S) ∩ F,
```

where s is +1 for a crossing edge directed out of S and −1 for one directed into S. Thus finitely many measured crossing directions determine e. An admitted assignment makes the right side either +1 or −1. Another value rejects that proposed completion. The finite witness must cover S's actual incidences and crossing edges. Merely presenting a sum of selected directions does not establish that coverage.

As a corollary, if H contains neither a cycle nor a double ray, every unmeasured edge has such a finite side and therefore a finite witness. This also explains why full leaf reconstruction is stronger than the one-question requirement. A finite side with unknown internal cycles can determine its one crossing edge by cancellation even though its internal orientations are not individually determined.

This is a positive replacement for assuming a finite whole. The graph criterion allows each question about an edge to have a finite internal certificate, while the family of such questions and their certificate sizes can remain unbounded. A finite set of requested edges uses the union of their finitely many witnesses. This does not assert a uniform bound for all requests or a procedure for finding a witness in an unspecified graph representation. A finite general proof can also cover an unbounded family through induction, without listing every represented edge. EF1 and EF6 distinguish these scopes already.

The cut δG(S) is internal to the same whole. Its other endpoints and measured contacts are included in G, not assigned to an exterior reservoir. Local balance, the declared difference class and completeness of the crossing incidence are the assumptions doing the work. The Cut's exclusion of an outside does not have to settle the size of the whole for this finite witness to be valid. Its Step 5 asks whether the relations of support close internally. M69 asks which orientations remain possible on a specified incidence graph. A cycle of support and a cycle that permits changing a configuration answer different questions. The book already requires joint compatibility after returning dependence, so this orientation example supplies no shortcut from support closure to a unique state. The existing Swift incidence constructors check complete finite declarations; M69 does not silently turn them into a representation of an infinite graph or a general finite-cut certificate. The missing implementation step is actual coverage of a finite region and its internal boundary, rather than a new marker asserting that its flux cancels.

## Detail: The Higgs mechanism

**Radial minimum and orbit calculation.** Consider a complex scalar doublet φ=(a,b) and the classical potential V(φ)=−m²x+λx², where x=φ†φ≥0, m²>0 and λ>0. This explicitly chosen quartic convention describes the usual tree-level symmetry-breaking potential. The [PDG electroweak review, §10.1](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2024/reviews/rpp2024-rev-standard-model.pdf) gives the doublet and vacuum convention, with a different normalization of the quartic coupling. Write x₀=m²/(2λ). Completing the square gives V(x)−V(x₀)=λ(x−x₀)². Hence the minimizing norm squared is uniquely x₀. The minimizing doublets form |a|²+|b|²=x₀, a three-real-dimensional sphere, rather than three candidate fields.

The corresponding orbit statement is also explicit. For a nonzero doublet of norm r, set

```
U(a,b) = (1/r) [[conj(b), a], [-conj(a), b]].
```

Its columns are orthonormal and its determinant is (|a|²+|b|²)/r²=1, so U lies in SU(2). It sends (0,r) to (a,b). Thus every pair of doublets with the same nonzero norm belongs to the same SU(2) orbit, and norm distinguishes these orbits. For this pointwise doublet problem the minimum is exactly one orbit. This establishes more than constancy of a chosen readout: it establishes completeness of that readout for the specified equivalence relation.

This does not identify an arbitrary full gauge-field configuration from its scalar norm. Derivatives, gauge fields, other fields and boundary conditions belong to the larger question. A point-dependent transformation must act on the gauge fields too. In the usual representative φ₀=(0,v/√2), the norm is v/√2 and x₀=v²/2, not |φ₀|=v under that convention. The standard model's three broken gauge directions are not three additional physical massless scalar particles. Higgs.swift supplies a three-successor expression, without deriving the group action or the particle spectrum from it.

The positive VI link is now specific: the potential fixes one scalar norm, and the explicit action proves that this norm identifies one pointwise doublet orbit. Neither result derives all physical fields, a mass mechanism from comparison mismatch, or permanent retention of a vacuum history. HiggsGaugeInvariant's interface conformance alone supplies none of the potential or action used in these calculations. A VI implementation needs those actual relations, as U2b needs the scheduler's actual constraints rather than a chosen singleton marker.

The two calculations sharpen the role of the target. A branch update can determine a new state without recovering its input. An invariant can identify an equivalence class when it is also complete for the declared equivalence: x and y are equivalent exactly when their invariant values agree. Constancy on each class gives only one direction of this statement. The explicit SU(2) matrix supplies the converse here. Thus identification of an orbit has an exact mathematical basis, while a coarse label that happens to agree cannot claim that basis. These are standard conditional calculations used to specify VI's correspondence, not new physical laws derived from its interface names.

## Detail: Discrete self-similarity in gravitational collapse

The original [Choptuik study](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.70.9) supplies a specific threshold comparison: numerical solutions of spherical massless-scalar collapse disperse or form a black hole as an initial-data parameter crosses p*. Near that threshold, black-hole masses exhibit power-law scaling with an exponent about 0.37. These are numerical results within a continuum model, not measurements of Planck-scale collapse. Repeated structure across separately evolved preparations is a stronger comparison than rescaling one image. Its domain, departures and numerical convergence matter. A shared VI description must retain those relations before claiming that the same pattern governs every scale or every matter model.

[Ecker, Ecker and Grumiller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14358) construct an infinite family of analytic discretely self-similar solutions in a large-D expansion and compare features with numerical finite-D critical solutions. That result makes repeated structure an interesting candidate for an operational reading. It does not by itself supply VI's finite carrier, monotone candidate elimination or a unique decoded answer. Nor is adding perturbative terms a proof of physical append-only memory. A VI mapping would need a declared finite approximation and a checked bridge to the physical question before any finite saturation result transfers.

**Stretching does not require a vortex or a loss of distinction.** For a smooth local velocity field u, an infinitesimal material separation obeys ξ̇=Lξ, where L=∇u. The symmetric part of L changes squared separation, since d|ξ|²/dt=2ξᵀsym(L)ξ. Its antisymmetric part contributes zero to this reading. Take u(x,y,z)=k(2x,−y,−z), with k>0. Its divergence and curl vanish, while its flow is

```
(x,y,z) ↦ (e^(2kt)x, e^(−kt)y, e^(−kt)z).
```

The flow stretches one direction and compresses two, has determinant one, and has an explicit inverse for every finite t. This is a local kinematic counterexample, not a finite-energy whole-fluid realization. It excludes inferring a vortex, a singularity or merged sources from an elongated shape alone. Inverse conditioning can worsen while exact recovery remains possible, as IiS10 already distinguishes from recovery at finite precision.

There is a precise gravitational comparison. For neutral test bodies in radial free fall in the uncharged, nonrotating black-hole model, [Crispino et al., §4, q=0](https://arxiv.org/html/1602.07232) obtain the geodesic-deviation relation ξ̈=(GM/r³)diag(2,−1,−1)ξ for infinitesimal separation in a freely falling orthonormal frame. The shared question is how neighboring trajectories separate. This is an acceleration relation, while L above specifies a velocity gradient. Identifying the two operators would omit one derivative and their different units. The black hole in this comparison has no spin, so rotation is not required for its tidal stretching.

At the Schwarzschild radius r_s=2GM/c², the coefficient GM/r_s³=c⁶/(8G²M²) decreases as mass increases. Thus the horizon is not a universal threshold of local tidal strength. Space's resolution discussion separates this causal boundary from local stretching and from the breakdown of a chosen continuum model. A common pattern of directional changes identifies a candidate relation to compare, not a common physical mechanism by itself.

## Detail: Finite energy, concentration and the class of continuations

A bound on one reading need not control another. Fix a nonzero smooth compactly supported divergence-free vector field v on ℝ³. Such a v can be constructed as (∂₂ψ,−∂₁ψ,0) for a smooth compactly supported ψ with a nonzero derivative in those directions. For ε>0 define

```
uε(x) = ε^(−3/2) v(x/ε).
```

Every uε is smooth and divergence-free. Substituting y=x/ε in the integral gives ∫|uε|² dx=∫|v|² dy, while sup|uε|=ε^(−3/2)sup|v| and ∫|∇uε|² dx=ε⁻²∫|∇v|² dy. Thus equal finite integral energy permits unbounded pointwise size and gradient size across this family. This is a family of fields, not a demonstrated Navier–Stokes trajectory or a singular physical fluid. Setting ε=ε(t) would still require the actual equation and its force regularity. The elementary scaling calculation does not solve that problem.

The same construction exposes content omitted by a limit description. For every fixed smooth compactly supported vector probe φ, substitution gives ∫uε·φ dx=ε^(3/2)∫v(y)·φ(εy)dy→0. The zero field family has these same limiting answers. But for a fixed smooth compactly supported scalar probe χ, ∫|uε|²χ dx→Eχ(0), where E=∫|v|²>0. Thus the velocity fields have weak limit zero while their energy measures tend to Eδ₀, concentrated at the origin. Applying the quadratic energy reading to the weak limit would give zero and discard this content. Each field's full probe readings can distinguish it. Taking the limits of fixed probes has omitted information needed by the nonlinear question. Retaining the energy measure repairs that specific omission, without recovering the entire profile or establishing a physical loss of energy. There is still no proposed physical time evolution in this calculation.

For a quantitative bound, the missing relation is control: an inequality of the form r(w)≤F(E(w),c(w)) in the relevant domain, where c contains the additional supported readings and F is bounded on the admitted inputs. An invariant E alone supplies no such inequality. S24 distinguishes conservation from other representation properties. S26 similarly exhibits duration omitted by a light-incidence reading. Neither conserved energy nor historical retention can be used as a complete description without establishing the questions it supports.

The [official Navier–Stokes problem statement](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/navierstokes.pdf) includes forced breakdown alternatives C and D. Smoothness and decay requirements apply to the prescribed force, including through the proposed singular time. This force is external to the fluid subsystem in that mathematical description. It need not be external to reality. An interpretation as a closed physical experiment would also need the internal source of the force and its coupling. Mere inclusion of an arbitrary prescribed function as a parameter does not construct that source.

This was a reading of theorem statements and selected construction and comparison steps. The full Lean build and independent Comparator check were not run here. The mathematical result is not added as a VI axiom or presented as an independent validation of the physical interpretation.
