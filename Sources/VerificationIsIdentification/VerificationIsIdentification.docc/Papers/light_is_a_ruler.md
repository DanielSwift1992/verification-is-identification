# Light Is a Ruler (Measure)

A light-based ruler compares emission, propagation and reception with an internal reference. A geometry summarizes specified path relations. Applying that geometry to a new contact requires retaining what the contact can distinguish, including the response at a join. The path, clock and material models below state those obligations. A written unit fixes the meaning of a result, while an actual instrument supplies evidence that the result applies.

## §0. Premises

Seeing Is Encoding separates a declared reading from its physical realization. Program Is Path supplies path composition. The metric below additionally requires undirected edges and positive weights. These assumptions are explicit rather than consequences of the existence of a symbolic inverse.

## §1. A unit and its realization

**LiR1 (A type can name a unit).** A shared type can keep the same unit declaration across expressions. This establishes consistency of notation. It does not make all realizations of that unit identical, supply a clock trajectory or remove the need to retain a calibration record. `StandardIsType` names a Tick and inherits a presented-law interface. `SharedDeclaredTick` compares two such interfaces, requiring their actual Tick fields and the returned unit to be one type. This checks notation, without establishing a physical standard or calibration.

## §2. Counting paths

**LiR2 (Path counts compose).** Give each edge a nonnegative integer weight. Define a path's count as the sum of its edge weights. The empty path has count zero, and concatenating compatible paths adds their counts. Both statements follow from the definition of a finite sum. This is an additive map on the path category. All paths together need not form a monoid, since arbitrary endpoints need not compose.

## §3. A graph metric

**LiR3 (Positive undirected path lengths give a metric).** In a connected undirected graph with positive integer edge weights, define d(x,y) as the minimum path count. A path exists by connectedness, and a minimum exists because its counts form a nonempty subset of the nonnegative integers. The empty path gives d(x,x)=0. Any path between distinct vertices contains a positive edge, so its count is positive. Reversal preserves count by the undirected weight assumption. Concatenating minimizing paths gives d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z). These prove the metric axioms.

Dropping those premises changes the result. Zero-weight edges can identify distinct vertices at distance zero. Directed edges need not give symmetry. Disconnected vertices have no finite distance by this definition. Optical reversibility is not supplied by an arbitrary symbolic rule's inverse, as Seeing Is Encoding SiE6 explains. A graph metric becomes a physical ranging model only after its paths and weights are related to the apparatus.

**LiR4 (A shared law is a modeling constraint).** Reusing the same edge rule gives the same symbolic rule at every application. That equality neither derives the speed of light nor establishes that physical propagation is independent of location, medium or time. Such an invariance must enter through the physical model and its evidence.

## §4. Modular readings

**LiR5 (Quotient and remainder identify an integer).** For integer length n≥0 and integer unit U>0, Euclidean division gives unique q,r with n=qU+r and 0≤r<U. The pair identifies n, whereas the remainder alone leaves lengths separated by U indistinguishable. A finite phase grid can use that remainder as its coordinate. Its relation to physical phase requires wavelength and propagation assumptions. Time-of-flight and interferometry require their own response laws and uncertainty bounds, not just these integer names.

## §5. Direction and return paths

**LiR6 (A second reading can separate a directional pair).** In the finite example, the matte reading keeps the declared total and the mirror reading keeps the two directional slots. The supplied east and west inputs have equal totals and different slots. This proves a pair-specific separation for these maps. It does not make every physical diffuse surface direction-blind or every mirror a complete directional detector.

Under a supplied reciprocal return-path model, an outward path and its reverse have total count twice the outward count. The example checks the corresponding doubling relation. Recovering a physical distance from return time additionally requires the propagation and clock relations. Including an observer's node in a path makes the modeled dependence explicit, without by itself deriving how a real glint moves.

## §6. Calibration

**LiR7 (Agreement of descriptions and realizations are different questions).** Two written laws can be compared under a declared mathematical relation. That comparison supplies no measurement of how either device realizes its law. Calibration connects the device, the reference and their records with an uncertainty model. All three may belong to one closed whole. A type's stable spelling does not make the implementing material immune to change.

**A defining number and an invariant relation have different jobs.** The [SI definition of the metre](https://www.bipm.org/en/si-base-units/metre) fixes the numerical value of the vacuum speed of light in metres per second. This defines the unit. It does not measure every possible light propagation or establish agreement between all physical realizations. The [SI defining constants](https://www.bipm.org/en/measurement-units/si-defining-constants) also include h and k. Their exact SI numbers do not make the corresponding physical relations mere conventions.

For example, let Δτ be one clock's elapsed reading between emission and return. Assign a radar coordinate d=c₀Δτ/2. Calculating 2d/Δτ then returns c₀ identically. No second propagation fact has been tested. Interpreting d as an independently realized spatial distance needs the path, motion and clock assumptions of LiR6. An independent comparison of returns, resonances or transported clocks can still disagree with the proposed propagation model. The definitional equality cannot settle that comparison.

**Constancy can be asked without choosing its numerical value.** Suppose two positive readings aₓ,bₓ belong to one comparison context x, and aᵧ,bᵧ to another y. Once multiplication and cancellation of these readings are justified, the same relative answer means

    aₓ bᵧ = bₓ aᵧ.

Rescaling both readings at x by any λₓ>0 and both at y by any λᵧ>0 multiplies both sides by λₓλᵧ. Thus agreement or disagreement survives these changes of unit. No outside clock is required. Separate changes of the two compared processes need not cancel. A positive common factor remains invisible to this comparison, so a stable ratio alone cannot prove each process individually unchanged. LiR8 identifies when additive readings and changes of reference are available in the first place. Arbitrary nonlinear relabeling is not already such a unit change.

For two return signals timed by the same clock, this comparison retains their relative delays before either is called a speed. Inferring a speed ratio additionally requires compatible path and propagation conditions. For resonators, a change in frequency comparison can involve material length, refractive response or propagation. [Nagel et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1008.1205v2) compare rotated vacuum and sapphire resonators. The instrument supplies a frequency comparison, and the physical interpretation must retain the apparatus response. An invariant numeral alone cannot isolate which relation changed. Likewise a dimensionless coupling such as α cannot be removed by changing units, but extracting it from observations still needs a response model. The [CODATA adjustment](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/pdf/JPCRD2022CODATA.pdf) retains those theoretical and experimental dependencies.

**A mass-dependent phase needs a relative clock reading.** In a fixed quantum comparison, replacing a time-independent Hamiltonian H by H+C I gives U′(t)=e^(−iCt/ħ)U(t). Hence U′ρU′†=UρU† for every input. A common energy offset cannot change any fixed effect's probability. In particular, the number mc²/ħ alone does not count distinguishable steps of a process. A stationary energy eigenstate retains its density operator while its vector acquires phase.

Mass can enter a relative comparison. In the semiclassical matter-wave model of [Loriani et al., Eqs. (1)–(3)](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.09102), a branch accumulates phase −mc²τ/ħ plus its electromagnetic interaction action divided by ħ. Comparing branches retains their proper-time difference and the preparation and laser contributions. The common-offset cancellation cannot remove a branch-dependent relation. Conversely, omitting those contacts cannot establish a clock from a single phase factor. This is IiS10's common-versus-relative phase distinction applied to a supplied physical action.

For a pure preparation with finite positive mean excitation under a time-independent Hamiltonian with a discrete spectrum bounded below, [Margolus and Levitin, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9710043) give the orthogonalization bound τ⊥≥πħ/[2(⟨H⟩−E₀)]. It constrains a specified transition to a perfectly distinguishable state. It does not make rest mass an attained ticking rate or make every computational step an orthogonal transition. The bound is invariant under the common offset above, since ⟨H+C I⟩−(E₀+C)=⟨H⟩−E₀. A physical use needs the generator, state preparation and definition of the distinguished event.

**A closed comparison exposes what unit choices cannot remove.** Take a connected finite comparison graph. Each directed edge i→j carries a positive scalar conversion rᵢⱼ, with reciprocal reverse. Under independent positive rescalings of the node readings, an edge becomes r′ᵢⱼ=(λⱼ/λᵢ)rᵢⱼ. Along a closed path every λ cancels. Its product H is therefore unchanged.

All edges can be made 1 by such rescalings exactly when every closed-path product is 1. Necessity follows from cancellation. For sufficiency, choose a spanning tree and a root. Recursively set λⱼ=λᵢ/rᵢⱼ along its outward edges. Every tree edge becomes 1. Each remaining edge completes a loop with the tree, so its transformed value must also be 1. Equivalently, the original edges admit single positive node values sᵢ with rᵢⱼ=sⱼ/sᵢ exactly under this loop condition. One root scale remains free. This classification permits independent node units. Two frequencies expressed in one retained common time unit cannot be independently rescaled while preserving that unit relation. Normalizing separate conversion labels does not erase a measured frequency ratio. Its content must remain in the relation between the chosen units.

For three retained comparisons r_AB=2, r_BC=3, r_AC=5, the loop A→B→C→A has product 6/5. No rescaling can turn these into ratios of one fixed value per node: the first two force r_AC=6. The obstruction concerns their joint interpretation. Path dependence, evolution or an inaccurate record may require a richer description. Reality has not become inconsistent. With uncertain measurements, refusal requires that no jointly admissible assignment of edge values closes every loop. Independently measured clock comparisons already support this kind of test: [McGrew et al.](https://www.nist.gov/publications/towards-optical-second-verifying-optical-clocks-si-limit) compare direct frequency ratios with ratios obtained through cesium references. Multiplying ratios computed from the same assigned node values would instead close by construction.

The edge labels above are multiplicative conversion factors. The same numerals used as lengths ask another question. Euclidean distances AB=2, BC=3, AC=5 describe B between A and C, with no contradiction. Distances 3,4,5 describe a right triangle, since 3²+4²=5². Neither interpretation follows from the three numerals alone. Path concatenation adds path lengths, while a direct endpoint distance can be shorter. Treating every relation as scalar multiplication would impose the very composition law the physical inquiry must establish. Space §5a separates reconstruction of composition from reconstruction of a metric.

**A returning form need not make every loop the identity.** The scalar argument has a more general relational version. On a connected finite graph, let each role i have a set Xᵢ of admitted forms, and let each edge supply a bijection Tᵢⱼ:Xᵢ→Xⱼ with inverse on the reverse edge. Seek one assignment satisfying xⱼ=Tᵢⱼ(xᵢ) on every edge. Choose an internal root r and a spanning tree. Its path maps Pᵢ:Xᵣ→Xᵢ have Pᵣ=id. Each edge then supplies a return map

    Lᵢⱼ = Pⱼ⁻¹ ∘ Tᵢⱼ ∘ Pᵢ : Xᵣ → Xᵣ.

The consistent assignments correspond exactly to the common fixed forms F=⋂ Fix(Lᵢⱼ). Indeed every assignment has xᵢ=Pᵢ(xᵣ), so its edge equations require xᵣ∈F. Conversely any xᵣ∈F gives a full assignment through those tree paths. Tree-edge return maps are identities, so only the remaining edges need testing. Thus F=∅ refuses a common assignment, and a singleton F determines it in these addressed domains. An additional root preparation restriction D⊆Xᵣ leaves exactly D∩F. A different tree changes the presentation, not the assignments. Changing the coordinates at each role conjugates the root return maps and transports F bijectively, together with any such restriction. No metric or multiplication of scalar readings entered this proof.

When D∩F is nonempty, a specified question about the assembled assignment has one determined answer exactly when its reading agrees for every root form in D∩F. Recovering the entire assignment is unnecessary for that answer. An empty set instead refuses the proposed description and supplies no realized answer. This is S9's factorization question applied after joint consistency.

Requiring every Lᵢⱼ=id would demand closure for every root form, a stronger condition. A nonidentity loop can fix a particular symmetric form. Positive scalar multiplication in the preceding calibration has no such nontrivial fixed form, which explains why its criterion reduced to loop products of 1. A physical contact that permits several answers is a relation rather than a bijection, and needs a joint assignment satisfying those relations. The bijective criterion cannot silently replace that wider contract.

Nor does a fixed form for each loop separately establish a common one. On addressed triples (a,b,c), let P exchange the first two fields and Q exchange the last two. P fixes (u,u,v), and Q fixes (u,v,v). Both sources retain the same outside contrast u≠v. A source fixed by both would require a=b=c and lose that contrast. `FirstLoopHasFixedSource` and `SecondLoopHasFixedSource` in `Checks/Interactions.swift` construct the two positive returns using existing field exchanges. `SharedLoopReturn` derives equality of the outside fields from both actual return equations on one source. The contrasting outside fields are exhibited independently. The two negative controls reject replacing separate witnesses by that common source and replacing one fixed-source return by a universal identity. These static constructions concern the stated relations, not a simulation of molecular folding.

For VI, a proposed physical constant therefore needs its compared processes, admitted context changes and preservation relation. A nonunit scalar loop is a finite obstruction to one shared calibration, stable under extensions retaining those edge facts and that interpretation, as M71 requires. For bijective contact maps the corresponding obstruction is the absence of a common fixed form. Neither derives a universal signal boundary from closure. IiS10 already shows a relative operation surviving cancellation of shared primitive phases. These arguments specify which relational residue a proposed normalization must preserve. They do not need an additional protocol that declares a number constant.

AC7 §6d applies this cancellation to specified path probabilities. Ratios p(x,y)/p(y,x) with unit closed products permit node factors, and reversal of a closed block then preserves its path weight under the declared factorization. Its joins and initial preparation are unchanged. This can justify comparisons of internal excursions without first requiring a stationary initial law. The probabilistic factorization remains a separate premise: a loop returning to the same displayed label can retain an orientation-dependent relation to the next loop. The scalar cancellation does not establish that the label exhausts this boundary.

## §6a. What Repeated Contacts Can Calibrate

**LiR8 (Ordered repetitions determine one additive real reading under a cofinal commuting comparison law).** Let a group of order-preserving bijections act on a linearly ordered carrier, and fix one internal occurrence p. Work on its orbit O. Select an action A with A(p)>p. A uniform real reading means a nondecreasing function t:O→ℝ and increments ρ(g) satisfying t(gx)=t(x)+ρ(g) for every retained action g and every x∈O. Normalize t(p)=0 and t(Ap)=1. Faithfulness here means strict increase on O, retaining every ordered distinction. This does not demand that one time coordinate distinguish all parts of reality.

A faithful uniform reading exists exactly when the following contact properties are satisfied:

1. The actions commute on O.
2. The reference A is cofinal in both directions: every x∈O lies between some two integer powers of A applied to p.
3. Every action g with g(p)>p has a positive power with gⁿ(p)≥A(p).

For necessity, an injective reading into real translations forces ghx=hgx, because the two read answers are t(x)+ρ(g)+ρ(h). Positive real increments of A eventually bracket any finite t(x). Likewise, a strictly positive ρ(g) eventually reaches 1 under repeated addition. These prove all three conditions. None follows from the existence of a written tick alone.

**Construction and uniqueness.** Assume the first two conditions. For each g and n>0, let kₙ(g) be the unique integer with

    A^(k_n(g))(p) ≤ g^n(p) < A^(k_n(g)+1)(p).

Strict advance by A and cofinality provide that integer. Applying any retained action to this comparison preserves its order and commutes with A and g. Thus the same integer brackets the comparison from every occurrence of O. No outside starting phase is being used. Repeating a comparison m times gives

    m kₙ(g) ≤ kₘₙ(g) ≤ m(kₙ(g)+1)−1.

Consequently the rational interval Iₘₙ(g)=[kₘₙ(g)/(mn),(kₘₙ(g)+1)/(mn)] is contained in both Iₘ(g) and Iₙ(g). Every finite family of these intervals has a common interval indexed by the product of its indices. Their lower endpoints are bounded above by every upper endpoint, and their widths 1/n approach zero. Real completeness therefore supplies exactly one common number ρ(g). This is a simultaneous consequence of the retained comparisons. It does not require that a device execute an infinite experiment before any finite bound is valid.

Commutation also gives kₙ(g)+kₙ(h)≤kₙ(gh)≤kₙ(g)+kₙ(h)+1. The shrinking bounds force ρ(gh)=ρ(g)+ρ(h). Moreover ρ(A)=1, and g(p)≤h(p) implies ρ(g)≤ρ(h). An action fixing p fixes its entire orbit by commutation and has ρ=0. Hence t(gp)=ρ(g) is well-defined, nondecreasing, and satisfies the uniform reading law. Any other normalized uniform reading must obey every interval Iₙ(g), so it is this same t. In the familiar real-line setting this is the standard translation-number construction and its additivity for commuting maps, discussed by [Farb and Franks, §2.2 and Corollary 2.8](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/papers/nonlin.pdf). The argument here exposes the order comparisons used, without importing a circle, invariant probability measure or smooth dynamics as VI premises.

**The lost distinctions have an exact test.** For g(p)>p, the constructed ρ(g) is zero exactly when gⁿ(p)<A(p) for every n>0. Such a g has kₙ(g)=0 for every n, and the common bound is zero. Conversely, if some gⁿ(p)≥A(p), then ρ(g)≥1/n>0. For any x<y in the orbit, translate their comparison back to p and apply this test to the relative action. The third condition is therefore exactly what makes the unique reading strictly increasing. Without it, the reading still exists but identifies some ordered occurrences. The obstruction is repeated advance that remains below the reference step, not the nonexistence of that advance. The normalized real description factors through precisely these zero-increment differences.

The choice of reference matters to existence. In the lexicographic ordered group ℤ², addition by A=(1,0) is cofinal and the resulting reading is t(m,n)=m. It loses the second-coordinate advances. Choosing A=(0,1) instead is not cofinal: no number of its repetitions reaches (1,0). No finite-valued additive real reading can assign this finer reference value 1 while remaining nondecreasing on the whole group, since the value at (1,0) would have to exceed every integer. Both references belong to the same source. Calling either one the universal tick cannot supply the missing condition.

Changing to another cofinal positive reference does not restore a lost distinction. A positive B is cofinal exactly when ρ(B)>0: cofinality makes some Bⁿp reach Ap, and such a comparison lets B's powers bracket everything A's powers can bracket. In that case, at a new internal origin q, uniqueness gives t_new(x)=(t(x)−t(q))/ρ(B). Its equal-answer classes are unchanged. A finer reference may describe a restricted region instead, but preserving a second reading there changes the retained content. It is more than rescaling the original scalar.

**Equal rates need not mean equal actions, even on ℝ.** Put A(x)=x+1. On [0,1] define c(s)=s/2 for s≤1/2 and c(s)=3s/2−1/2 for s≥1/2. Extend it periodically by C(m+s)=m+c(s) for m∈ℤ, 0≤s<1, and put B=A∘C. A and B are increasing bijections, commute, and both advance every real point. From the retained p=1/2,

    Aⁿ(p)=n+1/2,    Bⁿ(p)=n+2⁻⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾    for n>0.

Thus Aⁿ⁻¹p<Bⁿp<Aⁿp, giving kₙ(B)=n−1 and ρ(B)=1=ρ(A). Yet B(p)=5/4 differs from A(p)=3/2. Any normalized uniform real reading must give them the same answer and therefore cannot be faithful. The inverse residual C⁻¹ advances p under every positive repetition while remaining below 1, and hence below A(p)=3/2. This is exactly the failed third condition. The orbit already has an order-preserving inclusion in ordinary ℝ. What fails is its representation by uniform real translations. No infinitesimal numerical coordinates or physically asserted alternative world were required for the counterconstruction.

**A refusal can also be finite.** Suppose the same uniform reading law is required across a source domain containing x and y. If A(x)=B(x), then t(x)+ρ(A)=t(x)+ρ(B) forces ρ(A)=ρ(B). If A(y) and B(y) are retained as different, no injective reading can satisfy that law there. This uses one actual coincidence and one actual separation, without a limit, commutation, cofinality or an order assumption. In the displayed example A(0)=B(0)=1, while A(1/2)=3/2 and B(1/2)=5/4. Those two inputs give a finite refusal of a common faithful uniform clock on ℝ. They belong to different orbits, so this is an enlarged source contract relative to the earlier proof on O. M71 preserves the refusal under any extension that retains these facts and the common increment law.

**What a finite contact settles.** One exhibited bracket gives the certified interval kₙ(g)/n≤ρ(g)≤(kₙ(g)+1)/n. A retained equality gᑫp=Aᵐp gives the exact rational answer ρ(g)=m/q. Equal numerical rates alone do not reverse that implication unless faithfulness is established. Under faithfulness, every interval from x to gx has the same increment ρ(g), and equal increments force equal relative actions on this orbit. These are exact consequences of the joint comparison law. A finite unsuccessful search for gⁿp≥Ap cannot establish that all repetitions remain below it.

This supplies a route complementary to Space S27. That theorem starts from actual equal bisections. Here repeated contact comparisons construct the additive value and identify exactly what it erases. S28 tests whether the divisions belong to one common reading: its medial identity supplies the missing grid compatibility. Preserving that division relation and one crossed comparison of the reference interval forces affine transports to be translations. It thereby derives LiR8's faithful-clock conditions and connects the two reconstructions on a common source. To apply either route to an instrument, the order, common action domain, composition law and reference comparisons must belong to its supported record. Formal inverse comparisons are not automatically available future physical actions, as M55 distinguishes. Equal endpoint fields, the same source code and a common average rate do not by themselves establish these relations. LiR8's general proof is mathematical text, not a claim that `StandardIsType` already enforces it.

## §6b. Changing Light, Paths and Their Joins

The object being changed must be retained. Changing an incident field in a fixed linear medium changes its solution, while the propagation operator remains fixed. Changing a medium's response can change the available propagation. A change of units transports the reference along with its reading. These are different internal comparisons. Space S26 also shows why light incidence alone is not the complete clock-and-path record.

For a stationary isotropic, lossless, nondispersive ray model with positive refractive index n, optical length is ℓ(γ)=∫γ n ds₀. The reference path element ds₀ and this response law are supplied physical structure. An extremal optical path need not be the globally shortest path. [Leonhardt, §2 and §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/physics/0605227) gives an exact geometric example: on a plane the profile n(r)=2a/[1+(r/R)²], with a,R>0, produces the round-sphere line element of radius aR under stereographic projection. The omitted pole corresponds to the plane's infinity. A finite truncated device requires its own edge conditions. This identifies a spherical optical geometry for the stated ray family, not a spatial sphere experienced identically by every material process.

**LiR9 (Uniform path bounds preserve finite access).** Keep one family of admissible paths with the same endpoints and two nonnegative path lengths ℓ₀,ℓ₁. Suppose fixed constants 0<m≤M<∞ give mℓ₀(γ)≤ℓ₁(γ)≤Mℓ₀(γ) for every path. Define dⱼ(x,y)=infγ:x→y ℓⱼ(γ), with empty infimum +∞. Taking infima gives m d₀≤d₁≤M d₀. Thus finite access and zero-distance identifications agree. Where these are metrics, their topologies and Cauchy sequences agree. The result needs no attained minimizing path. In particular, a bounded positive reweighting alone cannot put a formerly finite reachable boundary at infinite distance. It must lose a uniform bound, change the allowed paths or change the question.

For the optical model this applies when m≤n₁/n₀≤M along all retained paths. It permits changed distances and curved optical geometry while preserving these access properties. The ideal fish-eye profile has no positive uniform lower bound relative to the whole Euclidean plane, so it does not contradict LiR9. A change of refractive index alone is not a derivation of a causal horizon. The direction of allowed propagation, dispersion and the participation of the apparatus need their own relations.

An ideal complementary profile is n(r)=2a/[1−(r/R)²] on r<R. For the conformal plane metric, K=−n⁻²Δ log n gives Gaussian curvature −1/(aR)². Its radial optical length from the centre is aR log[(R+r)/(R−r)], which diverges at R. This is the hyperbolic disk metric. Its index is unbounded at the edge, exactly where LiR9's upper bound fails. Truncating the profile gives a finite region of that optical geometry and restores finite path length to the truncation. The mathematical profile is not evidence that a finite apparatus realizes its divergent edge.

**LiR10 (Equal bulk optical geometry need not survive a common join).** Consider a fixed frequency, normally incident plane waves and a planar interface with no surface sources. Each region is homogeneous, isotropic and lossless with positive relative permittivity εᵣ and permeability μᵣ. Define its refractive index n=√(εᵣμᵣ) and relative wave impedance z=√(μᵣ/εᵣ). For incidence from a fixed vacuum port, continuity of tangential electric and magnetic fields gives

    1+r=t,       1−r=t/z.

These jointly force r=(z−1)/(z+1) and t=2z/(z+1), since z+1>0. This is the interface calculation in [Staelin, §9.1.4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/d3be4ea78b036a6362230fb41780cf54_MIT6_013S09_notes.pdf). Two admitted constitutive descriptions (εᵣ,μᵣ)=(4,1) and (2,2) have the same n=2, hence the same bulk phase propagation and ray metric. They have z=1/2 and z=1, giving reflected amplitudes −1/3 and 0. Their reflected intensity fractions are 1/9 and 0. The common incident port and reader distinguish the join without an absolute field label. A description retaining n alone therefore cannot answer this boundary question on the stated material family.

This is a conditional obstruction inside an electromagnetic model, not a claim that those two ideal constitutive values have been realized over every frequency. If the material family fixes μᵣ=1, n already determines z, and this particular freedom is absent. The failure concerns which relationships were discarded when reducing a wider family to its geometry.

**The missing boundary relation has a sufficient repair in this scope.** At that frequency, n and z determine εᵣ=n/z and μᵣ=nz. For a layer with nonzero propagation factor p and relative impedance z>0, write its tangential boundary state as F=(E,Z₀H). Its forward and backward amplitudes have E=A+B and Z₀H=(A−B)/z. Advancing them by p and p⁻¹ therefore gives

    F_right = T(p,z) F_left,
    T(p,z) = 1/2 [[p+p⁻¹, z(p−p⁻¹)],
                  [(p−p⁻¹)/z, p+p⁻¹]].

This follows by solving for A,B and substituting their two propagated values. Its determinant is one because (p+p⁻¹)²−(p−p⁻¹)²=4. Tangential-field continuity identifies neighboring layer boundary states, so ordered matrix products determine a finite stack's transfer relation. Equal transfer matrices are interchangeable in every such stack with the same ports and terminal constraints. Interference, including multiple reflections, is already in this relation. Optical length alone omits z. For a homogeneous lossless layer p is the unit-modulus phase factor fixed by its thickness, frequency and n. This sufficiency concerns one-dimensional monochromatic linear contacts. It does not extend by itself to a dispersive pulse, a nonlinear medium or arbitrary joint quantum preparations.

If the linear boundary model is established, two linearly independent input boundary states F₁,F₂ and their full complex outputs Y₁,Y₂ determine its matrix: T=[Y₁ Y₂][F₁ F₂]⁻¹. This follows by multiplication with the inverse input matrix. The inputs, phase reference and both boundary components must actually be available. Changing only the amplitude of one fixed input does not span this space, and separate intensity readings do not supply the complex outputs. These finite data determine a member of the declared linear family. They do not prove that every untested preparation obeys that family.

The VI issue is therefore contextual replacement. VI1 and VC15 require an identification to preserve the admitted contacts after substitution into a common context. The n-only description fails one explicit context. The full boundary transfer relation succeeds for the declared stack grammar, by composition. Calling a light description complete requires this question-and-contact scope. Neither a metric nor the word light certifies completeness for every process in the whole.

**Light can also alter the medium that subsequent light encounters.** [Fleischer et al.](https://www.princeton.edu/~jasonf/Papers/2DNature.pdf) optically induce a photonic lattice in an anisotropic photorefractive crystal and launch a differently polarized probe. Their coupled model retains total intensity and unequal material responses for lattice and probe. In the reported staggered regime, a defocusing response together with the lattice gives a localized output. Removing the lattice under those conditions increases spreading, while lowering probe intensity restores diffraction. These are measured finite-propagation comparisons, supported by a model, not proof of indefinite stability. They supply a joint behavior that the separately retained components do not establish. The crystal, applied bias, background illumination and preparation remain part of the mechanism.

IiS8 identifies a specific omitted relation that can matter to such writing. At a photosensitive contact, two mutually coherent scalar fields contribute |a+b|²=|a|²+|b|²+2 Re(a conj(b)). Their separate intensities do not determine the written response when that cross term changes. If a supported material response distinguishes those intensities, the omission can change the subsequent probe's propagation law. The selected contact must actually be sensitive to the difference. Orthogonal writing components do not automatically supply this cross term. A simultaneous transport of fields, medium and probe reference also need not change any answer. The experiment above supplies a physical route to studying these dependencies, without certifying every proposed phase-controlled geometry.

The resulting research question is concrete: which relation between preparation and medium is required to preserve the same future contacts? An effective propagation law may itself be a reading of the larger prepared system. Extending VI should retain that dependency before treating the smaller law as independent of its source.

## §6c. Flux, Geometry and the Boundary of a Transfer Description

**LiR11 (A conserved two-wave flux fixes an invariant response geometry).** Use flux-normalized forward and backward complex amplitudes (a,b) at the two ports of LiR10's lossless reciprocal stack. Its conserved net flux is J=|a|²−|b|². Requiring a linear transfer M to preserve J on every input gives M†DM=D, where D=diag(1,−1). Together with det M=1 this forces

    M = [[α, β], [conj(β), conj(α)]],     |α|²−|β|²=1.

Indeed M⁻¹=DM†D, compared entry by entry with the determinant-one inverse, gives the displayed conjugacies. These are physical-model premises about the joint flux and response, not consequences of global closure. [Barriuso et al., §II](https://arxiv.org/html/physics/0305040) develop this multilayer representation.

For positive net flux, r=b/a belongs to |r|<1. Substitution gives the joint action and its exact gap identity:

    Φ_M(r) = [conj(β)+conj(α)r]/[α+βr],
    1−|Φ_M(r)|² = (1−|r|²)/|α+βr|².

The denominator cannot vanish there, since |α|>|β|. Differentiation also gives |dΦ_M|=|dr|/|α+βr|². Dividing these identities proves invariance of 2|dr|/(1−|r|²). The hyperbolic line element has therefore emerged from preserving these two-wave relations. It was not imposed as the spatial metric of the apparatus. Within positive quadratic infinitesimal metrics invariant under the full displayed family, it is unique up to scale: rotations force an isotropic metric at zero, and (r−r₀)/(1−conj(r₀)r) carries any r₀ to zero with derivative 1/(1−|r₀|²). This last uniqueness uses the full transformation family, not just a few measured layers.

IiS8 exposes the corresponding sphere using the same coherent amplitude pair. Put I=|a|²+|b|², U=2 Re(conj(a)b), V=2 Im(conj(a)b). Then I²−U²−V²=J². Fixing I gives the sphere J²+U²+V²=I², while fixing nonzero J gives a hyperboloid. The preserved comparison selects which geometry applies. Forward/backward flux and total intensity in two co-propagating orthogonal modes have different physical roles. This is not permission to change the invariant by a choice of notation. The relation to Lorentz geometry is an established mathematical correspondence, discussed by [Barriuso et al., §II](https://arxiv.org/html/physics/0403140), rather than an identification of optical ports with spacetime events.

**There is a quantitative obstruction for finite transfer products.** With no incident wave from the far port, r=conj(β)/α and t=1/α. Hence the transmitted flux fraction is 1/|α|²>0 for every finite M. A finite product of finite matrices of this form cannot give perfect reflection. For each factor define ηⱼ=log(|αⱼ|+|βⱼ|)≥0. Its singular values are exp(±ηⱼ), so submultiplicativity of the operator norm gives η_total≤B=Σηⱼ and

    |t_total|² ≥ sech²(B) > 0   for finite B.

The equality 1/|α|²=sech²η follows from (|α|+|β|)(|α|−|β|)=1. No numerical search of layer thicknesses is needed. In flux-normalized interface coordinates η=|log(z₂/z₁)|/2, while lossless propagation in a uniform layer has η=0. Thus an impedance-ratio budget bounds suppression even when phases remain free. This belongs to the established family of transfer bounds, including [Visser, §II.2–§II.3](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9901030). It is a conditional bound for this contact grammar, not a prohibition on every finite optical device.

The response geometry also has a retained-phase limitation. M and −M act identically on every r but have opposite transmitted amplitudes. A coherent bypass can distinguish them. `ControlledPhaseRecovery` in `Checks/Seeing.swift` already constructs the relevant same-versus-opposite branch comparison and its dark output. The disk action is sufficient for its ratio questions, while a coherent extension needs the lift to the amplitude relation. This applies IiS10 rather than adding another completeness marker.

**LiR12 (A finite resonant attachment can defeat the transfer grammar without an infinite field).** Take the one-side-site specialization of the [Fano–Anderson model, §II.1](https://arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0505023). With unit couplings and zero onsite detunings, its stationary equations are

    λφₙ = φₙ₋₁ + φₙ₊₁ + δₙ₀ d,       λd = φ₀.

Here λ is spectral detuning, not optical carrier frequency. At λ=0 the side equation forces φ₀=0. Write the left field as A iⁿ+B i⁻ⁿ and the right field as C iⁿ, with no right incidence. Since i+i⁻¹=0, the neighbor equations give φ₀=A+B=C. Thus C=0, B=−A, and the junction equation gives d=2iA. All fields are finite. Without the attachment, B=0,C=A is admitted.

For λ≠0, eliminating d introduces φ₀/λ. At resonance that elimination fails, while the original system supplies φ₀=0 and finite d. The coefficient diverges in the reduced description. This exact monochromatic model does not assert an observed zero over finite bandwidth.

`ResonantSideContact` in `Checks/Seeing.swift` constructs this solution for an arbitrary formal complex input. The existing quarter-turn word supplies the incident and reflected neighbors. The original input and its reversed phase cancel at the junction. Two retained side contributions cancel the two actual left-neighbor contributions, and the transmitted zero remains zero under the next phase step. `PhaseCycleCancellation` supplies the free-lead recurrence through its four-address periodic word. The unit input is nonzero: `ResonantInputDiffersFromSilence` separates its amplitude from the zero value at the junction. The compiler rejects three negative controls: removing the reflected contribution (`RejectedResonantMissingReflection`), reversing the side contribution (`RejectedResonantSideSign`) and claiming the incident amplitude is transmitted (`RejectedResonantTransmission`). These are source-bound static cancellation certificates for the supplied resonance model. The compiler does not establish its physical realization or the full complex transfer theorem of LiR11.

**The general object at a join is a relation.** For an exact boundary relation R⊆X×Y, replacement by y=f(x) is valid exactly when every admitted x has one compatible y. A two-way transfer additionally requires one x for each admitted y. At perfect reflection, left-side fields cannot determine an independently incident right-side field. The full scattering relation can remain finite even though an invertible left-to-right transfer description fails. The real transmission zero remains a physical prediction of the model. Restoring the side mode repairs the explanation of that zero, without removing it.

This refines the search for a fundamental wall. First derive the refusal under stated internal relations. Then test whether another internal attachment preserves those relations and the question. Here a finite resonant attachment changes the contact grammar, and the omitted mode explains precisely why the old finite-transfer premise fails. A singular reduced coefficient alone cannot establish a universal boundary of reality.

## §6d. Which Internal Modes Constrain a Boundary?

**LiR13 (Boundary overlap decides the resonant refusal).** Replace LiR12's one side site by a finite Hermitian matrix H and a coupling vector w. The retained equations are

    (λI−H)d = wφ₀,       λφₙ = φₙ₋₁+φₙ₊₁+δₙ₀ w†d.

Take λ=2 cos q with 0<q<π, unit incident amplitude and no incident wave from the right. Let K=ker(λI−H), and P_K be the orthogonal projection onto K. Then transmission is zero exactly when P_K w≠0. An internal eigenfrequency alone does not suffice.

For necessity of the boundary constraint, multiply the side equation by u† for u∈K. It gives (u†w)φ₀=0. If P_Kw≠0, this forces φ₀=t=0 and reflection r=−1. The lead equation then requires w†d=2i sin q. The finite choice d=(2i sin q)P_Kw/‖P_Kw‖² satisfies both equations. Other solutions differ by vectors in K orthogonal to w. The scattering answer is fixed even if some internal amplitudes remain undetermined.

If P_Kw=0, the side equation instead has d=A⁺wφ₀+k, where A=λI−H, A⁺ inverts A on K⊥ and vanishes on K, and k∈K. Its feedback is Uφ₀ with U=w†A⁺w real and finite. The term k contributes nothing. Substitution at the lead junction gives

    t = 2i sin q / (2i sin q+U),       |t|² = 4 sin²q/(4 sin²q+U²) > 0.

This proves both directions within the declared one-point attachment model. Away from eigenfrequencies, U=w†(λI−H)⁻¹w. Its pole at an eigenvalue has residue ‖P_Kw‖². Contributions from one eigenspace cannot cancel that nonnegative residue. The end-coupled chains of [Miroshnichenko and Kivshar, §II.1–§II.3](https://arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0505023) are instances. This criterion retains the coupling that a list of internal frequencies omits.

**A chain supplies a refusal that survives changes of coupling strength.** Let the side system be a path of N sites with zero onsite detunings, every nearest-neighbor coupling nonzero, and a nonzero attachment at its first endpoint. For N=1, the sole side equation at λ=0 forces φ₀=0. For N≥2, the last equation forces the penultimate field to zero. Repeating backward forces every second field to zero. For odd N these are the even fields, and the first equation then forces φ₀=0. For even N they are the odd fields, including the attached field d₁. The feedback vanishes and the uniform lead transmits completely. Successive division by nonzero couplings supplies the remaining fields, so the required solutions are finite. Thus odd N gives t=0 and even N gives t=1 throughout this family, without equal coupling magnitudes. Zero detuning refers to a common internal frequency reference, not an absolute zero of frequency.

The recurrence dⱼ₋₁+dⱼ₊₁=0 for unit couplings has a static construction in `SideRecurrenceStep`. `TransparentSidePair` checks the two-site solution against its actual neighboring fields. `ExtendSideEndByPair` derives preservation of a zero terminal value after adjoining two sites. Together with LiR12's one-site construction these give the unit-coupling bases and extension rule. The general weighted proof above is separate. No spectrum or propagation loop is evaluated at runtime.

**Existence, visibility and width are different obligations.** A three-site path with real nonzero couplings a,b has the zero mode (b,0,−a). An attachment w=v e₁ sees it. An attachment w=v e₂ does not. The same internal eigenfrequency therefore gives opposite answers about a resonant obstruction at these two contacts. For the endpoint contact its residue is |v|²b²/(a²+b²). It can approach zero while every edge remains nonzero and the transmission zero remains pinned. Structural persistence alone supplies no positive uniform width or response bound. At a visible resonance d∈K, so w†d=(P_Kw)†d. Cauchy–Schwarz gives ‖d‖≥2 sin q/‖P_Kw‖, attained by the displayed solution. Weak overlap can therefore require a large internal amplitude norm for the same incident amplitude. SiE12 proves when the graph excludes all hidden eigenvectors at chosen contacts.

The exact zero also does not give a forbidden frequency interval. A finite H has finitely many eigenvalues, and the formula above gives nonzero transmission at every other point of the open lead band. For an incoming scattering wave packet with a nonzero square-integrable spectral amplitude supported in that band, multiplication by t cannot annihilate the entire packet: its zero set has measure zero. This concerns the declared linear scattering preparation, without an independently prepared internal excitation. It separates an exact monochromatic refusal from complete rejection of a finite-energy pulse.

For a fixed apparatus with a visible resonance inside the band, this transmission map is injective on such wave packets but has no uniformly bounded inverse on its range. Choose unit-norm inputs supported in shrinking neighborhoods where |t| tends to zero. Their transmitted norms tend to zero. Retaining both outgoing branches changes the result: |r|²+|t|²=1, so a↦(ra,ta) preserves the spectral norm and the joint reading (b,c)↦conj(r)b+conj(t)c recovers a with norm at most one. Exact recoverability, stable recovery from one branch and recovery from the joint source are distinct claims. The apparatus law and coherent phase references enter each decoder.

**An arbitrarily weak added contact need not preserve a monochromatic answer.** Take two side sites with H=[[0,g],[g,0]], attachment w=(v,0), and real v≠0. For g≠0,

    U_g(λ) = v²λ/(λ²−g²),       U_g(0)=0,       t_g(0)=1.

At g=0 the second site decouples and LiR12 gives t₀(0)=0. For nonzero g the full central-frequency solution instead has d₁=0 and d₂=−v/g. Its amplitude diverges as the added coupling tends to zero. The limits g→0 and λ→0 therefore do not commute. For fixed v≠0 and g→0, U_g(λ)=−v²λ/g² to leading order when |λ|≪|g|. The central transparency feature therefore narrows on the smaller detuning scale g²/v². The exact formula, rather than this expansion, fixes the claimed zeros and transmissions.

This discontinuity is absent for a fixed square-integrable incident spectrum a(q). Except at finitely many points, t_g(q)→t₀(q), and |t_g−t₀|≤2. Dominated convergence gives ‖(t_g−t₀)a‖₂→0. The input spectrum must remain fixed: concentrating it into the shrinking transparency window changes the preparation. Thus an arbitrarily weak internal contact can reverse an ideal single-frequency answer without producing a discontinuity in the scattered field of every fixed finite-energy preparation. A claim of negligible coupling must name the retained preparation and resolution.

It also derives −iS†∂_φS=Z†Z when the changed relation is a phase φ on the sewn paths and Z maps the incident source to the internal fields. This links a boundary phase comparison to internal accumulation. Interpreting that ratio as a delay requires the actual phase-to-frequency calibration, not an exterior clock or an absolute spatial ruler.

SG5 integrates this comparison over a full relative-phase cycle and obtains the number of accessible internal amplitude directions. For one lossless loop with real coupling parameters c²+s²=1, |c|<1, the boundary response is S=(c−e^{iφ})/(1−c e^{iφ}). Its intensity is one at every phase, but its phase makes one full turn and ∂_φ arg S=(1−c²)/|1−c e^{iφ}|². The integral of this nonnegative response is 2π regardless of the nonzero coupling strength. A narrower feature can have a larger response without changing that total. At exact decoupling the loop becomes invisible to the retained contact and contributes zero.

This is a comparison with the stated ring model, not a new experimental measurement or a claim that a physical ring has only one microscopic degree of freedom. **A physical comparison of structural persistence.** [Poli et al., *Selective enhancement of topologically induced interface states in a dielectric resonator chain* (2015), Figs. 2–4 and Methods](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/people/schomeru/publications/poli2015selective.pdf), report a mode concentrated on one sublattice of a 21-resonator microwave chain.
